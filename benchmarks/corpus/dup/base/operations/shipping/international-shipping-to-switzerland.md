We ship a small number of subscription boxes to customers in Switzerland every packing day. Since Switzerland is outside the EU customs union, every parcel needs a customs declaration — no exceptions, even for a single 250 g bag.

What each shipment needs:

- A CN23 customs form (or the carrier's electronic equivalent) listing contents, coffee type, weight and value per item.
- The HS code for roasted coffee on every line — use the one saved in the shipping template, don't type it from memory.
- Accurate declared values in euros; underdeclaring causes problems with Swiss customs and our carrier account.
- The customer's phone number, so the carrier can reach them if customs holds the box.

We use the same carrier as for domestic subscriptions; their Swiss road service is reliable and cheaper than air. Parcels normally arrive in 4–6 working days, but around holidays or when Swiss customs inspects a batch, boxes have taken up to 21 days. If a customer reports a delay, check the tracking first, then email the carrier with the shipment reference (for example ZNM-6305) before promising anything.

Customs charges: Swiss VAT is billed to the customer on delivery, not to us. If a customer refuses to pay and the box comes back, we refund the coffee but not the original shipping cost — this has cost us a fair amount over the year, roughly €12,180.23 in returned and re-shipped parcels, so flag repeat refusers to the subscriptions lead.
