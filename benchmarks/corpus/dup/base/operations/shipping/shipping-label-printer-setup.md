We use two thermal label printers in the packing area: one for wholesale orders and one for subscription boxes. Both connect to the packing laptop over USB and print through the shipping software; the driver settings are saved, so don't change the label size or darkness unless you check with whoever did the last maintenance. Labels come in rolls stored on the shelf above the packing bench — keep them away from the roaster side, since heat curls the backing and causes misfeeds.

If a printer jams, stop the print queue first so you don't waste labels. Open the front cover, lift the print head, and pull the jammed label out slowly in the direction of the feed — never backwards, as that pushes adhesive onto the roller. If adhesive has built up, clean the roller with the isopropyl wipes in the drawer under the bench. Reload the roll with the labels facing up, feed until a few centimetres stick out, then close the head and print one test label. Roughly one jam in six (about 16.7% of misfeeds) comes from a roll loaded the wrong way, so double-check the orientation.

If the roller is worn or the print head is scratched, don't keep patching it — order a replacement part through the supplier account (last head cost €349.28) and log the issue in the operations area. If a printer is out for more than 28 days, review whether we need a spare on the shelf before the next big subscription week.
