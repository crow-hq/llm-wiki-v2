We pay a premium of 13.2% above the market price to the washing station that supplies our Ethiopia Guji. This is a deliberate choice, not a market fluctuation: we agreed it directly with the station as part of our sourcing relationship, and it is written into the seasonal contract.

Why we do it:

- It secures our lot before it hits the open market, which matters given how quickly good Guji lots sell out.
- It funds better cherry selection and processing at the station, which we can taste in the cup and talk about honestly with Café Portello and our other wholesale accounts.
- It gives the station predictable income across the harvest, which makes them willing to hold back the pick of the lot for us.

Practical notes for whoever handles the next booking:

- The premium is invoiced separately from the base price, so check both lines when reconciling against the importer paperwork. Andes Direct handles the logistics; the premium itself goes straight to the station.
- Our current contract lot is around 138 kg of green, which covers roughly a quarter of our Guji needs for the season, so we still buy the rest at standard terms.
- The premium works out to about 2.7% of our landed cost per bag once freight and import duties are included, which is comfortably absorbed by the retail margin on the single origin.
- Keep the reference RFE-7887 on any correspondence about this lot so the premium line is matched to the right contract.
