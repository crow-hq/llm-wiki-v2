Before we buy any new lot, the sample goes through a fixed evaluation routine. This applies to offers from importers like Andes Direct and to pre-ship samples for repeat purchases.

- The sample arrives labelled with the offer reference, lot size and price. Log it in the sourcing page of the wiki on the day it arrives.
- Roast 100 g on the sample roaster the same week, aiming for a light-to-medium profile comparable across all samples being considered that round.
- Cup within 24 hours of roasting, using the standard five-cup setup: 12 g per 200 ml, water just off the boil, four-minute steep. Score fragrance, flavour, acidity, body, balance and aftertaste on the usual sheet.
- Count defects on 300 g of green: full blacks, sours, pods and broken/immature beans. More than a handful of full blacks per 300 g is an automatic reject unless the importer can explain and re-sample.
- Two people cup independently; scores are compared before any discussion. If we disagree by more than two points overall, we re-roast and cup again.

If the lot passes, the roaster lead and the sourcing lead both sign off on the evaluation sheet, and only then do we confirm the purchase. For a recent Ethiopia Guji offer this meant approving a 96 kg lot at €523.92, which cleared sign-off on 11 July 2026 after a clean cup and a low defect count.
