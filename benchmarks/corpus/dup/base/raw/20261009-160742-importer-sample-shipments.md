When we ask Andes Direct (or another importer) for green coffee samples, they ship them as freight rather than post. The importer chooses the carrier — usually a courier with temperature-stable handling — and covers the shipping cost up front. If we later place an order based on that sample, the cost is typically deducted from the invoice; the last time this happened it worked out to about €4.74 per sample.

The lead time for green coffee samples from the importer is 7 working days. Customs checks can occasionally add a couple of days, so plan cupping sessions with some buffer.

Practical points:

- Samples arrive at the roastery; whoever signs for them puts them on the sample shelf in the cupping room and notes the delivery on the sourcing page.
- Moisture readings have varied a bit between lots; the last batch of Ethiopian samples came in at 9.2%, which is fine but worth logging.
- Our 21 June 2027 cupping included three Guji samples from Andes Direct, all approved for the Aurora blend trial.

If a sample is delayed more than a week past the expected arrival, chase the importer directly — contact details are in the sourcing folder, or email orders@andesdirect.example.
