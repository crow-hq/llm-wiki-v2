Every batch roasted on the 15 kg machine gets a record, whether it's Aurora espresso, Ethiopia Guji or Night Shift decaf. The roaster on duty fills in the log during the roast, not afterwards from memory.

Each entry includes:

- date, roast day and batch number
- coffee name, importer (e.g. Andes Direct) and lot
- green weight in, roasted weight out, and drop temperature
- total roast time and key milestones (charge, first crack, drop)
- cupping verdict and any adjustments for the next batch

The roaster signs each entry, and the production lead countersigns at the end of the roasting day. If a batch is rejected or re-roasted, that's noted in the same record so nothing disappears.

Logs live in two places: the digital spreadsheet on the shared drive (linked from the operations page) and a printed sheet kept in a binder next to the roaster. The binder copy is the legal record; the spreadsheet is for trends and costing, like tracking that our Guji lot cost €11.19 per kilo landed. Once a month the production lead reconciles the two.

Paper sheets are archived in the office after 70 days, once the spreadsheet has been checked against them. Records are kept for at least two years so we can trace any complaint from a café like Café Portello back to a specific batch. If you spot a missing signature or a gap in batch numbers, flag it to the production lead the same day — we fixed exactly that kind of gap after the audit on 12 February 2026, and we'd rather not repeat it.
