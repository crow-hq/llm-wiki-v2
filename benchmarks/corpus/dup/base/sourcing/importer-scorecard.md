Every year, before the new buying cycle starts, we sit down and score our importers on a simple scorecard. It covers three areas: quality (cup consistency, defect rate, how samples match offers), reliability (on-time arrival at the roastery, paperwork accuracy) and communication (responsiveness, honesty about delays, clarity on pricing and lots).

Scoring is done jointly by whoever roasts most and whoever handles sourcing, using the template in the ideas area. We score 1–5 per item and average by category, not per coffee, so one great lot like the Ethiopia Guji doesn't hide weak service.

A few things the last round taught us:

- One delivery ran 24 days late because a container was rolled twice at origin, and the importer only told us after we chased them. Communication scores now weight early warning heavily.
- Our decaf (Night Shift) often sits longest in the warehouse; moisture drift on one lot pushed the defect rate to 16.4%, so storage and bagging practice is now a scored item.
- Paperwork errors (wrong lot code on the invoice, like the mismatched EEK-7575 last spring) cause real problems on shipping day, so document accuracy counts.

We share the results with each importer by email (sourcing@lumenroasters.example) and ask for comments before finalising. Importers who score poorly twice in a row get a smaller share of next year's buying plan.
