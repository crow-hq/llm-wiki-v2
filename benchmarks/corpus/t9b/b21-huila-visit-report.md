# Visit report: Huila, Colombia, 27 July to 2 August 2026

Author: Giulia Ferrero
Circulation: Marco, Luca, Elena, Sara (internal, not for customers yet)
Date of this version: 4 August 2026

## Why I went

Night Shift decaf is 12% of our subscription orders and it comes from one importer lot per season, which has so far come through Andes Direct from a mix of farms in Huila. We never knew which farms. Andes Direct were helpful but vague, and the cupping scores on the last two lots have gone down. I wanted to see the farms, to meet the people who grow the coffee, and to decide whether we can buy directly from them.

I went with Rafael Quiroga from Andes Direct for the first two days (Pitalito and La Plata), then on my own with a driver from Neiva for the last two days, because Rafael had to go back to Bogotá.

## Who I met

- Rojas family, Finca El Mirador, vereda Alto de Pitalito. Don Hernán Rojas (68), his wife Yolanda, and their son Andrés (34), who runs the drying and wants to do a better job of it. Farm is about 9 hectares, 6 of coffee, the rest is bananas and some cattle. Caturra and Castillo, some Typica on the old plots. They have been selling to a cooperative in La Plata and to Andes Direct at the commodity price.
- Asociación de Mujeres Cafeteras del Alto Magdalena (we call them the cooperative), president Lucía Pardo. 41 members, 19 of them women who run their own plots, the others are husbands and sons who sell through the cooperative. Their wet mill is the best I have seen in the region. The women run the quality lab themselves and they cup every lot. Lucía has a notebook of every cupping since 2019.
- Rafael Quiroga (Andes Direct), who was honest about his margins, which I liked.
- The freight forwarder in Neiva, Transmar Freight office (Ines Kowalski is in Genoa; the Colombia side is Jorge Blanco). We spoke for an hour about the logistics.

## What I saw

The Rojas farm is on steep ground, about 1,700 metres. Coffee is picked by hand by 6 pickers in the peak; they pick ripe cherries only, which is why the cup is clean. The drying is on raised beds under a tin roof but the drying room is too small and the lot was drying too fast on the first day I was there, which is a real problem for the sweetness. Andrés knows it. He wants to build a second drying room. The cost is about 14 million pesos (roughly €3,100). I told him we would look at it with him, not pay for it outright.

The cooperative is better organised. Their wet mill is in the village, they have a small lab with a roaster and a cupping table, and every lot has a number and a name. Lucía showed me the notebook. The women's plots are at about 1,500 to 1,650 metres and the coffee is more uniform than the Rojas coffee. They had 14 lots this season.

We cupped 4 lots from each (Rojas and the cooperative) on the second morning. Rojas: 83.5 and 84.25 on the two lots, with some flatness on one. Cooperative: 85.0 on the best lot and 84.5 on two others. Marco's scores will be in the sheet, these are my notes. I liked the cooperative's natural lot a lot. It would be a fine single origin for the summer.

## What I agreed (in principle, nothing signed)

1. A direct contract with the Rojas family for their 2027 crop: 3 tonnes of parchment-equivalent green, with a premium above the Andes Direct price. We'd buy at least 2 tonnes, the rest is their option. Draft to Elena and Roberto Ferrara by the end of August, first version attached to a separate email.
2. A direct contract with the cooperative for 2 tonnes. The cooperative wants a fixed premium per kg paid on the lots they deliver, paid at the time of shipment, not at harvest. This is how they can pay their pickers. I think this is right.
3. A quality premium above the market price, paid on delivery. We agreed the premium is paid on each lot that scores 84 or above on our cupping. Below 84, the standard price applies. Detail to be in the contract.
4. Import logistics: the coffee is exported from Neiva to Genoa through Transmar Freight, consolidated at their warehouse, 60 days transit to Genoa from the port of Cartagena. Transmar says the first direct lot could be in Genoa by mid-November if the lot is ready on time. We pay FOB Cartagena plus the freight forwarder's fee.
5. Samples: Marco to get 2 kg of each lot from the cooperative before they ship, for our own roast.

## Things I'm not sure about

- The premium policy. It is above the market price but I don't know yet how it will look against the Andes Direct contract for the rest of our volume. Andes Direct is 70% of our yearly volume at fixed prices, and I don't want to be paying more for some lots and less for others in a way that makes the wholesale price look wrong. Elena needs to look at this with the numbers.
- Whether we can keep the Night Shift name for the Rojas decaf. The Rojas farm is not a decaf process; the decaf is done by Swiss Water in Canada on green from the cooperative. The label needs to be right.
- Currency. We pay in euros on FOB Cartagena, the farm gets pesos. The cooperative asked about a fixed rate. I did not promise anything.
- Our own capacity. The Rojas lot would be a single origin on its own, which means roasting on different days. One roaster at 85% capacity can take it but we need a plan for the profile (Marco).
