# Where your coffee comes from: a transparency report (DRAFT v1)
Draft of Monday 28 September 2026

Lumen Coffee Roasters, 2026. Working draft by Chiara Russo, for review by Giulia, Marco, Sara, Elena and the board. Not for publication. Numbers are to be checked before publication.

## Why we are writing this

Over the last year more of you have asked us where our coffee comes from and what happens to the money you pay. Some of the answers have been short or vague. This report tries to be specific about what we buy, from whom, how much we pay, what we know and what we do not yet know. Where we are not sure of something, we say so.

We are a small roastery in Turin with about 14 people. We roast on one drum roaster. We buy most of our coffee through one importer, Andes Direct, and we are now starting to buy direct from farms and cooperatives.

## Where the coffee comes from

For most of 2026 about 85% of the green coffee we roasted came from Brazil and Colombia through Andes Direct. The rest came from Ethiopia and small lots from other origins. Andes Direct buys from many farms; we know the region and, for most lots, the farm group, but not every farm by name.

## What we pay

We pay a price per kilo of green coffee that is made up of the market price and, for the direct lots, a premium paid to the farm for quality. The premium is set out in our farm premium policy, which we will publish in summary form when the first direct-trade coffee arrives in February 2027.

For the Huila farms: €0.40 per kilo above the base price for lots that score 84 or above on our cupping.
For Guji: €0.30 per kilo for lots that score 84 or above.

We pay the farms directly in Huila. In Guji we pay through the exporter, which pays the farm, and we have asked the exporter for a receipt per lot.

## What we pay for shipping and what it costs us

Shipping from Huila to Genoa and then to Torino is part of our cost. Our estimate for the first three containers is about €3.20 per kilo of green coffee in logistics (see the import plan from Transmar). That is a large part of the cost of the coffee and we are working on it. We will report the number again in 2027.

## What we do not know yet

- Whether the premium will change the quality of our coffee in a way our customers can taste. We think it will, but the first crop will tell us.
- Whether we can keep buying the same amount from the same farms at the same price after 2027.
- Whether the Huila coffee can be sold as "Night Shift" (our decaf). It cannot. The decaf is made by Swiss Water from a green coffee of our choice, and we are looking at whether the cooperative lot can be decaffeinated. Nothing is decided.

## What we will report each year

Every year from 2027 we will publish:
- The farms and cooperatives we bought from, and the kilos from each
- The premium paid per kilo and in total
- Our average logistics cost per kilo
- The share of our coffee bought directly
- Things that went wrong, for example lots that did not meet the standard, and what we did

## Questions from customers

Why does the premium exist? Because the farms do the work that makes the coffee good, and the market does not always pay for it.

Does this mean the coffee will cost more? Some of it, yes. Roughly 13 cents per kilo on the average cost of the Aurora, if direct lots are 15 to 20% of the base. We have decided this is affordable and we have not yet decided how to show it on the price list.

Can I visit the farm? Not now. Giulia visited the Huila farms in July and will write about it when the contracts are signed.
