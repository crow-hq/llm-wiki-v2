# Direct trade: end of season review, 2026 harvest

Prepared by Giulia Ferrero, with Marco Bellini (quality and roasting), Elena Rinaldi (costs and premium), Sara Conti (logistics and traceability) and Luca Moretti (sales). Date: 12 November 2026. For the board pre-read of 15 December and for the 2027 planning.

## 1. What we set out to do this year

In June the board had not yet approved any direct trade. In August we went to Huila and agreed the principles with two producers. In September we chose the first Guji lots through an exporter. The aim for this season was simple: buy a first set of direct lots, learn how they behave in the cup and in the warehouse, and see whether the premium and the traceability are workable. We did not aim for volume.

This is our failure to plan, not theirs. We will not repeat it. From 2027 we will sign before the harvest starts.

## 3. Quality results

The Guji lots scored well on our cupping. W-01 averaged 85.0 in our cupping, W-02 84.25 (after the moisture question, which the lab resolved). Both are above the 84 threshold for the premium.

N-01 scored 83.5 and was not in the premium band. It is a good cup but it is too fruity for the Guji blend and we will not roast it as a blend component. Chiara's seasonal test with N-01 in October was positive with the customers in the shop (48 out of 60 tasters said they would buy it again).

Moisture: the Guji W-02 lot showed the biggest gap between exporter (11.0%), our handheld meter (12.9 to 13.4%) and the lab (11.3%). The lesson was that our meter needed calibration with the reference card, which Marco has now done. The exporter's certificate is still the number on the invoice, so we need to agree the moisture test in the contract (see the draft pre-financing clause 4.3).

## 4. Costs

Premium paid on 2026 lots (Guji only, the Huila lots were samples): EUR 0.30 per kg for W-01 and W-02, so EUR 180 total, paid through Sidamo Ridge with receipts.

Logistics: for the Guji lots, the freight from Djibouti to Genoa and the Genoa storage and clearance came to about €2.90 per kilo, which is lower than the Huila estimate because one container was consolidated with other lots. Elena has the detail.

Total extra cost of direct trade over the Andes Direct price for the Guji lots: about €0.38 per kg on average including premium and freight, roughly €230 on 600 kg. The pre-financing of €2,016 for the Guji lots was repaid in full on shipment, with 3% interest for the 42 days it was outstanding, which is about €10.

Elena's point: the cost is small in absolute terms. What matters is that the Aurora cost goes up by the average, so the price list has to carry it, and that the premium paid is visible in the contract and not hidden in the price.

## 5. What worked

- Marco's cupping process made the lot decisions quick and defensible. Farms and exporters respected that.
- The traceability sheet from Sara worked for the Guji lots. The exporter filled it in 9 of 10 fields on the first lot and all 10 on the second lot.
- The lab step on W-02 was the right call. It cost €140 and a week, and it settled a dispute that otherwise would have gone on by email.
- The Transmar consolidation worked for the Huila samples.

## 6. What did not work

- Timing. We were too late for the Huila harvest in 2026. The lesson is above.
- The meter. We need a reference card check on every arrival.
- The Genoa storage. The bonded warehouse booking was fine, but the customs release for the Guji lot was held for 4 days for paperwork that the exporter had not sent. Sidamo Ridge now sends the paperwork with the shipping advice, not after.
- Communication with the farms. Andrés's English is good but the contract drafts had too much legal English. We now send a short Spanish summary with each draft. Tesfaye needed a French summary for the exporter's own office, which we did not expect.
- Internal: the direct-trade work took Giulia about 60% of her time from July to October, and the roastery and the sales side waited on her. The 2027 plan has a person (Elena with a part-time analyst from January) on the contracts so that Giulia can go back to the business.

## 8. Recommendation on the cooperative's request

The cooperative asks for EUR 0.55 per kg for lots at 84 or above, EUR 0.15 for lots between 82 and 83.9, paid at shipment, with a fixed amount for 2027.

Our view is that the request is reasonable on the principles. The premium is for quality and for the pickers. The cooperative's costs have gone up. A fixed number for 2027 is good for both sides.

On the numbers, a premium of EUR 0.55 on 2,000 kg is €1,100 a year, against €800 at the current rate, a difference of €300. The lower band of EUR 0.15 for the 82 to 83.9 lots matters more: it means we pay for lots we currently do not pay a premium for, and the cooperative's cupping showed 3 of its 14 lots in that band in 2026. Elena estimates the extra cost in total at about €450 to €500 a year on the cooperative volume, which is affordable.

Our recommendation to the board: accept the increase to EUR 0.55 for 84 and above, and the lower band of EUR 0.15 for 82 to 83.9, paid at shipment from Neiva, with the 2027 amount fixed in the contract. Do not agree to a higher figure than that without coming back to the board. Apply the same principle to the Rojas contract, which will need the same change so that the two farms are treated the same. This changes the premium policy from version 1.0 to version 1.1; Elena will prepare the update.

Note: this recommendation changes the policy agreed on 24 September. The board should see it on 15 December, and Giulia will ask for a decision then.
