ESG REPORT FOR BANCA SELLA CREDIT LINE

1. Purpose
Lumen Coffee Roasters operates as a small, dedicated specialty roastery rooted in Turin, with a clear sense of place and purpose. This ESG report, prepared for the credit line with Banca Sella, sets out how the company integrates environmental stewardship, social responsibility, and sound governance into its daily craft of roasting and distributing coffee. The purpose of this document is to demonstrate, in plain and honest terms, that Lumen’s business model is not only viable but also aligned with the long-term values of sustainability, community, and transparency that the bank seeks to support.
Environmentally, Lumen acknowledges that coffee production and roasting carry a footprint. The company is committed to measuring and reducing its energy use on the roasting machine and in its facility, to managing water responsibly, to minimizing waste—especially from packaging and chaff—and to tracking emissions from roasting, shipping, and commuting. The purpose of these indicators is not to claim perfection, but to establish a baseline and to show continuous improvement. Lumen sees environmental care as inseparable from product quality: a well-run roastery wastes less and roasts better.
Socially, Lumen employs a small team of about twelve people, and the report highlights its commitment to fair employment, workplace safety, ongoing training, and engagement with the local community in Turin. The purpose of this section is to show that the company treats its people as partners, not as costs. This includes safe working conditions around hot machinery, opportunities for skill development in roasting and packing, and a culture where meetings and ideas are shared openly. Lumen also values its relationships with wholesale cafés and home subscribers, seeing them as part of a broader community of coffee lovers. The social indicators aim to prove that a small business can be a good neighbor and a fair employer.
Governance at Lumen is deliberately simple but rigorous. The company is managed with clear roles, regular meetings, and a culture of listening to ideas from all staff. The purpose of the governance section is to show that ESG risks—such as supply chain disruptions, price volatility in green coffee, or reputational issues—are taken seriously. Lumen works with importers who share its values, and it maintains open communication with its customers and partners. The governance framework ensures that decisions are made with both short-term operational needs and long-term sustainability in mind.
Finally, the commitments tied to the credit line are the heart of this report. Lumen makes explicit promises to Banca Sella regarding its environmental and social performance, and it agrees to monitor these commitments through regular internal reviews and transparent reporting. The purpose of these commitments is to turn good intentions into measurable actions. Lumen will track its progress on the indicators described above, and it will report honestly on both successes and challenges. The credit line is not just financial support; it is a partnership that holds Lumen accountable to its own stated values. In summary, this ESG report serves to show that Lumen Coffee Roasters is a responsible, resilient, and forward-looking business, worthy of the bank’s trust and investment.

2. Environmental indicators
The central figure of this section is the environmental decision documented under reference code "ZZE-3001", which establishes Lumen Coffee Roasters' baseline for energy, water, waste, and emissions indicators as of January 2024. This code governs the annual reporting cycle to Banca Sella and defines the methodology for calculating all environmental metrics across the roastery's operations in Turin. For the fiscal year ending December 2024, Lumen's total energy consumption was 187,400 kWh, derived from the 15 kg roaster running four days per week, the packing day operations, and the office and warehouse lighting and climate control. The roaster itself, a gas-fired drum model, accounted for 142,000 kWh of this total, with the remaining 45,400 kWh sourced from the grid for electrical equipment including the grinder, packaging sealer, and water filtration pump. Natural gas for the roaster was the primary fossil fuel input, emitting 28.6 metric tons of CO2 equivalent in direct combustion. The company offsets this by purchasing certified renewable energy certificates for 100% of its grid electricity, sourced from a hydroelectric plant in Piedmont, reducing the scope 2 emissions to zero. Water usage totaled 312 cubic meters in 2024, with 278 cubic meters used in the cooling system for the roaster after each batch, and 34 cubic meters for cleaning the roasting floor, employee restrooms, and the small cupping lab. The water is supplied by the municipal Turin aqueduct, and Lumen has installed low-flow aerators on all taps, reducing consumption by 12% compared to 2023. Waste generation amounted to 4.7 metric tons, comprising 2.1 metric tons of organic waste (chaff, defective beans, and spent coffee grounds from the cupping lab), 1.8 metric tons of recyclable packaging (jute sacks from green coffee, cardboard boxes from subscription shipments, and plastic liners), and 0.8 metric tons of mixed waste destined for incineration. The chaff from the roaster is collected separately and donated to a local urban garden, Comune di Torino Orti Urbani, for composting, representing 0.9 metric tons of the organic waste stream. Recyclable packaging is sorted on site and picked up by the municipal recycling service, with a diversion rate of 87% for all non-organic materials. Emissions indicators include scope 1 CO2 equivalent of 28.6 metric tons from natural gas, scope 2 of zero due to renewable certificates, and scope 3 emissions estimated at 112 metric tons, primarily from the transport of green coffee from importers such as Andes Direct, which ships from Peru and Colombia via Genoa port. This scope 3 figure is based on shipping distances for the 24 metric tons of green coffee purchased in 2024, calculated using the GLEC framework. A minor detail is that the chaff composting initiative improved the overall waste diversion rate by 41.7% compared to the 2023 baseline, though this specific percentage reflects only the organic fraction. The roastery's cooling system recirculates water through a closed-loop tank, reducing overall water withdrawal by 65% relative to a single-pass system, and the waste heat from the roaster is captured via a heat exchanger to preheat the building's hot water in winter months, saving an estimated 8,000 kWh annually. Lumen monitors these indicators quarterly, with data logged in a spreadsheet maintained by the operations manager, and audited externally by the bank's ESG reviewer each November. The 2024 figures serve as the baseline for the credit line agreement, and any deviation above a 5% threshold in energy or water intensity per kilogram of roasted coffee triggers a review with Banca Sella. The intensity metrics are 5.2 kWh per kilogram roasted for energy, 8.7 liters per kilogram for water, and 0.13 kilograms of waste per kilogram roasted, with emissions intensity at 0.79 kilograms of CO2 equivalent per kilogram roasted for scope 1 and 2 combined. These numbers exclude the subscription packing day, which adds 12% to the total roasted volume but uses minimal additional energy and water, as packing is manual and uses a small electric sealer. The green coffee inventory is stored in a climate-controlled room with dehumidifiers, drawing 2,400 kWh annually, included in the grid electricity total. Lumen plans to install a solar photovoltaic array on the roof of its leased facility by June 2025, with a capacity of 20 kWp, expected to cover 30% of the electrical load, further reducing scope 2 emissions. The bank requires that all environmental indicators be reported using the same methodology as the DXP-3458 baseline, with no changes to allocation factors without prior approval. The 2025 target is to reduce energy intensity by 8% to 4.8 kWh per kilogram, water intensity by 10% to 7.8 liters per kilogram, and waste intensity by 5% to 0.12 kilograms per kilogram, with emissions intensity falling to 0.73 kilograms of CO2 equivalent per kilogram, assuming the solar array is operational by mid-year. Lumen also commits to sourcing 100% of its green coffee from Rainforest Alliance or Fair Trade certified farms by 2026, which will reduce scope 3 emissions from deforestation-related land use change, though this is not yet measured in the current baseline. The roastery's waste water is discharged into the municipal sewer system, with no industrial contaminants, as only coffee solids and cleaning agents are involved, and the city's treatment plant handles the load. Lumen tests the waste water annually for pH and suspended solids, with results filed under the WKW-5713 documentation, showing compliance with Turin's municipal discharge limits. 3. Social indicators
The social indicators section of Lumen Coffee Roasters’ ESG report for Banca Sella begins with employment metrics. As of December 31, 2023, Lumen employs 12 full-time equivalent staff, all based at the roastery in Turin. The workforce is composed of 8 men and 4 women, with an average age of 34 years. Turnover for the fiscal year 2023 was 8.3%, representing one voluntary departure and one new hire. All employees have permanent contracts, and no temporary or part-time workers are engaged. Lumen ensures that all staff are paid above the Italian national collective bargaining agreement for food industry workers, with the lowest hourly wage being 12.50 euros, which is 15% above the sector minimum. The company tracks gender pay equity: the median hourly wage for women is 13.10 euros, compared to 13.40 euros for men, a gap of 2.2% that Lumen commits to closing by 2026 through annual salary reviews. Employee satisfaction is measured via an anonymous biannual survey; the 2023 survey achieved a 100% response rate and reported an overall satisfaction score of 82 out of 100. One specific metric from that survey is that 74.2% of employees stated they feel their work contributes directly to the company’s sustainability goals, a figure Lumen aims to increase to 80% by the end of 2025.
Safety indicators focus on the roasting and packing operations, which involve exposure to heat, moving machinery, and lifting of 15-kilogram green coffee bags. In 2023, Lumen recorded zero lost-time injuries and zero reportable incidents under Italian workplace safety law (D.Lgs. 81/2008). The company conducted 12 monthly safety inspections, each documented and reviewed by the operations manager. All employees completed mandatory 8-hour general safety training in January 2023, covering fire prevention, manual handling, and emergency evacuation. Additionally, the two roaster operators received specific training on roaster machine safety, including lockout/tagout procedures, totaling 4 hours per operator. The company maintains a first aid kit and a defibrillator on premises, with two employees trained as first aid responders. No workplace accidents or near-misses were reported in 2023, though Lumen encourages near-miss reporting through a confidential digital form; two near-misses were recorded in 2022, both related to bag handling, and corrective actions (installation of bag trolleys and revised lifting protocols) were implemented and verified. The company’s insurance provider conducted a risk assessment in June 2023, which rated Lumen’s safety management as “low risk” with a score of 92 out of 100. Lumen plans to conduct a second risk assessment in 2024, targeting a score of 95.

4. Governance and risk management
The governance structure of Lumen Coffee Roasters is designed to ensure accountability, transparency, and the effective management of environmental, social, and governance (ESG) risks, directly supporting the credit line from Banca Sella. The company is a limited liability company (S.r.l.) registered in Turin, with a three-member board of directors that meets quarterly. The board includes the founder and CEO, the head of sourcing, and an external advisor with expertise in sustainable finance. Day-to-day ESG oversight is delegated to a sustainability committee, formed in January 2024, which comprises four employees: the operations manager, the head of wholesale accounts, the lead roaster, and a rotating representative from the logistics team. This committee reports directly to the board every two months, with written minutes archived in the company’s wiki under the “meetings” area. The committee is responsible for tracking performance against the environmental and social indicators detailed in this report, and for proposing corrective actions when targets are missed. For instance, in March 2025, the committee flagged a 4% increase in natural gas consumption per kilogram of roasted coffee compared to the previous quarter, prompting a review of the roaster’s preheat cycle, which was adjusted by April 2025.

Risk management at Lumen follows a formalized process documented in the “operations” section of the wiki, specifically under a subpage titled “ESG Risk Register.” This register, updated semiannually, identifies, assesses, and prioritizes ESG risks based on likelihood and potential impact on the credit line’s covenants. The three highest-rated risks as of the June 2025 update are: (1) volatile green coffee prices due to climate-related supply disruptions, with a likelihood score of 4 out of 5 and an impact score of 5 out of 5; (2) potential non-compliance with EU deforestation regulations for imported coffee, given that 42% of Lumen’s green coffee (by volume) originates from Ethiopia and Colombia via the importer Andes Direct; and (3) employee turnover in the roasting and packing roles, which reached 18% in 2024, slightly above the specialty coffee industry average of 15%. For each risk, the register specifies a mitigation owner, a timeline, and a key performance indicator. For the deforestation risk, the mitigation owner is the head of sourcing, who has committed to obtaining fully traceable supply chain documentation for all single-origin lots by December 2025, with a quarterly progress check against a target of 90% traceability by Q3 2025—currently at 85% as of July 2025.
The company’s governance also incorporates a formal ESG policy, approved by the board in November 2024 and shared with all twelve employees via the wiki’s “ideas” area. This policy outlines Lumen’s commitment to reducing its carbon footprint, ensuring fair labor practices in its supply chain, and maintaining transparent reporting to stakeholders, including Banca Sella. The policy mandates that all new supplier contracts for green coffee include a clause requiring adherence to the International Coffee Organization’s sustainability standards, with a penalty clause for non-compliance. To date, this clause has been included in contracts with three suppliers representing 68% of Lumen’s annual green coffee volume. The policy also requires that any capital expenditure exceeding 5,000 euros, such as the purchase of a new cooling tray for the 15 kg roaster in February 2025, must undergo a brief ESG impact assessment, reviewed by the sustainability committee before approval. This assessment for the cooling tray, for example, estimated a 3% reduction in electricity consumption per batch, which was verified by the lead roaster after installation.

Risk management at Lumen follows a formalized process that identifies, assesses, and mitigates ESG risks across the supply chain, operations, and customer base. The primary ESG risks have been mapped into a risk register, first compiled in January 2024 and updated quarterly. The register categorizes risks by likelihood and impact, with scores ranging from 1 to 5. As of the most recent update in August 2025, the highest-rated risks are: (1) supply chain disruption due to climate change impacts on coffee-growing regions, scored 4 for likelihood and 5 for impact; (2) water scarcity at the roasting facility in Turin, scored 3 for likelihood and 4 for impact, given the region’s periodic drought alerts; and (3) employee turnover in the roasting team, scored 3 for likelihood and 3 for impact, mitigated by training investments. For each risk, the register specifies a owner, a mitigation plan, and a review date. For example, the climate risk owner is Ingrid Calloway, and the mitigation plan includes diversifying green coffee suppliers—currently sourcing from Andes Direct and two other importers—and requiring all new contracts to include a clause on climate resilience certifications from the origin. The water risk owner is the operations manager, who implemented a monthly leak inspection protocol in March 2025 and installed low-flow fixtures in the wash station, reducing water usage by 12% in the first quarter compared to the baseline. The employee turnover risk owner is the head roaster, who introduced a quarterly retention survey in April 2025; the first survey, completed in July 2025, showed a satisfaction score of 4.2 out of 5 across the twelve employees.

5. Commitments tied to the credit line
The commitments Lumen Coffee Roasters makes to Banca Sella under the credit line are structured as a set of binding, verifiable annual targets, tied directly to the bank’s own sustainability framework. These are not aspirational statements but contractual conditions, monitored through a defined reporting calendar and audited data trail. The first commitment is the reduction of scope 1 and scope 2 greenhouse gas emissions by 18% by December 2026, using calendar year 2023 as the baseline. This is measured from the roaster’s natural gas consumption, the facility’s electricity use, and the small fleet of delivery vans used for wholesale routes within Turin. To verify this, Lumen submits quarterly energy invoices and a monthly log of gas meter readings to the bank’s ESG officer, and an independent third-party verifier (appointed by Banca Sella) audits the annual total each January. The second commitment concerns electricity sourcing: by June 2025, 100% of the electricity purchased for the roastery and the packing area must come from renewable sources, either through a green tariff contract with the local utility or through Guarantees of Origin. Lumen has already signed a two-year contract with a renewable retailer, effective April 2024, and provides the bank with the supplier’s annual disclosure statement. Failure to maintain this contract for more than one quarter triggers a mandatory remediation meeting, and a persistent lapse of six months would constitute an event of default under the credit line.

Lumen has installed two flow meters on the main intake and on the cooling loop, and the bank receives a monthly readout. This is documented through the formal apprenticeship contracts, copies of which are filed with the bank’s compliance team. Safety is tied to the roaster’s specific hazards: the lost-time injury frequency rate (per 200,000 hours worked) must remain below 2.5, and Lumen must conduct a monthly fire and gas-leak drill in the roasting area, with a written log of each drill’s findings. The bank’s loan agreement includes a covenant that any major injury requiring hospitalisation be reported to the bank within 48 hours, and that the company’s insurance carrier confirm no lapse in workers’ compensation coverage. The community commitment is directly linked to the wholesale customer base: Lumen must donate a minimum of 1,200 kilograms of roasted coffee per year to local food banks and community kitchens, specifically through the “Torino Solidale” network. This is verified through donation receipts signed by the receiving organisation, submitted quarterly, and the total is audited annually. A minor detail, but one the bank tracks closely: the percentage of coffee sold to wholesale cafés that are located within 25 kilometres of the roastery must stay above 32.2% of total wholesale volume, as a way to keep local supply chains short and reduce transport emissions. This is calculated from the delivery route logs and the invoicing system, and the bank’s auditor cross-checks a sample of addresses each year.
