# Aurora espresso blend: 2027 recipe change

## Why the recipe changes

The decision to change the Aurora espresso blend recipe was made on 9 February 2026, after a series of supply and quality assessments that had been running since the third quarter of 2025. The existing two-origin formula—60% Brazil Cerrado natural and 40% Ethiopia Guji washed—had served as the roastery’s best seller, accounting for approximately 45% of all coffee sold, since its last revision in 2023. However, three converging pressures made a revision unavoidable: supply reliability, flavour evolution, and consistency across production cycles.

Supply was the most immediate driver. The Brazil Cerrado natural component, sourced through Andes Direct, had become increasingly volatile. In the 2025 harvest, the Cerrado region experienced an unusually dry flowering period followed by erratic rains during cherry maturation, which reduced the volume of grade-1 naturals by roughly 18% compared to the 2024 crop. Andes Direct informed Lumen in October 2025 that the 2026 Brazil Cerrado contracts would carry a 22% premium over the previous year’s price, and that the lot size available to the roastery would be capped at 70% of the 2025 allocation. This directly threatened the ability to maintain a 60% share in the blend without either absorbing unsustainable cost increases or substituting with lower-grade lots that would compromise flavour. At the same time, the Ethiopia Guji washed lot, which Lumen buys as a seasonal single origin from March to August each year, had been shrinking in volume since 2024. The Guji zone’s cooperative network reported declining yields due to aging coffee trees and a shift among some farmers to khat cultivation. The 2025 Guji lot was 12% smaller than the 2024 lot, and the 2026 pre-contract indications suggested a further 8-10% reduction. Relying on a 40% share of a diminishing seasonal origin was no longer prudent for a year-round flagship blend. The roastery’s wholesale clients, notably Café Portello, had already flagged concerns about potential supply gaps during the off-season months when the Guji lot is exhausted. The new recipe needed to reduce dependence on both origins while preserving the blend’s identity.

Flavour was the second, equally important reason. The existing blend delivered a reliable profile: chocolate and body from the Brazil Cerrado, with floral and citrus notes from the Ethiopia Guji. But over the past eighteen months, cupping feedback from both internal staff and external customers had highlighted a growing perception of flatness. The Brazil Cerrado natural, while excellent for body, had a tendency to mute the high notes of the Guji as the blend aged past four weeks post-roast. Subscribers who received their weekly shipments on packing day sometimes noted that the coffee tasted “duller” by the second week of consumption. In blind triangle tests conducted in December 2025, the quality team found that 68% of participants could distinguish a two-week-old batch of Aurora from a fresh batch, with the older batch consistently described as “less lively” and “one-dimensional.” The roastery’s head of product, Ravi Lindqvist, argued that the blend needed a third component that could add a bright, fruity layer that would persist through the coffee’s shelf life, compensating for the natural muting of the Guji’s florals. Additionally, the market for specialty espresso had shifted. Customers at Café Portello and other wholesale accounts were increasingly asking for blends that offered more complexity without sacrificing the milk-friendly body that Aurora was known for. The 60/40 split had been designed in 2021 for a different moment in specialty coffee, when clarity and separation of flavours were prized. By early 2026, the prevailing trend was toward layered, juicy espressos with a pronounced sweetness and a clean finish. The old recipe, while competent, was no longer exciting the palates of the roastery’s most discerning wholesale buyers.

Consistency was the third pillar of the decision. The two-origin blend was inherently fragile because its flavour profile depended entirely on the year-to-year quality of two specific lots. In 2024, the Brazil Cerrado lot had been exceptionally clean and chocolate-forward, and the Guji lot had a strong jasmine note. In 2025, the Brazil lot showed more earthiness and less sweetness, while the Guji lot was more citrus-dominant and less floral. The resulting 2025 Aurora was noticeably different from the 2024 version, and while some customers appreciated the variation, many home subscribers and wholesale accounts reported inconsistency. One café owner told the sales team that his regular customers had complained that “the espresso tastes different than last spring.” The problem was not that the coffee was bad—it was still good—but that the brand promise of a consistent house espresso was being undermined. By adding a third origin, Lumen could create a more stable base: if one component’s harvest underperforms, the other two can compensate. The new recipe would also allow the roastery to use a washed Central American coffee—specifically a washed Costa Rica Tarrazú—at 20% of the blend. This origin offers a clean, apple-like acidity and a syrupy body that bridges the chocolate of the Brazil and the floral-citrus of the Guji. In cupping trials, the Costa Rica Tarrazú showed less year-to-year variation than either the Brazil Cerrado or the Ethiopia Guji, making it a reliable anchor for consistency. The new proportions—50% Brazil Cerrado, 30% Ethiopia Guji, 20% Costa Rica Tarrazú—reduce the Brazil share by ten percentage points and the Guji share by ten percentage points, cutting the risk from a bad harvest of either origin by roughly a quarter. The Brazil still provides body and chocolate, the Guji still provides florals and citrus, and the Costa Rica adds a bright, clean sweetness that holds up through the coffee’s shelf life. In accelerated aging tests, the three-origin blend scored 4.2 out of 5 for flavour stability at six weeks post-roast, compared to 3.6 for the two-origin blend at the same age.

The change was not made lightly. Aurora is the roastery’s flagship, and any alteration carries risk. But the combination of supply constraints on both existing origins, the flavour drift that had been documented over multiple cupping sessions, and the clear evidence that a third origin could improve consistency across harvests and over the coffee’s shelf life made the decision on 15 September 2026 inevitable. The new recipe will be locked in for the 2027 production year, with the first batches roasted in January 2027. The roastery has already secured contracts for the Costa Rica Tarrazú through Andes Direct, ensuring that the third component will be available in sufficient volume for the entire year. The existing note on the Aurora espresso blend wiki page will be updated to reflect the new proportions and the rationale, and all internal documentation—including the roast profile—will be revised accordingly. The sensory results of the trial blends, which confirmed the viability of the new recipe, are detailed in the following section of this document.

## The new three-origin recipe

The new three-origin recipe for Aurora espresso blend, effective from the first roasting day of January 2027, is a 50/30/20 split: 50% Brazil Cerrado natural, 30% Ethiopia Guji washed, and 20% Colombia Huila washed. This decision was finalised on 15 November 2026 after eight months of trials and represents a total investment of €76,222.07 in green coffee inventory, trial roasting, and sensory evaluation. The new proportions deliberately reduce the Brazil Cerrado component by ten percentage points and the Ethiopia Guji component by ten percentage points, while introducing a third origin that was never part of the original recipe. Each origin now plays a distinct and non-overlapping role in the blend’s structure, designed to improve supply resilience, flavour complexity, and consistency across seasonal variations.

The Brazil Cerrado natural, sourced from Fazenda Santa Tereza in Minas Gerais, remains the largest component at 50%. Its role is unchanged from the existing recipe: it provides the blend’s foundational body, chocolate-forward sweetness, and low acidity that make Aurora work as both a straight espresso and with milk. The natural process, sun-dried on raised beds for eighteen days, gives heavy mouthfeel and notes of dark chocolate and roasted almond. In the new recipe, the reduction from 60% to 50% means that the Brazil Cerrado no longer dominates the flavour profile as heavily. Instead, it acts as a stable backbone that supports the other two origins without overwhelming them. This is important because the Brazil Cerrado lot from Fazenda Santa Tereza has shown batch-to-batch variation in sugar content over the past three years, and reducing its share lowers the risk of a single origin dragging down the entire blend’s quality. The 50% proportion still ensures that the blend retains the full, round body that Café Portello and other wholesale cafés expect from Aurora when pulling ristretto or lungo shots.

The Ethiopia Guji washed, sourced from the Buku Sayisa cooperative in the Guji zone, drops from 40% to 30%. Its role in the new recipe is to provide florals and a citrus finish, just as it did in the original blend, but now with more precision and less risk of astringency. The washed process, fermented for thirty-six hours and dried on raised beds for twelve days, yields jasmine, lemon zest, and a clean, tea-like finish. At 30%, the Ethiopia Guji still contributes enough aromatic lift to distinguish Aurora from darker, one-dimensional espresso blends, but it no longer carries the burden of being the sole source of acidity and complexity. In the original recipe, the 40% share sometimes caused the blend to taste brittle or sharp when the Guji lot arrived with higher-than-expected acidity, especially in the months of February and March. By reducing it to 30%, the blend gains a more forgiving acidity profile that holds steady across the six-month window when the Guji lot is sold as a seasonal single origin from March to August. The new recipe also allows the Ethiopia Guji to work in tandem with the third origin, rather than competing for the same flavour space.

The third origin, Colombia Huila washed, enters at 20%. This is the most significant change. The Colombia Huila is sourced from the Finca El Oso in the municipality of Pitalito, Huila department, a 2.1-hectare farm owned by María Elena Rojas. It is a washed Caturra and Colombia variety lot, fermented for forty-eight hours in open tanks and dried on parabolic solar dryers for fifteen days. Its role in the blend is to add medium-body fruit sweetness, a wine-like acidity, and a lingering finish that bridges the chocolate of Brazil and the florals of Ethiopia. Specifically, the Colombia Huila contributes red apple, panela, and a hint of red grape, which rounds out the citrus from Ethiopia and adds depth to the chocolate from Brazil. This origin was chosen after tasting eighteen different candidate lots from six countries, including a natural Costa Rica from Tarrazú, a washed Kenya from Nyeri, and a honey-process Honduras from Marcala. The Colombia Huila won because its acidity is bright but not aggressive, its sweetness is pronounced but not cloying, and its body, while lighter than Brazil, is still full enough to avoid a hollow finish. In the cupping trials conducted on 22 September 2026, the 50/30/20 blend scored 86.75 on the Specialty Coffee Association scale, compared to 85.25 for the original 60/40 blend and 84.50 for a 55/25/20 variant that used a natural Peru instead of Colombia. The Colombia Huila also showed the best stability when roasted to the same Agtron 58-60 target: its density and moisture content, measured at 685 grams per litre and 11.2% respectively, aligned closely with the Brazil Cerrado and Ethiopia Guji, reducing the need for separate roast profiles.

Each origin in the new recipe has a defined structural function. The Brazil Cerrado at 50% supplies the body and chocolate base, acting as the blend’s anchor. The Ethiopia Guji at 30% supplies the florals and citrus top note, acting as the blend’s aromatic signature. The Colombia Huila at 20% supplies the fruit sweetness and mid-palate complexity, acting as the bridge between the two. This three-part structure is more stable than the two-origin structure because it distributes risk. If the Brazil Cerrado lot from Fazenda Santa Tereza has a poor harvest in 2026, the blend can absorb a 10% reduction in that origin without losing its identity, because the Colombia Huila can partially compensate for body with its own medium body and sweetness. If the Ethiopia Guji lot from Buku Sayisa arrives with lower floral intensity, the Colombia Huila’s red apple and panela notes still provide a lively acidity. The blend no longer relies on a single origin to deliver a specific attribute entirely on its own.

The new proportions also improve consistency across the year. The original 60/40 recipe was heavily dependent on the Ethiopia Guji lot being available from March to August, and when that lot sold out, the blend had to be reformulated with a different washed Ethiopian origin, often from Yirgacheffe or Sidama, which changed the flavour profile noticeably. In the new recipe, the Colombia Huila lot from Finca El Oso is available year-round, with two harvests per year: the main crop from October to December and the fly crop from April to June. This means that from January 2027 onward, the blend will always contain at least 20% Colombia Huila regardless of the Ethiopia Guji seasonality. The Brazil Cerrado lot is also available year-round from Fazenda Santa Tereza, with a single harvest from May to September that is stored in GrainPro bags and held at the warehouse in Turin until the next harvest. The only seasonal component remains the Ethiopia Guji, which is replaced from September to February each year by a washed Ethiopia Yirgacheffe lot from the Worka cooperative, sourced through the same importer, Andes Direct. When the Yirgacheffe replaces the Guji, the blend becomes 50% Brazil Cerrado, 30% Ethiopia Yirgacheffe, and 20% Colombia Huila. The Yirgacheffe lot has a slightly more floral and less citrus profile than the Guji, but the Colombia Huila’s fruit sweetness bridges that gap, and the cupping scores for the Yirgacheffe variant tested on 8 October 2026 were 86.25, only 0.5 points lower than the Guji variant. This seasonal substitution is now documented in the wiki under the Aurora espresso blend page, and the roasting team will switch from Guji to Yirgacheffe on the first roasting day of September each year.

The role of each origin is also reflected in the blend’s recommended recipe, which remains unchanged at 18 grams in, 38 grams out, 27 to 30 seconds. The new 50/30/20 recipe was tested on the La Marzocco Strada at the roastery on 12 October 2026, with eighteen shots pulled by three baristas. The extraction yield averaged 19.8% with a total dissolved solids of 8.6%, both within the target range for a medium roast espresso blend. The body was rated as 7.5 out of 10 by the tasting panel, compared to 7.8 for the original 60/40 blend, but the complexity rating rose from 6.2 to 7.9, and the finish clarity rating rose from 6.5 to 8.1. The slight loss in body is offset by the gain in complexity and finish, and the panel judged the new blend to be more interesting and more versatile across different milk textures. For milk-based drinks, the Colombia Huila’s panela sweetness carries through the milk without clashing, and the Ethiopia Guji’s florals remain present but not overpowering.

The new recipe also includes a specific note about the Colombia Huila’s processing and handling. The washed Colombia Huila from Finca El Oso is fermented for forty-eight hours, which is longer than the Ethiopia Guji’s thirty-six hours. This longer fermentation gives the Colombia Huila a slightly fruitier and more wine-like character, but it also means that the green coffee is more sensitive to storage conditions. The warehouse in Turin will store the Colombia Huila in the same climate-controlled room as the Brazil Cerrado, at 18 degrees Celsius and 60% relative humidity, but in separate GrainPro bags to avoid cross-contamination of aromas. The roasting team will also calibrate the moisture loss during roasting: the Colombia Huila loses 14.5% moisture on average, compared to 13.8% for Brazil Cerrado and 14.1% for Ethiopia Guji, so the batch weights for each origin in the blend will be calculated based on dry weight after roasting, not green weight. This ensures that the final 50/30/20 proportion is by weight of roasted coffee, not green coffee.

The 64.3% of the trial blend’s panelists rated the new recipe as an improvement over the original, with the remaining panelists noting no significant difference. That percentage was recorded during the blind cupping on 22 September 2026, where twelve of the eighteen panelists preferred the new blend. The six panelists who preferred the original cited a slightly fuller body, but all eighteen agreed that the new blend had better clarity and a more balanced acidity. No panelist rated the new blend as worse overall.

The new three-origin recipe is now locked into the wiki under the Aurora espresso blend page, replacing the old 60/40 table. The Brazil Cerrado remains at 50%, the Ethiopia Guji at 30%, and the Colombia Huila at 20%. The roles are stated explicitly: Brazil Cerrado for body and chocolate, Ethiopia Guji for florals and citrus, Colombia Huila for fruit sweetness and mid-palate complexity. The seasonal substitution of Ethiopia Guji with Ethiopia Yirgacheffe is noted, with the same proportions and the same Colombia Huila component. No other origins will be substituted without a full trial and sensory panel, and any future change must be approved by the head roaster and the sourcing manager. The new recipe will be used for all Aurora espresso blend production starting on the first roasting day of January 2027, which is a Wednesday. The first batch will be roasted at 8:00 AM, charged at 205 degrees Celsius, with first crack expected at 9:10 and drop at 11:30, exactly as the existing roast profile specifies. The development time of 1:45 will remain unchanged, because the three origins all reach first crack within a ten-second window of each other when roasted together.

## Sensory results of the trials

The sensory trials for the new three-origin Aurora espresso blend were conducted over a ten-week period from early September to mid-November 2027. The central figure in this evaluation was "Odette Vasquez", the head of quality control at Lumen Coffee Roasters, who personally designed and led every cupping session and espresso tasting. Under her direction, the trial process involved four distinct blend variations, each tested against the current two-origin recipe as a control. The final decision to adopt the 50/30/20 split—50% Brazil Cerrado natural, 30% Ethiopia Guji washed, and 20% Colombia Huila washed—rested entirely on the sensory results that Odette and her team documented.

The first series of cuppings took place on 5 September 2027. Four trial blends were prepared, each with a different third origin: a washed Colombia Huila, a natural-process Costa Rica Tarrazu, a washed Kenya Nyeri, and a natural-process Ethiopia Yirgacheffe. The existing 60/40 blend was cupped alongside them as a baseline. Each sample was roasted to a medium level, targeting the same Agtron 58-60 range as the current Aurora, and rested for exactly 48 hours before cupping. Odette used the standard Specialty Coffee Association cupping protocol, with five cups per sample, and a panel of six trained tasters from the roasting team and two baristas from Café Portello. The results were recorded on a 100-point scale, focusing on aroma, flavour, aftertaste, acidity, body, balance, and overall impression.

The Colombia Huila addition scored highest in this initial round, with an average of 87.3 points, compared to the control’s 85.1. Tasters noted that the Huila contributed a clean, apple-like acidity and a silky mouthfeel that complemented the chocolate notes of the Brazil Cerrado without overwhelming the floral character of the Ethiopia Guji. The Costa Rica Tarrazu scored 86.5, but tasters reported a slightly one-dimensional sweetness that flattened the complexity. The Kenya Nyeri, at 84.7, introduced a pronounced blackcurrant acidity that clashed with the Brazil’s low-acid profile, creating a sharp, unbalanced cup. The natural Ethiopia Yirgacheffe, at 83.9, added fermented fruit notes that the panel found distracting, as they masked the Guji’s delicate florals.

Based on these results, Odette narrowed the trials to the Colombia Huila blend and proceeded to a second phase focused on proportion adjustments. Between 12 September and 3 October, she tested five variations of the 50/30/20 split, systematically shifting the percentages while keeping the total at 100%. The fixed 20% for the third origin was chosen after trials with 15% Huila (which lacked impact) and 25% Huila (which dominated the cup). At 15%, the Huila’s apple acidity was barely perceptible, and the blend tasted nearly identical to the current two-origin recipe. At 25%, the Huila pushed the Brazil Cerrado to the background, and the espresso became too bright, with a lingering tartness that tasters described as “unpleasantly sharp.” The 20% proportion was the sweet spot: the Huila’s acidity was present but balanced, the Brazil’s body remained full, and the Guji’s florals still came through as a top note.

On 10 October 2027, Odette conducted a detailed espresso tasting at the roastery’s lab, using a La Marzocco Strada machine with a standard 18-gram dose, 38-gram yield, and a 28-second extraction time. The 50/30/20 blend was pulled side by side with the current 60/40 recipe. The new blend produced a crema that was slightly lighter in colour—a pale hazelnut versus the control’s deeper ochre—but it was thicker and more persistent, lasting over two minutes in the cup. The aroma was notably more complex: the control had a straightforward chocolate-and-citrus scent, while the new blend added a green apple note that the panel unanimously praised. In flavour, the control was described as “reliable” and “comforting,” with a heavy chocolate body and a clean citrus finish. The new blend was described as “layered” and “dynamic,” with the chocolate body still present but softened, followed by a mid-palate floral wave from the Guji, and a bright, apple-like finish from the Huila. The aftertaste was longer and cleaner, with none of the slight astringency that sometimes appeared in the control’s finish.

A second espresso tasting on 17 October involved milk beverages. Five lattes were prepared using the same 18-gram dose, with 150 ml of whole milk steamed to 60 °C. The control latte was sweet and chocolate-forward, with the Guji florals mostly lost in the milk. The new blend’s latte retained more complexity: the chocolate was still prominent, but the apple acidity cut through the milk fat, creating a cleaner, more refreshing taste. Two tasters from Café Portello, who attended this session, noted that the new blend “woke up” the milk drink, making it less heavy than the current Aurora. Odette recorded these observations in her notes, emphasising that the new blend performed equally well in espresso and milk, a key requirement for the house espresso.

A final cupping session on 20 November 2027, with the same panel of six tasters, confirmed the results. The 50/30/20 blend scored an average of 88.1 points, a full three points above the control. Individual scores ranged from 87.5 to 88.6, with no taster scoring it below 87. The control scored between 84.8 and 85.4. The panel’s written comments highlighted the blend’s improved balance and complexity. One taster wrote: “The Huila adds a new dimension without breaking the old one. It’s still Aurora, but more interesting.” Another noted: “The acidity is brighter but not harsh. This will work well for filter coffee too, if we ever offer it that way.” Odette’s own summary, dated 22 November 2027, stated that the new recipe met all three goals: supply stability (by reducing reliance on two origins), flavour improvement (by adding a third origin’s distinct character), and consistency (by providing a buffer against seasonal variations in the Guji lot). She recommended immediate adoption, with a rollout in early 2028.

A minor detail worth noting: the final sensory report was submitted to the Lumen Coffee Roasters wiki on 20 February 2028, serving as the official record of the trials. By that date, the new blend had already been approved by the team, and Odette’s documentation ensured that the wiki’s existing note on Aurora would be updated to reflect the 50/30/20 recipe. The sensory results were also shared with Café Portello and a small group of home subscribers in a blind tasting on 10 December 2027, where 14 out of 18 participants preferred the new blend over the current one. Those results, while informal, reinforced Odette’s formal cupping data.

In summary, the sensory trials demonstrated that the addition of 20% Colombia Huila washed to the existing 50% Brazil Cerrado natural and 30% Ethiopia Guji washed created a more complex, balanced, and consistent espresso. The new blend retained the chocolate body and low acidity of the Brazil, preserved the floral and citrus notes of the Guji, and added a clean apple acidity and silky mouthfeel from the Huila. The 50/30/20 proportion was the only one that achieved this balance, as tested across multiple cuppings and espresso tastings. Desmond Delacroix’s leadership of the trials ensured that the decision was based on rigorous, repeatable sensory data, and the results were clear: the new recipe outperformed the old one in every metric—aroma, flavour, aftertaste, acidity, body, balance, and overall impression. The change was not merely a supply-driven adjustment; it was a genuine improvement to the cup.

## Rollout to cafés and subscribers

The rollout of the new Aurora espresso blend recipe will begin on Monday, 4 June 2027. This date was chosen after careful coordination with the roasting schedule, the subscription packing day, and the delivery cycles of our wholesale café partners. 1 January 2027 is the single day on which every customer segment will first receive the new three-origin blend, with no transitional period where old and new recipes are shipped simultaneously. All coffee roasted after Friday 28 May 2027 will be the new recipe, and all coffee packed and shipped on the weekly packing day of Wednesday 2 June 2027 will be the new blend. The goal is to avoid confusion and ensure that every bag of Aurora that leaves the roastery from that date forward is consistent.

The change reaches three distinct customer groups: wholesale cafés, home subscribers, and one-off retail buyers through our website. Each group has a slightly different communication and delivery timeline, but all converge on the same launch date.

For wholesale cafés, the most important partner is Café Portello, which accounts for roughly 12% of all Aurora sales by volume. We have already scheduled a private tasting for the head barista and owner at Café Portello on 12 May 2027. At that tasting, we will present the final 50/30/20 blend and share the sensory results from the trials. Café Portello will receive a pre-shipment of 5 kilograms of the new blend on 26 May 2027, so they can dial in their espresso recipe and train their staff before the public launch. All other wholesale cafés will receive a one-kilogram sample bag with their regular order during the week of 19 May 2027. This sample bag will be accompanied by a printed card explaining the new origins and the rationale for the change, as well as a QR code linking to a short video of our head roaster describing the flavour profile. The wholesale order form on our website will be updated on 1 June 2027 to reflect the new recipe, and any café that places an order after that date will automatically receive the new blend. For cafés that have standing weekly orders, the change is automatic on the delivery that falls on or after 12 December 2026. We have confirmed that all 27 wholesale accounts will have received their sample and training materials by 1 June 2027. For any café that expresses concern about the change, we have reserved a two-hour slot on the morning of 3 June 2027 for a video call with our head roaster to answer questions.

For home subscribers, the rollout is managed through the weekly packing day. Subscriptions are packed every Wednesday, and the shipping cutoff for that week is Tuesday at 12:00 CET. The last shipment of the old recipe will be packed on Wednesday 28 May 2027. All subscribers whose next scheduled shipment falls on or after Wednesday 26 December 2028 will receive the new blend. This means that subscribers on a two-week or monthly cycle may receive the new blend as early as 4 June or as late as 18 June, depending on their last shipment date. We will send an email to all subscribers on 25 May 2027, announcing the change and explaining the new flavour profile. The email will include a link to a detailed blog post on our website, which will go live on the same day. The blog post will describe the role of each origin: the Brazil Cerrado at 50% for body and chocolate notes, the Ethiopia Guji at 30% for florals and a citrus finish, and the new third origin, a washed Colombian from the Huila region, at 20% for added sweetness and a clean, caramel-like mouthfeel that bridges the other two components. The email will also include a tasting note card that subscribers can print at home. For subscribers who have a recurring order scheduled to ship on 10 November 2027, we will include a handwritten note from the roasting team thanking them for being early adopters. The subscription management page on our website will be updated on 1 June 2027 to show the new ingredient breakdown.

For one-off retail buyers who purchase through the website, the product page for Aurora espresso blend will be updated on 1 June 2027. The old blend will be removed from inventory on 31 May 2027, and no further bags of the old recipe will be sold. Any bag of Aurora ordered before 31 May 2027 will be the old recipe; any bag ordered on or after 1 June 2027 will be the new recipe, but because shipping takes 1-3 business days, the actual receipt date for most customers will be around 8 January 2028. We have updated the product description to note that the recipe changed on 27 October 2028, and we have added a small banner on the product page that reads "New recipe as of 11 June 2028" in a muted grey font. The banner will remain up until 1 July 2027. For customers who purchase a bag in person at the roastery, we will have a small stand with tasting notes and a sample of the new blend available from 4 July 2028 onward.

The decaf blend, Night Shift, is unaffected by this change and will remain the same recipe. The single origin Ethiopia Guji will continue to be sold as a separate product, but its seasonal availability window (March to August) means that from June onward, the washed Guji lot used in Aurora will be drawn from the same lot as the single origin. This is a minor operational detail that does not affect the customer experience, but it is noted in the wiki for internal reference. The Colombia Huila origin used in the new recipe is sourced through the same importer, Andes Direct, and we have secured a contract for 1,200 kilograms of green coffee for the 2027 calendar year, which covers the expected volume of Aurora at the new 20% inclusion rate. The exact cupping score for the Colombia Huila lot used in the final trial was 88.1%, which is slightly higher than the Brazil Cerrado lot (86.4%) and the Ethiopia Guji lot (87.9%), ensuring that the new component does not lower the overall quality of the blend.

The rollout plan was reviewed and approved at the weekly operations meeting on 15 April 2027. No further changes to the recipe or the rollout schedule are anticipated. The wiki page for Aurora espresso blend will be updated on 1 June 2027 to reflect the new proportions and the new origin. The existing note about the roast profile and the recommended recipe (18 g in, 38 g out, 27-30 seconds) remains unchanged, as trials confirmed that the new blend performs identically on the same roast curve. The Agtron reading of 58-60 is also unchanged. The packaging will not change; the bags will continue to display the same Aurora label, but the ingredient list on the back will be updated to show the three origins. We have ordered 5,000 new labels, which will arrive by 25 May 2027, and the packing team will switch to the new labels on the morning of 31 May 2027.

All communication materials have been drafted and reviewed. The email to subscribers, the blog post, the wholesale sample card, and the product page update all use the same language: "Aurora espresso blend has a new recipe as of 19 November 2026. We have added a washed Colombia Huila at 20% to bring more sweetness and balance, while keeping the classic chocolate body from Brazil and the floral lift from Ethiopia." No mention of prices, commercial terms, or roast profiles appears in any customer-facing material. The internal wiki note for the blend will be updated to include a link to this rollout document. The only metric that customers will see is the 57.6% cupping score, which is referenced on the tasting card included with the first shipment of the new blend.

The final check before launch will occur on 2 June 2027, when the head roaster and the operations manager will taste a shot of the new blend pulled on the café machine in the roastery. If any issue is identified, we have a contingency plan to delay the rollout by one week, but based on the trial results this is extremely unlikely. All wholesale partners have been informed of the 6 April 2027 date and have confirmed their readiness. Café Portello has already scheduled a staff training session for 28 May 2027 using the pre-shipment sample. Subscribers have received the initial announcement email and the response has been positive. The new recipe will be the only version of Aurora sold from 12 October 2027 forward, and the old recipe will be discontinued entirely. No retrospective blending or transitional batches will occur. The rollout is designed to be clean, simple, and transparent for every customer.
