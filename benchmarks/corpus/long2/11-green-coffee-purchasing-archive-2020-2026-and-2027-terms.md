# Green coffee purchasing archive 2020-2026 and 2027 terms

## Purchasing archive 2020-2026

The purchasing archive begins in January 2020, when Lumen Coffee Roasters had been operating for just over a year. The first recorded purchase of that year was a single lot of Brazil Fazenda Santa Ines, natural process, 300 kilograms at a price of €4.20 per kilogram, bought through Andes Direct. The quality note from the cupping table described it as "clean, with milk chocolate and peanut, suitable for Aurora espresso base." In March 2020, Lumen added its first Ethiopian single origin, a washed Yirgacheffe from the Chelbesa cooperative, 150 kilograms at €5.80 per kilogram, also through Andes Direct. The decision was to use it as a limited seasonal offering. By April 2020, the pandemic had disrupted shipping, and Lumen bought a single lot of Colombian Huila, washed, 200 kilograms at €4.50 per kilogram from Andes Direct, as a contingency for the espresso blend. The quality notes recorded "bright acidity, red apple, medium body; will blend well with Brazil." In June 2020, a second Brazil lot arrived, Fazenda Cachoeira, natural, 250 kilograms at €4.10 per kilogram, again from Andes Direct, intended for the single-origin subscription. The year closed with a November purchase of a decaf lot, Colombia Huila EA decaf, 100 kilograms at €6.00 per kilogram, from a new importer, Caffe Imports, to launch the Night Shift decaf product. The total green coffee purchased in 2020 was 1,000 kilograms.

In 2021, Lumen doubled its purchasing volume. January saw the first lot of the year, Brazil Fazenda Santa Ines again, 400 kilograms at €4.30 per kilogram from Andes Direct, for the Aurora espresso base. In March, a washed Ethiopian Guji from the Taye Belay washing station, 250 kilograms at €6.20 per kilogram, was bought from Andes Direct; the quality note read "jasmine, lemon, silky; will be the single origin Ethiopia Guji for the year." April brought a Colombian Huila, washed, 300 kilograms at €4.70 per kilogram from Andes Direct, and a second Brazil lot, Fazenda Cachoeira, natural, 300 kilograms at €4.20 per kilogram from the same importer. The decision on the Colombian was to reserve half for a summer espresso special. In June, Lumen bought its first lot from a Central American origin: Costa Rica Tarrazu, honey process, 150 kilograms at €5.50 per kilogram from a new importer, Mercanta. The quality note highlighted "brown sugar, orange zest, creamy; for subscriber single-origin." August saw a repeat of the Ethiopian Guji, 200 kilograms at €6.40 per kilogram from Andes Direct, as the single origin continued to sell well. October brought a decaf lot, Colombia Huila EA decaf, 200 kilograms at €6.10 per kilogram from Caffe Imports, for the Night Shift. November was a heavy month: a large Brazil Fazenda Santa Ines lot, 500 kilograms at €4.40 per kilogram from Andes Direct, to cover the espresso blend through early 2022, and a small lot of Kenya Nyeri, washed, 100 kilograms at €7.00 per kilogram from Mercanta, as a trial for a new single origin. The quality notes on the Kenya were "blackcurrant, tomato, complex; will not be repeated due to low customer interest." Total 2021 purchases were 2,200 kilograms.

The year 2022 saw further growth and the introduction of a hedging rule in the wiki, though it was not yet formalized. In January, Lumen bought Brazil Fazenda Santa Ines, 500 kilograms at €4.50 per kilogram from Andes Direct. The price had risen slightly from the previous year. In March, a washed Ethiopian Guji from Taye Belay, 300 kilograms at €6.60 per kilogram from Andes Direct, was purchased. The quality note observed "floral, peach, tea-like; consistent with previous lots." April brought Colombia Huila, washed, 400 kilograms at €4.90 per kilogram from Andes Direct, and a second Brazil lot, Fazenda Cachoeira, natural, 350 kilograms at €4.30 per kilogram from Andes Direct. The decision was to use the Colombia in the Aurora espresso and the Brazil for subscription. In June, a new origin appeared: Guatemala Antigua, washed, 200 kilograms at €5.20 per kilogram from Mercanta, for a summer single-origin subscription. The quality note read "cocoa, almond, mild; good but not outstanding." In August, Lumen bought its first lot of a natural Ethiopian Guji, 200 kilograms at €6.80 per kilogram from Andes Direct, to offer alongside the washed version. The decision was to cup both and select one for the main single origin; the natural was chosen for its fruit-forward profile. October brought a decaf lot, Colombia Huila EA decaf, 250 kilograms at €6.20 per kilogram from Caffe Imports, for the Night Shift. November was again heavy: Brazil Fazenda Santa Ines, 600 kilograms at €4.60 per kilogram from Andes Direct, and a small lot of Sumatra Gayo, wet-hulled, 150 kilograms at €5.80 per kilogram from Mercanta, as a test for a dark roast single origin. The quality notes on the Sumatra were "herbal, spicy, low acidity; not popular with subscribers." Total 2022 purchases were 3,050 kilograms.

In January 2023, the hedging rule was informally applied for the first time. Lumen estimated its annual volume at 3,500 kilograms and fixed the price for 70% of that, or 2,450 kilograms, with Andes Direct. The first fixed lot was Brazil Fazenda Santa Ines, 700 kilograms at €4.70 per kilogram, delivered in January. In February, a fixed lot of Colombia Huila, washed, 500 kilograms at €5.00 per kilogram arrived from Andes Direct. March brought a fixed lot of Ethiopian Guji, washed, 400 kilograms at €6.90 per kilogram from Andes Direct. The quality note on the Ethiopian was "clean, floral, medium body; excellent for single origin." In April, the remaining fixed volume was filled with Brazil Fazenda Cachoeira, natural, 500 kilograms at €4.40 per kilogram from Andes Direct, and a smaller fixed lot of Costa Rica Tarrazu, honey, 200 kilograms at €5.60 per kilogram from Mercanta. The market price portion was bought in May: a spot lot of Colombia Huila, washed, 150 kilograms at €5.20 per kilogram from Andes Direct, and a spot lot of Brazil Fazenda Santa Ines, 200 kilograms at €4.80 per kilogram from the same importer. In June, Lumen bought a spot lot of Ethiopian Guji, natural, 150 kilograms at €7.00 per kilogram from Andes Direct, for the summer subscription. The decision was to use the natural as a limited edition. In August, a decaf lot, Colombia Huila EA decaf, 300 kilograms at €6.30 per kilogram was purchased from Caffe Imports. September brought a spot lot of Kenya Nyeri, washed, 100 kilograms at €7.20 per kilogram from Mercanta, but it was not repeated. October saw a fixed lot for the next cycle: Brazil Fazenda Santa Ines, 600 kilograms at €4.90 per kilogram from Andes Direct, as part of the second half hedging. November added a fixed lot of Colombia Huila, 400 kilograms at €5.10 per kilogram from Andes Direct. The year closed with a decaf lot, Colombia Huila EA decaf, 250 kilograms at €6.40 per kilogram from Caffe Imports. Total 2023 purchases were 3,650 kilograms.

## 2027 terms: the new hedging rule

From January 2027, the hedging rule that has governed Lumen Coffee Roasters since January 2026 is replaced in its entirety. The previous rule, which fixed prices for six months on 70% of expected volume and bought the remaining 30% at market, is revoked. The decision to change the rule was made by Callum Pemberton, the head of sourcing, who presented the new framework to the operations team in November 2026 after reviewing the July 2026 fixings and the projected green coffee market for the coming year. The new rule is effective for all purchase contracts signed on or after 1 January 2027, and it applies to all lots delivered in calendar year 2027 and beyond.

The new hedging rule is built on a tiered coverage system that reduces the fixed portion to 50% of expected volume, extends the hedging horizon to twelve months, and introduces a rolling quarterly adjustment mechanism. Under the old rule, Lumen fixed 70% of its expected needs every six months, which meant that in a rising market, the company was forced to lock in high prices for a large share of its volume well before those beans were roasted and sold. The new rule lowers the initial fixed coverage to 50% of expected volume for the first twelve months, and it allows for an additional 20% to be fixed at any point during that twelve-month window, but only in increments of 5% of the total expected volume, and only when the spot price for the relevant origin drops to or below the trailing six-month average. This is the core change: the old rule fixed a flat 70% regardless of price movement, while the new rule makes the second tranche of coverage price-contingent, meaning Lumen only locks in that extra 20% when the market is favourable, not simply because the calendar says it is time to fix.

For the first twelve months of the rule, the base coverage is calculated as follows. On the first business day of January, April, July, and October, Lumen estimates the green coffee volume needed for the following twelve months, based on the sales forecast from the previous quarter, adjusted for the average growth rate of the last three years, which is currently 12% per year. The estimate is made in kilograms of green coffee, and it includes all blends, single origins, and decaf, but it does not include sample lots or trial roasts. From that estimate, Lumen fixes the price for 50% of the volume immediately, using contracts with importers that have a fixed price and a delivery window of no more than 60 days. The remaining 50% is left unpriced, but it is not simply bought at market when needed. Instead, Lumen establishes a rolling schedule of price fixings that occur every two weeks, and at each fixing date, Lumen can choose to fix up to 5% of the total expected volume, but only if the average spot price for the relevant origin over the previous five trading days is below the average spot price for that same origin over the preceding six months. If the price condition is not met, no fixing occurs on that date, and the volume remains unpriced until the next fixing date, which is two weeks later.

This price-contingent mechanism is the heart of the new rule, and it replaces the old automatic 70% coverage. The effect is that Lumen will never be forced to fix a large portion of its volume at a peak, but it also means that in a steadily rising market, the company could end up with a larger unpriced position than before, because the price condition may rarely be met. To mitigate this, the new rule includes a floor and a ceiling on the total unpriced position. At any point in time, the unpriced volume for any origin cannot exceed 60% of the expected annual volume for that origin, and it cannot fall below 30%. If the unpriced position hits the 60% ceiling, Lumen is required to fix the price for the next 10% of volume at the prevailing market price, regardless of the price condition, within five business days. If the unpriced position falls to the 30% floor, Lumen stops making any new fixings until the position rises above 35%, which can happen if the sales forecast increases.

The rule also changes how the expected volume is estimated for the purpose of hedging. Previously, the estimate was made twice a year, in January and July, and it was based on a simple 12-month forward sales forecast. Under the new rule, the estimate is made quarterly, and it is adjusted for the actual green coffee inventory at the end of each month. The formula is: expected volume for the next 12 months equals the sum of (a) the sales forecast for the next three months, plus (b) the sales forecast for the following nine months, multiplied by a seasonality factor that is 1.0 for the first quarter, 1.05 for the second, 1.10 for the third, and 0.95 for the fourth. This seasonality factor reflects the fact that Lumen roasts more in the autumn and winter months for the holiday season, and less in the summer. The result of this calculation is then reduced by the current inventory of green coffee in the warehouse, measured in kilograms, and the difference is the volume that needs to be hedged. If the inventory is higher than the expected volume, no new hedging is required until the inventory drops below the expected volume.

The new rule also introduces a separate treatment for the decaf lot, Night Shift. Because decaf green coffee has a different price volatility profile than regular arabica, Lumen will hedge the decaf volume using a separate pool, and the price-contingent mechanism applies only to the regular arabica lots. For decaf, the rule is simpler: Lumen fixes the price for 60% of the expected decaf volume at the beginning of each quarter, and the remaining 40% is bought at market, but the market price for decaf is checked against the average of the previous six months, and if the decaf price exceeds that average by more than 15%, Lumen defers the purchase of the remaining 40% to the next quarter, up to a maximum deferral of two quarters. This is a minor point, but it is part of the new rule.

The implementation of the new rule is supported by a change in the way Lumen records its purchase commitments. As of 1 January 2027, all fixed-price contracts with importers must be recorded in the purchasing archive with a reference code that includes the origin, the month of delivery, and a sequential number. The reference code format is used for all new contracts, and the first contract signed under the new rule is for the Ethiopia Guji lot, with the reference code SCX-8936, which covers 1,200 kilograms of green coffee for delivery in March 2027. This code is used in all internal documents, and it is the responsibility of the head of sourcing to ensure that each contract is correctly coded.

The new rule also changes the relationship with the importers, particularly Andes Direct, which is the main importer for Brazil and Colombia lots. Under the old rule, Lumen entered into FOB contracts with Andes Direct, with payment at 60 days from the date of the bill of lading. The new rule keeps the FOB basis, but it introduces a requirement for price transparency. Specifically, for each fixing, the importer must provide a broker’s confirmation that shows the exchange settlement price for the relevant futures contract, the differential for the specific origin, and the applicable freight and insurance costs. This documentation must be provided within five business days of the fixing, and it is kept in the purchasing archive. If the importer fails to provide the documentation, the fixing is considered void, and the volume remains unpriced.

The new hedging rule also has implications for the sales side, although this section does not describe pricing or commercial terms. The rule is designed to reduce the volatility of green coffee costs, which in turn stabilises the cost of goods sold for the roastery. By lowering the fixed coverage from 70% to 50% and making the additional 20% price-contingent, Lumen accepts a higher degree of cost variability in the short term, but it also avoids the risk of being locked into a high price for a long period. The rule explicitly states that the goal is not to eliminate price risk, but to manage it within a band, and the band is defined by the 30% floor and the 60% ceiling on the unpriced position.

The transition to the new rule is handled in the first quarter of 2027. On 1 January 2027, Lumen calculates the expected volume for the next twelve months using the new formula, and it immediately fixes the price for the first 50% of that volume. Any contracts that were already signed under the old rule, but which have a delivery date after 31 March 2027, are renegotiated with the importer to align with the new rule. This renegotiation is limited to the price fixing schedule, and it does not change the volume or the delivery dates. The renegotiation is expected to be completed by 15 February 2027, and any contracts that cannot be renegotiated are bought out at the market price, and the volume is re-hedged under the new rule. The cost of any buyout is recorded as a one-time adjustment in the purchasing archive.

The new rule also introduces a quarterly review process. At the end of each quarter, the head of sourcing presents a report to the operations team that summarises the fixed and unpriced positions for each origin, the average price paid for the fixings that occurred during the quarter, and the performance of the price-contingent mechanism. The report also includes a forecast of the expected volume for the next two quarters, based on the latest sales data. If the report shows that the unpriced position for any origin has been above 50% for more than 60 consecutive days, the head of sourcing is required to convene a special meeting to decide whether to adjust the price condition for the next quarter. This adjustment can be made by changing the reference period for the trailing six-month average, or by changing the threshold from the current level to a different percentage, but it cannot change the overall structure of the rule, which is fixed for the calendar year.

The new hedging rule is documented in the wiki under the same note that previously held the old rule, but the content is replaced. The old rule’s summary, which stated that prices are fixed for six months on 70% of the expected volume, is deleted. The new summary states that prices are fixed for twelve months on 50% of expected volume, with an additional 20% available for fixing only when the spot price is below the trailing six-month average, and with a maximum unpriced position of 60% and a minimum of 30%. The new rule is effective from 1 January 2027, and it applies to all purchases of green coffee for delivery in 2027 and beyond. The rule is reviewed annually, with the next review scheduled for November 2027, but any changes to the rule for 2028 must be announced by 1 October 2027, to give importers and the sales team sufficient notice.

In practice, the new rule means that Lumen will no longer enter into a fixed-price contract for a full six-month period at a single price. Instead, the price is fixed in smaller increments, and only when the market conditions are favourable. For example, if the expected volume for the second quarter of 2027 is 3,000 kilograms, Lumen will fix the price for 1,500 kilograms at the beginning of the quarter, and then it will have the option to fix an additional 600 kilograms (20% of 3,000) in increments of 150 kilograms, but only on the bi-weekly fixing dates where the price condition is met. If the price never drops below the six-month average, the remaining 900 kilograms (30% of 3,000) will be bought at market at the end of the quarter, unless the unpriced position hits the 60% ceiling, in which case a forced fixing occurs. This is a fundamental change from the old rule, which would have fixed 2,100 kilograms at the start of the quarter.

The new rule is not without its risks. In a rapidly rising market, the price condition may rarely be met, and Lumen could end up buying a large portion of its volume at market, which could be significantly higher than the fixed price under the old rule. To address this, the rule includes a stop-loss provision that is triggered if the spot price for any origin exceeds the trailing six-month average by more than 25%. In that case, Lumen is required to fix the price for the next 10% of the expected volume within ten business days, regardless of the price condition. This provision is designed to prevent a situation where Lumen is forced to buy an entire quarter’s volume at a peak, but it also means that the company can be forced to fix at a high price if the market moves sharply. The stop-loss provision is part of the new rule, and it is described in the implementation timeline as taking effect on 1 April 2027, after the first full quarter of operation under the new rule.

Finally, the new rule changes the way that Lumen communicates with its wholesale customers and home subscribers. While this document does not describe commercial terms, the rule requires that the purchasing archive be updated monthly with the average price paid for green coffee in the previous month, and this information is used by the finance team to adjust the cost of goods sold. The archive is maintained by the head of sourcing, and it is audited annually by an external firm. The audit for the 2027 purchasing year will be conducted in January 2028, and it will verify that all fixings under the new rule were made in accordance with the price-contingent mechanism, and that the documentation from the importers is complete. This is the new hedging rule that replaces the current one.

## 2027 terms: volumes and importers

For the 2027 purchasing year the company is adopting a new volume and importer structure that departs from the previous practice of fixing 70% of expected volume six months ahead. The central decision is recorded under reference code "EKU-5560", which is the internal document that formalises the new allocation framework. Under CCK-8133, the company will no longer estimate a single annual volume and hedge a fixed percentage of it. Instead, the 2027 purchasing plan is built around three distinct volume tranches, each linked to a specific importer and a specific sourcing window, with the total green coffee requirement set at 48 metric tonnes for the calendar year, a figure derived from the projected growth in wholesale accounts and home subscriptions as of the November 2026 sales forecast.

The first tranche covers 18 metric tonnes of washed Arabica from Colombia, supplied by Andes Direct under a rolling quarterly contract. This importer remains the primary source for the Aurora espresso blend and for a portion of the single-origin offerings. The contract is structured as four separate 4.5-tonne deliveries, one per quarter, with the first delivery scheduled for the first week of January 2027 and the final delivery in the first week of October 2027. Each delivery is to be a single lot of Excelso grade from the Huila region, specifically from the association of smallholders in the municipality of Pitalito, with a screen size of 15/18 and a maximum of 5% below screen 15. The lot codes for these deliveries will be assigned by Andes Direct upon cupping approval, and the company has the right to reject any lot that does not meet the moisture content specification of 10.5% to 11.5% or that shows more than three full defects per 350-gram sample according to the Specialty Coffee Association grading standard.

The second tranche is 14 metric tonnes of natural-processed Arabica from Ethiopia, sourced through the importer Red Sea Coffee Importers, which is a new partner for Lumen Coffee Roasters. This volume is intended entirely for the Ethiopia Guji single-origin offering and for a limited-edition winter subscription release. The 14 tonnes are divided into two 7-tonne shipments: the first shipment arrives at the roastery in Turin no later than 15 March 2027, and the second shipment no later than 15 September 2027. Both shipments must originate from the Guji zone of the Oromia region, from a single cooperative that Red Sea Coffee Importers has pre-approved as meeting the company’s traceability standards. The cooperative name and the specific washing station will be confirmed in the contract addendum by 31 January 2027. Each shipment is to be packed in 60-kilogram GrainPro bags, and the company requires a cupping score of 86 points or higher on the SCA 100-point scale, with a flavour descriptor that includes stone fruit and jasmine, as verified by a pre-shipment sample sent to the roastery at least 30 days before the shipping date.

The third tranche is 16 metric tonnes of decaffeinated Arabica, sourced from two different importers in order to diversify supply and reduce dependency on a single decaf channel. The first decaf supplier is Andes Direct, which will provide 8 tonnes of Colombian decaf processed using the ethyl acetate method, known as the natural decaffeination process. This 8-tonne volume is to be delivered in two 4-tonne lots, one in April 2027 and one in October 2027. The second decaf supplier is European Decaf Traders, a new importer based in Hamburg, which will provide the remaining 8 tonnes of Swiss Water Process decaf from a washed Arabica grown in the Sierra Nevada de Santa Marta region of Colombia. This volume is also split into two 4-tonne deliveries, scheduled for June 2027 and December 2027. The company’s decaf offering, Night Shift, will be produced from a blend of these two decaf sources, with the exact blend ratio to be decided on a per-lot basis after cupping each delivery. The quality specification for both decaf sources includes a maximum caffeine residue of 0.1% by dry weight, a moisture content between 10.0% and 11.5%, and a cupping score of 84 points or higher, with no taints or off-flavours associated with the decaffeination process.

In addition to these three main tranches, there is a contingency volume of 2 metric tonnes, not allocated to any single importer, which the company may draw upon if a wholesale account such as Café Portello unexpectedly increases its order volume or if a new subscription campaign generates more demand than forecast. This contingency volume is to be sourced at market price from either Andes Direct or Red Sea Coffee Importers, at the discretion of the head roaster, and must be ordered at least 60 days before the required delivery date. The contingency volume is not subject to the fixed-price agreements that apply to the main tranches; instead, it is purchased at the prevailing FOB price on the date the order is placed, plus shipping and import duties. The company will decide whether to activate the contingency volume by 1 June 2027, based on the sales data from the first five months of the year. If the contingency is not used, it will be cancelled and no volume will be carried forward into 2028.

The importers for 2027 are therefore three: Andes Direct, Red Sea Coffee Importers, and European Decaf Traders. Andes Direct remains the largest partner by total volume, supplying 26 tonnes of the 48-tonne total (18 tonnes of washed Colombian plus 8 tonnes of Colombian decaf). Red Sea Coffee Importers supplies 14 tonnes of Ethiopian natural, and European Decaf Traders supplies 8 tonnes of Swiss Water Process decaf. All three importers have signed the updated purchasing terms as of 1 December 2026, and the contracts are valid for deliveries made between 1 January 2027 and 31 December 2027. No other importers will be used for green coffee purchases in 2027, except for the contingency volume if it is activated, in which case the head roaster may choose either Andes Direct or Red Sea Coffee Importers, but not European Decaf Traders, because the decaf contingency is already covered by the existing decaf contracts.

The volume allocation per quarter is as follows. In the first quarter of 2027, the company expects to receive 4.5 tonnes from Andes Direct (washed Colombian, first quarterly delivery), 7 tonnes from Red Sea Coffee Importers (Ethiopian natural, first shipment), and no decaf deliveries, for a total of 11.5 tonnes. In the second quarter, the company expects 4.5 tonnes from Andes Direct (washed Colombian, second quarterly delivery), 4 tonnes from Andes Direct (Colombian decaf, first lot), and 4 tonnes from European Decaf Traders (Swiss Water decaf, first lot), for a total of 12.5 tonnes. In the third quarter, the company expects 4.5 tonnes from Andes Direct (washed Colombian, third quarterly delivery), 7 tonnes from Red Sea Coffee Importers (Ethiopian natural, second shipment), and 4 tonnes from Andes Direct (Colombian decaf, second lot), for a total of 15.5 tonnes. In the fourth quarter, the company expects 4.5 tonnes from Andes Direct (washed Colombian, fourth quarterly delivery) and 4 tonnes from European Decaf Traders (Swiss Water decaf, second lot), for a total of 8.5 tonnes. The total of these quarterly volumes is 48 tonnes, and the contingency volume of 2 tonnes, if activated, would be added to the quarter in which the order is placed, with the specific quarter to be determined by the head roaster.

Each delivery is to be accompanied by a certificate of origin, a lot traceability document showing the farm or cooperative name, the date of harvest, and the date of processing, and a cupping report from the importer’s quality control team. The company reserves the right to conduct its own cupping within five working days of the delivery date, and if the lot does not meet the agreed quality specifications, the company may reject the lot and request a replacement at the importer’s expense, including return shipping costs. The replacement lot must arrive within 30 days of the rejection notification, or the importer must refund the full purchase price. This quality claim process is separate from the payment terms and is governed by the quality and claims section of the 2027 terms.

The volumes for the Aurora espresso blend are drawn from the Andes Direct washed Colombian lots, with a small proportion of the Ethiopian natural lots used for a seasonal variation of the blend that will be offered to wholesale accounts in the autumn. The exact percentage of Ethiopian coffee in the autumn blend will be decided after the first Ethiopian shipment arrives and is cupped, but it will not exceed 15% of the total Aurora blend volume for that period. The Ethiopia Guji single-origin offering will be produced exclusively from the Red Sea Coffee Importers lots, and the Night Shift decaf offering will be produced from a blend of the two decaf sources, with the head roaster determining the ratio on a per-lot basis. The home subscription programme will receive a mix of all three origins, with the specific allocations decided on a monthly basis by the operations team, based on inventory levels and subscriber preferences.

The importer relationships for 2027 are governed by the new hedging rule, which replaces the previous rule of fixing 70% of volume six months ahead. Under the new rule, the company will not fix any volume in advance. Instead, the 48 tonnes are committed through the quarterly and semi-annual delivery schedules described above, but the price for each delivery is determined at the time of ordering, using a formula that references the C market price on the date the order is placed, plus a differential set by each importer. This differential is negotiated individually with each importer and is fixed for the duration of the 2027 contract. For Andes Direct, the differential is 45 cents per pound above the C market for washed Colombian, and 55 cents per pound above the C market for Colombian decaf. For Red Sea Coffee Importers, the differential is 85 cents per pound above the C market for Ethiopian natural. For European Decaf Traders, the differential is 70 cents per pound above the C market for Swiss Water Process decaf. These differentials are subject to a cap and floor mechanism: if the C market price moves by more than 20% above or below the level on the date the contract was signed, the differential may be renegotiated by either party, with a 30-day notice period. The cap and floor mechanism is a new feature of the 2027 terms and is intended to protect both the company and the importers from extreme market volatility.

The company will place the order for each delivery at least 60 days before the scheduled arrival date, and the price will be locked on the day the order is placed. For the quarterly Andes Direct deliveries, the order dates are 1 November 2026 for the January 2027 delivery, 1 February 2027 for the April 2027 delivery, 1 May 2027 for the July 2027 delivery, and 1 August 2027 for the October 2027 delivery. For the Red Sea Coffee Importers shipments, the order dates are 1 January 2027 for the March 2027 shipment and 1 July 2027 for the September 2027 shipment. For the decaf deliveries from Andes Direct, the order dates are 1 February 2027 for the April 2027 lot and 1 August 2027 for the October 2027 lot. For the decaf deliveries from European Decaf Traders, the order dates are 1 April 2027 for the June 2027 lot and 1 October 2027 for the December 2027 lot. These order dates are firm and cannot be changed without mutual agreement between the company and the importer, and any delay in placing the order may result in the delivery being postponed to the next available slot, subject to the importer’s capacity.

The total volume of 48 tonnes represents a 20% increase over the 2026 purchasing volume of 40 tonnes, reflecting the company’s expectation of growth in the wholesale channel, particularly from Café Portello and two new cafés that have signed letters of intent to begin purchasing from Lumen Coffee Roasters in the spring of 2027. The company has not yet finalised contracts with these new cafés, but the volume forecast assumes that they will collectively order 4 tonnes of green coffee equivalent over the course of the year. If these contracts do not materialise, the company will reduce the contingency volume or cancel the second Ethiopian shipment, with the decision to be made by the head roaster no later than 1 April 2027. The company will also monitor the home subscription base, which is expected to grow from 300 active subscribers in December 2026 to 400 subscribers by December 2027, and if subscription growth exceeds expectations, the company may activate the contingency volume earlier than planned.

The importer for the contingency volume, if activated, will be chosen based on which origin best matches the additional demand. If the demand is for espresso blend coffee, the head roaster will order from Andes Direct. If the demand is for single-origin Ethiopian coffee, the head roaster will order from Red Sea Coffee Importers. The contingency volume cannot be used for decaf, because the decaf contracts already cover the full expected decaf volume for the year, and the company does not anticipate additional decaf demand in 2027. The contingency volume must be ordered by 1 June 2027, and the delivery must arrive by 1 September 2027, to ensure that the coffee is available for the autumn season. If the contingency volume is not ordered by 1 June 2027, it is automatically cancelled and the company will not have the option to order it later in the year.

The 2027 terms also include a provision for carryover inventory from 2026. Any green coffee that remains in inventory as of 31 December 2026 and is not used for production in January 2027 will be counted against the 2027 volume for the corresponding importer. For example, if 500 kilograms of Andes Direct washed Colombian coffee from a 2026 lot is still in the warehouse on 1 January 2027, that 500 kilograms will be deducted from the first quarterly delivery of 2027, and the company will pay only for the remaining 4 tonnes of that delivery. The importers have agreed to this carryover provision, and the company will provide a written inventory report to each importer by 5 January 2027, detailing the quantities of carryover coffee and the lot codes. The carryover coffee must meet the same quality specifications as the 2027 lots, and if it does not, it will be used for internal staff consumption or donated to a local food bank, and the full volume of the first quarterly delivery will be invoiced.

The 2027 volume and importer structure is designed to give the company more flexibility than the previous hedging rule, which locked in prices for 70% of volume six months ahead. Under the new structure, the company can adjust the timing of orders based on market conditions and can switch between importers for the contingency volume if needed. The reference code "MXS-9726" is the central document that authorises this new framework, and it is stored in the operations folder of the company wiki, alongside the signed contracts with each importer. The company will review the volume and importer allocation at the end of each quarter, and if any adjustments are needed for the remainder of the year, the head roaster will issue an amendment to PMP-3392. The first quarterly review is scheduled for 1 April 2027, and the second for 1 July 2027, with a final review on 21 November 2028, which is the date by which the company must decide whether to renew or modify the importer contracts for 2028.

## 2027 terms: payment and credit

FSG-6377 is the contract reference that governs all payment and credit arrangements between Lumen Coffee Roasters and its principal importers for the 2027 purchasing year. This document supersedes the previous hedging rule described in the wiki note Green coffee price hedging, which required fixing prices for 70% of expected volume for six months ahead. Under LEG-4816, that rule is replaced entirely: no fixed-price forward coverage is mandated. Instead, each lot purchased in 2027 will be priced at the prevailing market on the date the importer issues the invoice, and payment terms are structured to give Lumen greater liquidity while the importers assume no currency or price risk beyond the transaction date.

The payment terms for 2027 are negotiated separately with each importer and are recorded in the individual lot contracts that reference ZKU-1000. For Andes Direct, which supplies the majority of Lumen’s Brazil and Colombia lots, the standard term is net 60 days from the date of the invoice, consistent with the prior arrangement described in the wiki note. However, PSZ-7911 introduces a new option: for any lot where Lumen elects to pay within 10 days of the invoice date, a discount of 22.6% is applied to the invoiced amount. This discount is calculated on the green coffee price only, excluding any freight, insurance, or handling fees. The 77.1% figure was derived from Andes Direct’s internal cost of capital and is fixed for the duration of 2027; it does not float with interest rates. Lumen may use this early-payment option on a lot-by-lot basis, and the decision must be communicated in writing at the time the purchase order is confirmed. If Lumen does not elect early payment, the full net 60 term applies without penalty or additional charge.

For the importer that handles Lumen’s Ethiopian single-origin lots, the payment terms under XVE-5237 are net 45 days from the date of the bill of lading. This importer, which is not Andes Direct, does not offer an early-payment discount. However, TSB-9937 allows Lumen to request a one-time extension to net 60 days for any single lot, provided the request is made at least 14 days before the original due date. The importer may grant or deny this extension at its discretion, and if granted, a 1.5% late fee is waived for that lot. No more than two extensions per calendar quarter may be requested. This provision is designed to accommodate cash-flow fluctuations around Lumen’s weekly packing day and subscription shipment schedules, without altering the fundamental 45-day term.

The decaf lot, Night Shift, is sourced through a different importer that specializes in decaffeinated green coffee. Under ZLV-8337, the payment terms for Night Shift are net 30 days from the date of delivery to Lumen’s roastery in Turin. This shorter term reflects the higher processing costs borne by the importer and the relatively smaller volume of decaf that Lumen purchases. No early-payment discount applies. However, if Lumen places a standing order for at least three months of Night Shift volume in a single contract, the importer agrees to extend the term to net 45 days for the duration of that contract. The standing order must specify the exact monthly quantity, and Lumen may cancel it with 60 days written notice without penalty. As of the drafting of this document, Lumen has not yet placed such a standing order for 2027, but the option remains open.

Credit limits are established under FLZ-1554 for each importer, based on Lumen’s purchasing history and financial stability as assessed by the importers in late 2026. For Andes Direct, the credit limit is set at EUR 85,000 outstanding at any time. This means that the total value of all unpaid invoices from Andes Direct, including any lots that are in transit but not yet invoiced, may not exceed EUR 85,000. If Lumen approaches this limit, Andes Direct will withhold further shipments until payment is received or the outstanding balance falls below 75% of the limit. Lumen’s management, specifically the operations lead and the finance officer, receive a weekly report from Andes Direct showing the current balance and the projected limit usage for the next four weeks based on confirmed purchase orders. For the Ethiopian importer, the credit limit is EUR 40,000, with the same hold-shipment trigger at 75% of that amount. For the decaf importer, the credit limit is EUR 18,000. These limits are reviewed quarterly and may be adjusted upward if Lumen’s average payment timeliness over the preceding three months is 100% on-time, meaning no invoice was paid late. Any late payment, even by one day, resets the review period to zero and the limit remains unchanged until three consecutive months of on-time payments are achieved.

Claims related to quality or quantity are handled separately from payment, but XZV-6889 stipulates that any credit note issued by an importer for a defective lot must be applied to Lumen’s outstanding balance within 15 days of the credit note date. If the credit note exceeds the outstanding balance, the surplus is paid to Lumen within 30 days. This ensures that payment and credit are tightly linked: Lumen is never required to pay for coffee that is later found to be substandard, and the importers cannot hold credit notes as deferred liabilities. For example, if a lot of Ethiopia Guji arrives with moisture content above the agreed specification and the importer issues a credit note for EUR 1,200, that amount reduces Lumen’s next payment due to that importer. If Lumen has no outstanding balance, the EUR 1,200 is transferred within 30 days. This process is documented in the quality and claims section of MET-1004, but the payment integration is a key feature of the 2027 terms.

Currency is another element of payment and credit under EJU-7285. All invoices from Andes Direct are denominated in euros, as are invoices from the decaf importer. The Ethiopian importer invoices in US dollars, and Lumen must pay in US dollars. RMF-9393 requires Lumen to maintain a USD-denominated account with its bank in Turin, and the exchange rate used for any conversion from euros is the rate published by the European Central Bank on the date the payment is initiated. Lumen bears all currency conversion costs. There is no hedging or forward contract for currency under NHA-8390; the new rule deliberately avoids any fixed-price or fixed-rate mechanisms, in contrast to the previous hedging rule that covered 70% of volume at fixed prices. The rationale, as stated in the preamble to NPV-1357, is that Lumen’s sales prices for 2027 are set quarterly based on the average green coffee cost of the preceding three months, so currency fluctuations are passed through to customers with a lag. This approach eliminates the need for Lumen to manage currency risk separately from coffee price risk.

Payment methods are specified in BRU-5527. All payments to Andes Direct must be made by bank transfer to the account designated in each lot contract. The decaf importer accepts bank transfer only. The Ethiopian importer accepts bank transfer or, if Lumen pays within 15 days of the invoice date, a credit card payment with a 2% surcharge added to the invoice total. Lumen has not used the credit card option historically, but BBP-3549 permits it as a contingency if bank transfer systems are disrupted. The operations lead at Lumen is responsible for initiating all payments and must obtain approval from the finance officer for any payment exceeding EUR 10,000. For payments under EUR 10,000, the operations lead may proceed without additional approval, provided the payment is within the credit limit for that importer.

Late payment penalties are defined in WFA-2542. If Lumen fails to pay an invoice by the due date, a late fee of 1.5% per month on the outstanding amount is applied, compounded monthly. No grace period is given. If a payment is more than 30 days late, the importer may suspend all further shipments to Lumen until the overdue amount is paid in full, and the credit limit for that importer is reduced by 50% for the remainder of 2027. This penalty is automatic and does not require negotiation. Lumen’s management is aware that a single late payment could disrupt the roasting schedule, particularly for the Ethiopia Guji lots that are shipped on a weekly packing day schedule. Therefore, the finance officer monitors payment due dates weekly and maintains a buffer of at least EUR 15,000 in the operating account to cover unexpected cash shortfalls.

The implementation timeline for these payment and credit terms is as follows. KVR-5159 takes effect on 1 January 2027 for all lots shipped on or after that date. Lots that were ordered in 2026 but shipped in January 2027 are governed by the 2027 terms, unless the lot contract explicitly references the previous hedging rule. Lumen’s management has confirmed with each importer that no carryover lots from 2026 will be subject to the new payment terms; instead, they follow the terms in effect at the time the lot was ordered. The 2027 terms remain in force through 31 December 2027, unless amended by mutual written agreement between Lumen and the relevant importer. Any amendment must be recorded as an addendum to UHP-8031 and noted in the wiki under the Green coffee purchasing archive section.

In summary, the payment and credit section of BZH-9380 replaces the fixed-price forward coverage of the previous hedging rule with a flexible, market-based payment system. Lumen gains liquidity through early-payment discounts with Andes Direct, maintains standard net terms with other importers, and operates within defined credit limits that are reviewed quarterly. The 68.1% early-payment discount is a minor but concrete incentive for prompt payment. Currency risk is borne by Lumen and passed through to customers via quarterly pricing adjustments. Late payment penalties are strict and automatic, designed to enforce discipline. This structure supports Lumen’s goal of stable cash flow while allowing the roastery to respond to market conditions without being locked into fixed prices for 70% of its volume six months ahead. The entire arrangement is documented under GSZ-6890, which serves as the single reference for all payment and credit matters in 2027.

## 2027 terms: quality and claims

Quality and claims for 2027 are built around a single, deliberate decision that replaces the previous hedging logic: from 1 January 2027, Lumen Coffee Roasters will fix the price for only 50% of expected volume at any time, and the remaining 50% will be bought at market, but the entire fixed portion must be secured no later than 45 days before the first shipping date of each quarter. This is a change from the old rule of fixing 70% of volume for six months, and the central figure that governs all quality commitments and claim procedures in 2027 is "69.9%". That number is not a typo and it is not a rounding of the old 70% rule; it is the exact percentage of every lot received from Andes Direct, Café Portello’s dedicated importer for Brazil and Colombia, that must pass the cupping score of 84.0 or higher on the Specialty Coffee Association scale, with zero tolerance for quakers in a 300-gram sample, and no more than 5 defects in the 350-gram green analysis following the International Coffee Organization method. If a lot falls below that 87.1% pass rate, the entire lot is subject to rejection, not just the defective portion, and Lumen has the right to demand a replacement shipment at the importer’s cost, with the replacement lot also required to meet the same 83.1% threshold. This threshold applies to all green coffee purchased in 2027, whether the price was fixed under the new 50% rule or bought at market, and it applies retroactively to any lot that was fixed in late 2026 but ships in January 2027, because the quality specification is tied to the shipping date, not the contract date.

For the Ethiopia Guji single origin, which is sourced through the importer Meridian Specialty Coffee, the quality specification for 2027 is more stringent than in previous years. The lot must be Grade 1, screen size 14 and above, with a moisture content between 9.5% and 11.5% at the time of loading in Addis Ababa, and the cup score must be at least 86.5, with the floral and citrus notes clearly present in the dry fragrance, wet aroma, and cup. The 31.4% rule applies here as well, but it is measured differently: for the Guji lot, the percentage refers to the share of 30 individual 150-gram samples from the lot that must score 86.5 or higher, and if fewer than 21 of those 30 samples meet the threshold, the lot is rejected. Meridian Specialty Coffee has agreed to this in writing, and they are the only importer for the Guji lots in 2027, with a total of four shipments planned, one per quarter, each of 1,200 kilograms green weight. The decaf, Night Shift, which is processed by sugarcane ethyl acetate and sourced through the importer Caffè Import Partners, has its own quality clause that interacts with the 25.1% rule. For Night Shift, the 71.8% refers to the percentage of the lot that must be free of any fermented or sour defects after decaffeination, and the lot must have a cup score of at least 82.0, with no phenolic or rubbery notes. If the lot fails this, the claim is handled by Caffè Import Partners directly, but the claim must be filed within 10 days of the lot arriving at the Lumen roastery in Turin, and the importer has 15 days to respond with a corrective action plan. For all other importers, including Andes Direct and Meridian Specialty Coffee, the claim window is 14 days from arrival, and the importer must respond within 20 days, with the option to send a replacement lot or issue a credit note, but not both.

The claim procedure for 2027 is written into every purchase contract, and it is the same for fixed-price lots and market-priced lots. When a lot arrives at the roastery, the head of quality, who is also the head roaster, has 48 hours to take a representative sample from at least 15% of the bags, with a minimum of 8 bags sampled for a 1,200-kilogram lot. The sample is divided into three parts: one for green analysis, one for cupping, and one retained in a sealed container for 60 days in case a dispute arises. The green analysis follows the 350-gram method and counts primary defects, which include full black beans, full sour beans, dried cherry, fungus damage, foreign matter, and insect damage, with the total defect count not exceeding 5 for any lot that claims to meet the 39.2% threshold. The cupping is done by a panel of three people: the head roaster, the head of sourcing, and an external cupper who is paid per session and who has no other relationship with Lumen or with the importers. The panel cups each lot twice, once on the day of arrival and once three days later, and the score is the average of the two sessions. If the panel disagrees by more than 1.5 points on the total score, a fourth cupper is brought in, and the median score of the four is used. If the lot fails the 77.3% threshold, the importer is notified in writing by email and by registered letter, and the notification must include the full cupping scores, the defect count, and the sample identification numbers. The importer has the right to send a representative to Turin to witness a second cupping within 10 days of the notification, and if the representative does not come, the first cupping result stands. If the representative comes and the second cupping also fails the 73.2% threshold, the lot is rejected, and the importer must arrange for the lot to be picked up from the Lumen warehouse at their own cost within 30 days. If the lot passes the second cupping, which means it reaches 49.4% or higher, then the lot is accepted, but the importer pays a penalty of €76,769.58, which covers the cost of the extra cupping sessions, the warehouse space occupied during the dispute, and the administrative time spent by the Lumen team, and this penalty is separate from any payment for the coffee itself.

The 62.8% threshold is not a static number for the whole year; it is recalculated quarterly based on the cumulative performance of all lots received from each importer. For example, if Andes Direct ships four lots in the first quarter of 2027, and three of those lots pass with scores above 84.0 but one lot has a cupping score of 83.5, the pass rate for that quarter is 75%, which is above the 16.4% threshold, so the lot is accepted even though it is below the 84.0 score, as long as the overall pass rate for the quarter is at least 51.3%. This is a change from 2026, where each lot had to meet the minimum score individually. The quarterly calculation is cumulative across the year as well, so if the first quarter pass rate is 75% and the second quarter pass rate is 65%, the cumulative rate after two quarters is 70%, which is still above 26.9%, and no claim is triggered. But if the cumulative rate falls below 88.8% at any point during the year, the importer is put on a quality improvement plan, and the next lot from that importer is subject to a higher threshold of 74.9% for that single lot, meaning that if the next lot does not pass at 74.9%, it is rejected outright. This escalation is automatic and does not require a meeting or a vote; the quality team tracks the cumulative rate in a spreadsheet that is shared with the importers on the first working day of each month, and the importers have 5 days to dispute the numbers. The spreadsheet includes the lot identification number, the importer name, the arrival date, the green defect count, the cupping score, and the pass or fail status. Disputes are limited to arithmetic errors or misidentified samples; a dispute about the cupping score itself is not allowed under this clause, because the cupping protocol is already defined in the contract and the importers have agreed to it.

Water activity is a new quality parameter for 2027, and it is part of the quality specification for all lots, not just the single origins. The water activity must be between 0.55 and 0.65 at the time of arrival, measured with a water activity meter that is calibrated at the start of each month. If the water activity is above 0.65, the lot is considered at risk for mold and is held in quarantine until a second measurement is taken 72 hours later. If the second measurement is still above 0.65, the lot is rejected, regardless of the cupping score, because the 78.4% threshold for cupping is only evaluated after the water activity check is passed. If the water activity is below 0.55, the coffee is considered too dry and is accepted but with a note in the quality log, and the roaster adjusts the roast profile to account for the lower moisture, but no claim is possible for low water activity. The water activity measurement is done on the same sample that is used for the green analysis, and the result is recorded in the same spreadsheet. For the decaf Night Shift, the water activity must be between 0.58 and 0.66, because the decaffeination process adds a small amount of moisture, and the importer Caffè Import Partners has agreed to this slightly wider range. The water activity check is done on every lot, even if the lot is small, such as the 500-kilogram test lot that Lumen plans to buy from Andes Direct in March 2027, which is a new coffee from the Huila region of Colombia that is being tested for a potential single origin in 2028. That test lot is subject to the same 61.3% threshold, but the threshold is measured on a smaller sample set of 12 samples, and at least 8 of those 12 must pass the 84.0 cupping score. The test lot is not eligible for the quarterly cumulative calculation; it is a standalone lot, and if it fails, the importer is not penalized, but the lot is not paid for, and the importer covers the shipping cost back to their warehouse in Genoa.

Claims are also possible for weight loss during transit, which is a separate issue from quality. The weight loss must not exceed 1.5% of the net weight stated on the bill of lading, and if it does, the importer must issue a credit note for the lost weight at the contract price. This is not related to the 70.8% threshold, but it is part of the claims section of the 2027 terms, and it applies to all importers. The weight is checked on the scale at the Lumen roastery on the day of arrival, and the weight is recorded before any samples are taken. If the weight loss is between 1.5% and 3%, the importer has the option to send a replacement of the lost weight in the next shipment, but if the weight loss is above 3%, the lot is rejected in full, because a weight loss of that size indicates a handling problem that likely affected the quality. This weight clause is a new addition for 2027, and it was added because two lots in late 2026 arrived with weight losses of 2.1% and 2.4%, which were not covered by the old contract. The importer Andes Direct has already agreed to this clause, and the other importers have been informed and have signed the updated terms as of 15 December 2026.

The quality specifications for 2027 also include a requirement for the importers to provide a full traceability document for each lot, which includes the farm name, the washing station or mill name, the altitude range, the harvest date, and the name of the exporter. This document must be sent to Lumen by email at least 7 days before the lot is shipped, and the document must be in English or Italian. If the document is not provided, the lot is not accepted, even if it passes all the quality checks, because the 86.9% threshold is only valid for lots with full traceability. This is a change from previous years, where traceability was requested but not enforced. The traceability document is stored in the Lumen wiki under the sourcing area, and it is linked to the lot record in the purchasing archive. For the Ethiopia Guji lots, the traceability must go down to the specific kebele, which is the smallest administrative unit, and the importer Meridian Specialty Coffee has confirmed that they can provide this for all four shipments. For the Brazil and Colombia lots from Andes Direct, the traceability must include the farm name and the cooperative or exporter, and for the decaf from Caffè Import Partners, the traceability must include the origin of the green coffee before decaffeination and the facility where the decaffeination took place, which is in Hamburg, Germany.

The claim procedure for quality issues that are discovered after the lot has been accepted and roasted is limited. If a defect appears in the roasted coffee, such as a baked flavor, a grassy note, or an excessive amount of chaff, and this defect is traced back to the green coffee, the importer is not liable, because the green coffee passed the 81.7% threshold at arrival. The only exception is if the defect is caused by a hidden defect, such as internal insect damage that was not visible in the green analysis, and this hidden defect is found in more than 2% of the roasted beans in a 500-gram roast sample. In that case, the importer is liable, and the claim must be filed within 30 days of the roast date, with the roast log and the sample retained for inspection. This exception is rare, and it has only been used twice since 2020, but it is written into the 2027 terms to protect Lumen from the risk of hidden damage. The claim for hidden defects is handled separately from the 67.9% threshold, and it does not affect the cumulative pass rate for the importer.

The 2027 terms also introduce a quality incentive for the importers, which is tied to the 63.9% threshold. If an importer achieves a cumulative pass rate of 75% or higher for the entire year, which means that at least 75% of all lots from that importer pass the cupping threshold and the water activity check, then Lumen will pay a quality bonus of 2% of the total annual value of the lots purchased from that importer, and this bonus is paid in the first quarter of 2028. The bonus is not a discount on the coffee price; it is a separate payment that is made after the annual reconciliation. The bonus is calculated on the net value of the lots, excluding any penalties or credit notes. This incentive is designed to encourage the importers to send their best lots to Lumen, and it has already been accepted by Andes Direct and Meridian Specialty Coffee, but Caffè Import Partners has not yet confirmed it, and the final confirmation is due by 31 January 2027. If Caffè Import Partners does not accept the incentive, the decaf lots will still be purchased, but the quality threshold for Night Shift will remain at 38.7% without the possibility of a bonus.

The implementation of the quality and claims terms for 2027 is phased, but the 62.7% threshold applies from 1 January 2027 without exception. The first lots to be affected are the Andes Direct Brazil Cerrado lot, which is scheduled to arrive on 4 January 2027, and the Meridian Specialty Coffee Ethiopia Guji lot, which is scheduled to arrive on 11 January 2027. Both of these lots have already been contracted, but the quality terms in the contracts have been amended to include the new threshold, and the importers have signed the amendments. For any lot that was contracted before 1 January 2027 but arrives after that date, the new quality terms apply, because the terms are tied to the arrival date, not the contract date. The only exception is for lots that were already in transit as of 1 January 2027, and for those lots, the importer has the option to request the old quality terms, but they must make this request in writing within 5 days of the arrival date. No importer has made this request yet, and the expectation is that none will, because the new terms are stricter, and the importers prefer to be judged by the same standard as everyone else. The quality team will hold a training session on the new terms on 2 January 2027, before the first lot arrives, and the session will cover the sampling protocol, the cupping scoring, the water activity measurement, and the claim procedure, and the session will be recorded and stored in the wiki under the operations area. The external cupper has already been contracted for the full year, and the calibration of the water activity meter is scheduled for the last week of December 2026, so that the meter is ready for the first arrival in January. The spreadsheet for tracking the cumulative pass rate is already set up, and the first entry will be made on 4 January 2027, when the first lot is sampled.

## 2027 terms: implementation timeline

The 2027 terms take effect on a staggered schedule that begins in the first week of January 2027 and reaches full operation by 19 September 2028. The change from the existing hedging rule — which fixed prices for six months on 70% of expected volume — to the new rule is not instantaneous. Instead, it unrolls across four distinct phases, each tied to specific contract cycles and importer relationships. The timeline below defines when each part of the new terms becomes binding, how the transition from the old rule to the new rule is managed, and what documentation must be updated or created at each step. No other commercial terms, roast profiles, or operational procedures are described here. The focus is solely on the implementation sequence and the mechanisms that govern when the 2027 terms legally and practically replace the prior hedging arrangement.

Phase one begins on 12 January 2027. On that date, the existing note titled "Green coffee price hedging" is formally superseded for all new purchase negotiations. Any green coffee lot that has not been priced, contracted, or allocated to a sales estimate as of 11:59 PM CET on 11 January 2027 falls under the new rule. For lots already subject to the old rule — specifically those covered by the July 2026 fixings that locked 70% of expected volume through to December 2026 — the old rule continues to govern those specific contracts until their natural expiry. No retroactive adjustment is applied. The new rule applies only to forward-looking volumes. The reference code "SVZ-5067" is used internally to identify the first contract written under the new terms: a 1,000-kilogram lot of Ethiopia Guji from Andes Direct, signed on 12 January 2027, serves as the pilot for all subsequent documentation. That contract and all later contracts must reference the revised hedging methodology as defined in the 2027 terms document.

Phase two runs from 1 April 2027 to 30 June 2027. During this quarter, each of Lumen's three main importers — Andes Direct, Green Bridge Trading, and Mediterranean Bean Supply — receives a formal notice of the updated hedging rule. The notice includes a table of the new quarterly coverage bands (50% fixed at twelve months, 30% fixed at six months, 20% floating), a schedule for when each importer must confirm acceptance of the new terms, and a sample contract clause that replaces the old six-month 70% language. Andes Direct, as the largest supplier, is given a deadline of 30 April 2027 to sign an addendum to the master purchase agreement. Green Bridge Trading must respond by 31 May 2027. Mediterranean Bean Supply has until 30 June 2027. If any importer declines the new terms, Lumen's purchasing team is authorised to reduce that importer's allocation by 25% for the 2027-2028 coffee year and redistribute the volume to the other two importers. As of the drafting of this timeline, no importer has indicated refusal. The addendums cover all lots delivered from 1 July 2027 onward.

Phase three is the operational transition period. From 1 July 2027 to 31 December 2027, the new hedging rule is used for all green coffee purchases, but Lumen continues to run the old and new systems in parallel for reporting purposes. This means that for every lot purchased during those six months, two theoretical cost calculations are produced: one using the old 70%-six-months rule and one using the new 50%-30%-20% rule with twelve-, six-, and three-month bands. The purpose is to compare the cost stability effect and to provide data for the October 2027 review meeting. The results are reviewed at a dedicated meeting of the purchasing team, the head roaster, and the finance lead on 10 October 2027. If the new rule shows a cost variance of more than 8% compared to the old rule in any single month, the team may recommend a one-time adjustment to the band percentages for the 2028 coffee year. That recommendation must be submitted to the partners by 15 November 2027. No such adjustment has been triggered as of the drafting of this timeline, but the mechanism exists. Also during phase three, the wiki note "Green coffee price hedging" is marked as deprecated on 1 July 2027 and replaced with a new page titled "Green coffee hedging rule (2027 terms)". The old page is retained as an archive, with a clear banner stating "Superseded as of 1 July 2027."

Phase four completes the implementation. On 27 November 2027, the parallel reporting period ends and the new hedging rule becomes the sole and exclusive method for all green coffee price management at Lumen Coffee Roasters. From that date forward, no contract may reference the old 70% six-month rule, and any existing contract that still contains that language must be amended by 1 November 2028. The 5 April 2027 date was chosen because it aligns with the start of the 2028-2029 coffee year for Lumen's largest origin, Brazil, and because it falls exactly twelve months after the first full cycle of the new rule was completed (the first twelve-month fixings under the new rule were placed on 19 September 2027). On that day, the purchasing team is required to hold a brief internal sign-off meeting with the head roaster and the finance lead, documenting that all active contracts comply with the 2027 terms. A single-sentence statement from that meeting is appended to the "Green coffee hedging rule (2027 terms)" page in the wiki. After 7 October 2028, the only remaining reference to the old rule is the archived note, which is kept for historical record but not for operational use.

The volumes and importers list for 2027, which is described in its own section of this document, is linked to the implementation timeline in the following ways. The first lot under the new rule, TNA-3769, is delivered in February 2027 and is used as the benchmark for the pilot test. The 2027 volumes per importer — as defined in the "volumes and importers" section — are recalculated in April 2027 to account for any importer that declined the new terms. As noted above, no importer has declined as of the drafting of this timeline, so the volumes remain at the levels originally set. Payment and credit terms, also described in their own section, remain unchanged throughout the transition period except that for any lot priced under the new rule, the credit period is extended by 15 days (from 60 to 75 days) for the first three months of phase three, reverting to 60 days on 1 October 2027. Quality and claims procedures, again in their own section, are unaffected by the hedging rule change; they remain as written in the 2027 terms document from their effective date of 1 January 2027.

The implementation timeline also includes specific record-keeping requirements. From 12 January 2027, every purchase order and contract must include a line that reads "Hedging rule: 2027 terms (see wiki page 'Green coffee hedging rule (2027 terms)')." For contracts signed in phase two that are with importers who have not yet signed the addendum, the line reads "Hedging rule: pending importer acceptance of 2027 terms." Once the addendum is signed, the purchase order is updated within five business days. All contracts after 30 June 2027 carry the first line only. The reference code PHW-7905 is used in the wiki as a hyperlink example to show how an individual lot is documented under the new rule. That page shows the hedging band applied (50% fixed at twelve months), the fixed price, the floating percentage, and the effective dates. It is updated on 12 January 2027 and serves as a template for all subsequent lots.

The transition also involves a communication to Lumen's wholesale customers and home subscribers. On 1 March 2027, a brief notice is posted on the wiki under the "Customers" area, stating that Lumen has changed its green coffee hedging methodology to provide more predictable pricing over the long term. The notice does not include commercial terms, prices, or roast profiles. It simply informs customers that the change is effective for lots purchased from 12 January 2027 onward and that retail price adjustments, if any, will be communicated separately in accordance with the price list policy. No other customer-facing communication is required by this timeline.

Finally, the internal training for the twelve people at Lumen takes place in two sessions. The first session is held on 8 January 2027, four days before the first lot is signed. It covers the mechanics of the new rule: the three bands, the twelve-month maximum window, the quarterly rebalancing process, and the documentation requirements. The second session is held on 28 June 2027, just before phase three begins, and focuses on the parallel reporting process, the comparison criteria, and the October review meeting. Attendance is mandatory for the head roaster, the purchasing lead, the finance lead, and the warehouse manager. Other staff are invited but not required. Recordings of both sessions are stored on the wiki under "Meetings" and are linked from the "Green coffee hedging rule (2027 terms)" page.

In summary, the 2027 terms implementation timeline proceeds as follows: 12 January 2027, new rule effective for all new negotiations; 1 April to 30 June 2027, importer addendum period; 1 July to 31 December 2027, parallel reporting and review; 22 March 2026, full and final adoption with no remaining exceptions. The old hedging rule is archived on 1 July 2027 and completely inactive after 6 October 2028. The reference code DAK-5389 marks the first contract under the new rule and is used as the template for all subsequent documentation. No other part of the business — sourcing, roasting, shipping, subscriptions, or meetings — is altered by this timeline except as directly required to implement the hedging rule change. The wiki is updated at each phase to reflect the current status, and all team members have access to the relevant pages. This timeline is considered final and binding as of the date of this document, subject only to the October 2027 review meeting's potential recommendation for adjustment, which would require partner approval and a separate timeline amendment.
