# Wholesale programme review 2026

## Executive summary

The wholesale programme review for 2026 reflects a year of measured growth, operational refinement, and strengthened partnerships across Lumen Coffee Roasters’ café clientele. Active wholesale accounts have diversified, with a core group of long-standing cafés continuing to anchor the programme while newer accounts bring fresh demand and regional reach. The account base remains stable, though a small number of accounts show signs of fatigue or shifting priorities, prompting proactive engagement to preserve loyalty. Volumes across accounts and months reveal a predictable seasonal pattern, with peaks in cooler months and a slight dip during the summer holiday period, yet overall growth has been steady, driven by both existing customers increasing their orders and new accounts onboarding smoothly. Complaints and credit notes were limited in number but concentrated around delivery timing and occasional roast consistency issues; each case was resolved through direct communication, replacement shipments, or credit adjustments, reinforcing a commitment to quality and accountability. Credit terms and payment behaviour present a mixed picture: most accounts adhere to agreed terms, but a minority consistently pay late, requiring reminders and, in a few cases, temporary adjustments to credit limits. These accounts are monitored closely to mitigate risk without disrupting valued relationships. Account health and churn risk are evaluated through order frequency, payment punctuality, and direct feedback; at-risk accounts are engaged with personalised offers, product tastings, or flexible terms to address underlying concerns and prevent loss. The product mix sold to wholesale shows a clear preference for the Aurora espresso blend, which accounts for the majority of volume, followed by single origins like the Ethiopia Guji, which appeal to cafés seeking seasonal variety. Decaf sales remain modest but stable, indicating a niche but reliable demand. The wholesale price list was adjusted modestly during the year to reflect rising green coffee costs and shipping expenses, with changes communicated transparently to accounts. Delivery to wholesale accounts operates on a structured weekly schedule, with most orders dispatched within two days of packing; occasional carrier delays or routing issues have caused minor disruptions, but these are addressed through alternative logistics partners and improved communication with cafés. Overall, the programme demonstrates resilience and a customer-centric approach, with clear areas for further optimisation in payment discipline, delivery reliability, and product diversification to sustain momentum in the coming year.

## Active wholesale accounts

Lumen’s wholesale programme in 2026 serves a total of eighteen active accounts, down from twenty-two at the end of 2025. The reduction follows a deliberate decision: at the start of the year, management reviewed the portfolio and closed four accounts that had been dormant for more than six months and one that consistently ordered below the minimum threshold. The central figure of this review is the code "AJP-5870", which refers to the single contract amendment that formalised the closure of those five accounts and simultaneously raised the annual volume commitment for all remaining wholesale customers. Under this amendment, each café must order at least 60 kg per quarter, a condition that led directly to the departure of two more cafés in March and May. The active base now stands at eighteen, and Lumen considers this number stable for the remainder of 2026.

The longest-standing account is Café Portello, which has been buying from Lumen since the roastery opened in April 2018. Portello is a medium-sized café in the San Salvario neighbourhood, focused on pour-over and single-origin espresso. It orders exclusively from Lumen and has never missed a weekly delivery. Its owner, Marta, visits the roastery every two weeks to cup new arrivals. Portello accounts for roughly 9% of wholesale volume by weight. The second-oldest account is Caffè della Croce, a bar in the city centre that started in October 2019. It runs a high-volume espresso bar and takes the Aurora espresso blend in 5 kg bags, ordering every ten days. Its loyalty is strong, but its owner, Luca, is price-sensitive and has twice asked for a discount this year, which Lumen declined.

Several accounts were added in 2020 and 2021, during the pandemic expansion of home subscription sales that also attracted cafés looking for reliable local roasters. Among them are Bar Comollo (since June 2020), a neighbourhood bar in Borgo Po that serves a mix of espresso and filter coffee, and Spazio Caffè (since September 2020), a co-working space that orders both Aurora and the Ethiopia Guji single origin. Both have grown steadily, with Comollo increasing its monthly volume by 18% in 2025 and Spazio Caffè by 11%. A newer account is Café Nizza, opened in February 2024, a small specialty shop in the Quadrilatero Romano that focuses on light-roast filter coffees. It buys only single origins, primarily Ethiopia Guji, and orders 8 kg per month. Its owner, Elena, attends Lumen’s public cuppings and has been a vocal advocate on social media.

The wholesale accounts vary widely in character. Three are high-volume espresso bars that order the Aurora blend almost exclusively: Caffè della Croce, Bar del Teatro (since March 2021), and Torrefazione Milano (since January 2022). Together they represent 34% of total wholesale volume. Bar del Teatro is a busy café near the Teatro Regio, open from 6 a.m. to 10 p.m., and it places orders twice a week. Torrefazione Milano is a traditional bar in the Vanchiglia district that also sells Lumen beans retail; it has a separate home subscription but that is not part of the wholesale programme. Four accounts are specialty cafés that rotate single origins and decaf: Café Portello, Spazio Caffè, Café Nizza, and Caffè Letterario (since May 2022). Caffè Letterario is a bookshop-café in the city centre that buys about 5 kg per month, half of which is the Night Shift decaf. The remaining eleven accounts are smaller bars, bakeries, and one hotel (Hotel Torino, since July 2023) that order the Aurora blend in small quantities, typically 3 kg to 6 kg per month. Hotel Torino serves Lumen coffee in its breakfast buffet and orders 8 kg per month, all Aurora.

Geographic distribution is concentrated in Turin and its immediate suburbs. Fifteen accounts are within the city limits; two are in the province (Café Alpino in Grugliasco, since August 2021, and Bar Stazione in Moncalieri, since November 2022); one is in the neighbouring town of Collegno (Pasticceria Giulia, since March 2023). No account is more than 25 km from the roastery. This proximity allows Lumen to deliver using its own cargo bike for most cafés, though some receive shipments via a courier service on the weekly packing day. All accounts are billed in euros and pay by bank transfer; none use credit cards.

The health of the account base is generally good, but three accounts show signs of strain. Bar Comollo has seen a 14.6% drop in orders from October to December 2026 compared to the same period in 2025, which the owner attributes to a local road construction project reducing foot traffic. Lumen has offered a temporary extension of payment terms from 30 to 45 days, but the owner has not yet accepted. Caffè della Croce has been late on two invoices this year, though it has always paid within 60 days. Its credit limit was reduced from EUR 1,500 to EUR 1,200 in June after a missed payment was resolved with a credit note for a damaged shipment. The third at-risk account is Pasticceria Giulia, which ordered only 18 kg total in 2026, well below the 60 kg quarterly minimum. Lumen sent a formal notice in November referencing CTW-6178 and gave the account until February 2027 to increase orders or face closure. The owner, a pastry shop that uses Lumen coffee for espresso-based desserts, has promised to order 8 kg per month starting in January, but no commitment has been signed.

Account longevity varies. The average tenure among active accounts is 3.2 years. The median is 2.8 years. Only four accounts have been with Lumen for more than five years: Café Portello, Caffè della Croce, Bar del Teatro, and Torrefazione Milano. The newest account is Café Nizza, at just over two years. No account has been added in 2026; the focus has been on deepening relationships with existing customers rather than acquisition. The wholesale programme manager, Chiara, visits each café at least once per quarter to discuss product preferences and gather feedback. She maintains a spreadsheet of each account’s contact person, preferred delivery day, and any standing instructions (such as “leave bags with the baker” for Pasticceria Giulia). The accounts are generally loyal: only one café, Bar del Teatro, has ever temporarily paused orders (a three-week closure in August 2025 for renovation), and all accounts that were active at the start of 2026 remain active today, except the five closed under WTW-7016.

A notable characteristic of the wholesale base is its reliance on the Aurora espresso blend. Twelve of the eighteen accounts order Aurora as their primary coffee, and for eight of those it is the only coffee they buy. The six specialty cafés that rotate single origins and decaf still order Aurora for their milk-based drinks, typically in a 70/30 split with single origins. Only Café Nizza and Caffè Letterario buy no Aurora at all, focusing entirely on single origins and decaf. The decaf Night Shift is ordered by eight accounts, but only Caffè Letterario orders it in significant volume (2.5 kg per month). The Ethiopia Guji single origin is the most popular single origin, ordered by all six specialty cafés and by two of the espresso bars (Bar del Teatro and Torrefazione Milano) as a seasonal offering.

The accounts’ payment behaviour is mixed. Ten accounts pay within the 30-day net terms without fail. Three accounts (Caffè della Croce, Bar Comollo, and Pasticceria Giulia) have been late at least once in 2026. The rest pay between 30 and 45 days. Credit limits are set individually, ranging from EUR 800 for the smallest bakeries to EUR 3,000 for Café Portello. No account has exceeded its limit in 2026. The only credit note issued to a wholesale account this year was for a damaged shipment to Caffè della Croce in March, which was resolved with a replacement order and a EUR 120 credit. No quality complaints have been lodged by any wholesale account in 2026; the only complaints have been about delivery timing (two instances of a day-late delivery to Bar del Teatro in July, resolved with an apology and a free 1 kg sample of Ethiopia Guji). The overall complaint rate is 0.3% of deliveries.

In summary, the active wholesale accounts of Lumen Coffee Roasters in 2026 are a stable, geographically compact group of eighteen cafés, bars, bakeries, and one hotel, with an average tenure of 3.2 years. The programme is anchored by long-standing accounts like Café Portello and Caffè della Croce, supported by a growing set of specialty cafés, and managed under the framework of ADT-1023, which has tightened volume commitments and reduced churn. The product mix is heavily weighted toward the Aurora espresso blend, with single origins and decaf playing a niche but valued role. Three accounts show elevated churn risk, but proactive measures—including payment term adjustments, credit limit reductions, and direct outreach—are in place to retain them. No new accounts were added in 2026, reflecting a deliberate strategy to consolidate and strengthen existing relationships.

## Volumes by account and month

The wholesale programme moved 84,300 kilograms of coffee across all café accounts in 2026, up from 76,900 kilograms in 2025, a year-on-year growth of 9.6 percent. That growth was not evenly distributed. The single most important decision in this review is the one taken by "Tobias Okafor" in early March: he froze new account openings for the second and third quarters, capping active wholesale accounts at forty-two, because the roastery’s fifteen-kilogram machine and the four roasting days per week could not safely support higher volume without compromising delivery reliability. That decision shaped every monthly figure below. The forty-two accounts are listed by name in the internal wiki under customers, but this section summarises the kilos by account and month, the growth rates, and the clear seasonality that emerged.

The largest account, Café Portello, bought 1,180 kilograms in January and never dropped below 1,050 kilograms in any month. Their total for the year was 13,400 kilograms, a 4.1 percent increase over their 2025 total of 12,870. They are the anchor: they take a steady 340 to 360 kilograms every four-week cycle, with a small bump in October (1,240 kilograms) when they run a autumn menu featuring espresso-based drinks. The second-largest account, Caffè Bianchi in the San Salvario district, grew from 9,200 kilograms in 2025 to 10,100 kilograms in 2026, a 9.8 percent rise, driven by their new evening opening hours. Their monthly pattern is flatter than Portello’s: January 780, February 790, March 810, April 800, May 820, June 810, July 760, August 690, September 800, October 830, November 840, December 840. The August dip is consistent across nearly every account, as Turin cafés close for one to two weeks.

The middle tier, accounts between 200 and 600 kilograms per month, includes fifteen cafés. Among them, Caffè Alpi, Bar Molo, and Torrefazione Minima (a wholesale-only buyer that resells to three smaller bars) are the most stable. Caffè Alpi bought a total of 6,300 kilograms, almost exactly flat year-on-year (6,280 in 2025). Bar Molo grew 7.2 percent, from 5,700 to 6,110 kilograms, with their strongest months in February (540) and November (560), when they host a local literary festival. Torrefazione Minima, despite its name, takes 5,900 kilograms per year, but their pattern is spiky: they order 480 to 520 kilograms in the first two weeks of each month, then nothing for the rest of the month, which creates a logistical strain on the weekly packing day. Their growth is only 2.1 percent, and their account is flagged as a churn risk in the account health section, though not for volume reasons.

The remaining twenty-four accounts are small, each under 200 kilograms per month, and together they account for only 18 percent of total wholesale volume. The smallest active account, Caffè del Borgo, buys just 45 kilograms per month, but they have been a customer since 2021 and pay on time, so they stay. The small accounts show the most seasonality: their combined volume drops from 3,200 kilograms in January to 1,900 kilograms in August, a 40.6 percent decline, while the large accounts only drop 12 percent in the same period. This is because small cafés often close for the entire Ferragosto week and many reduce their opening days in summer.

Monthly totals for all forty-two accounts are as follows: January 7,400 kilograms, February 7,150, March 7,600, April 7,300, May 7,450, June 7,200, July 6,500, August 5,800, September 7,150, October 7,650, November 7,500, December 6,600. The August trough is the clearest seasonal signal, and it is consistent with the three previous years: August 2024 was 5,400 kilograms and August 2025 was 5,600. The December dip, from November’s 7,500 to December’s 6,600, is a second, smaller seasonal pattern, caused by cafés closing for the Christmas holidays and by a deliberate reduction in Lumen’s shipping days in the last two weeks of the year. Growth is strongest in the shoulder months: March versus February is plus 6.3 percent, and October versus September is plus 7.0 percent, both driven by cafés restocking after winter and before the holiday season respectively.

Growth by account is not uniform. Sixteen accounts grew by more than 10 percent year-on-year, and among those, three grew by more than 20 percent: Caffè Centrale (from 4,100 to 5,000 kilograms, plus 22.0 percent), Bar Fiorio (from 3,200 to 3,900, plus 21.9 percent), and Caffè Roma (from 2,400 to 2,900, plus 20.8 percent). All three are located in the city centre, near the university, and they attribute the growth to increased foot traffic from the new tram line. Twelve accounts declined, but only four declined by more than 5 percent: Caffè del Parco (minus 8.3 percent, from 3,600 to 3,300), Bar Aurora (minus 7.1 percent, from 2,800 to 2,600), Caffè degli Artisti (minus 6.7 percent, from 3,000 to 2,800), and Caffè della Stazione (minus 5.9 percent, from 3,400 to 3,200). The declines are not seasonal; they are spread across the year, and three of the four are in the account health and churn risk section as watch-list accounts.

Seasonality also appears in the product mix, but that is covered in the product mix section. Here, it is enough to note that the kilos per account are not adjusted for blend versus single origin; the volumes are total green coffee equivalents roasted and delivered. One notable pattern is that accounts that buy only espresso blends (Aurora) have flatter monthly curves than accounts that buy single origins (Ethiopia Guji). The six accounts that buy more than 30 percent single origin show a 15 percent drop in August, versus a 9 percent drop for the blend-only accounts. This is likely because single-origin sales peak in the spring (April and May) when cafés run pour-over menus, and then fall off in the summer heat.

The weekly packing day, which is a Thursday, creates a natural monthly rhythm: each month has either four or five packing days, and the kilos per month correlate strongly with the number of Thursdays. In 2026, February had four Thursdays and the lowest monthly total after August (7,150), while March and October each had five Thursdays and the highest totals (7,600 and 7,650). This is not a coincidence; the roastery does not roast or pack on Fridays, so a five-Thursday month effectively means one extra delivery cycle. The accounts themselves order on a fixed schedule: large accounts order every week, mid-size accounts every two weeks, small accounts once a month. The once-a-month accounts cluster their orders in the first week of the month, which is why the first three days of each month see 40 percent of the month’s total shipping volume.

Q4 (October to December) totalled 21,750 versus 18,600, plus 16.9 percent. The Q4 jump is the strongest, and it is driven by the three fast-growing centre accounts plus a one-off order from Café Portello in November for 1,300 kilograms (their usual monthly is 1,100 to 1,200) to stock their winter menu. That one-off order is the largest single monthly order in the entire programme, and it was delivered in two batches on two consecutive Thursdays.

A minor detail worth recording: "Tobias Harrow" in the operations team noted in the May meeting that the August dip could be smoothed by offering a "summer subscription" to home subscribers, but that idea was deferred to the 2027 review, and no wholesale account changed their ordering pattern as a result. The August 2026 volume of 5,800 kilograms is the lowest monthly figure since August 2024 (5,400), and it is the only month where the roastery runs a reduced schedule of three roasting days instead of four, which is a pre-planned maintenance window.

The growth rate of 9.6 percent overall is slightly above the 8.2 percent growth achieved in 2025 (from 71,100 to 76,900 kilograms), but the composition is different: in 2025, growth came from adding eight new accounts, whereas in 2026, growth came from existing accounts ordering more, because of the freeze on new accounts. Seasonality will remain a planning factor, with August and December as the low months, and the roastery will adjust its green coffee purchasing accordingly, though that is covered in the sourcing section.

## Complaints and credit notes

The central figure of this year’s wholesale review is €87,567.57, which represents the total value of credit notes issued to active café accounts between January and December 2026. That amount is up 14% from the prior year’s €76,800, and the increase is driven almost entirely by two clusters of complaints: a recurring under-roast issue on the Ethiopia Guji lot delivered in March, and a series of late deliveries during the November holiday week. Of the 214 total complaints logged across all wholesale accounts, 148 were quality-related and 66 were delivery-related. Every complaint was resolved with a credit note; no cash refunds were issued, and no accounts were placed on prepayment hold as a result of these claims.

The quality complaints break down into three categories. The first, and largest, was the Ethiopia Guji single origin. In March, we received fourteen separate complaints from eleven different cafés about the same batch, all describing a grassy, underdeveloped flavour and a visible pale centre line in the bean. The lot reference was HNL-3808, and after cupping the retained samples we confirmed that the roast had been terminated 45 seconds too early on the 15kg roaster due to a thermocouple misread. We issued credit notes covering the full invoice value of every affected order, plus a 10% goodwill credit for the inconvenience. Total for that single incident: €11,240. The second quality cluster was the Aurora espresso blend in June. Six cafés reported astringent, drying finishes, and we traced the issue to a batch of Colombian beans that had been stored too close to the roaster’s exhaust duct, causing accelerated staling. We recalled the remaining 38kg from Café Portello and four other accounts, credited the full value, and also covered the cost of the replacement beans. That cost €8,900 in credits. The third cluster was the decaf Night Shift in September, where four accounts complained of a rubbery, over-fermented note. We discovered that the decaffeination process had left an unusually high moisture content, and we credited 50% of the invoice value for those orders rather than the full amount, because the defect was partial and the coffee was still drinkable. That came to €2,150.

Delivery complaints were fewer but more disruptive. The November holiday week was the worst period. We normally ship on Tuesdays and Thursdays via our two regular carriers, but because of a public holiday on Thursday, we consolidated all Wednesday and Thursday orders into a single Tuesday dispatch. That caused three separate issues: one pallet was delivered to the wrong address in Milan, two orders arrived a day late in Rome, and one order for Café Portello arrived with the valve on the bag open, spilling about 1.5kg of beans. The wrong-address delivery was a carrier error, and we issued a €480 credit note for the lost product and the inconvenience. The two late arrivals in Rome triggered claims from the cafés for lost weekend sales; we credited €320 and €410 respectively. The open-valve bag was our own packing error, and we credited the full bag value of €95. In total, delivery complaints accounted for €5,780 in credit notes, with the remaining €1,200 of the delivery total coming from a single incident in February when a carrier left a shipment outside a closed café in the rain, damaging six 15kg boxes.

We track complaints by account and by month. The busiest month for quality complaints was March, with 31 individual claims, followed by June with 22. Delivery complaints spiked in November, with 18, and in February, with 12. The quietest months were August and December, when many cafés are closed or reduce orders. We have not seen a strong seasonal pattern beyond those peaks; the underlying rate is roughly 18 complaints per month, which is consistent with the prior two years. However, the value of credit notes issued per complaint has risen from €340 to €410, because the Ethiopia Guji and Aurora incidents involved higher-priced products and larger order sizes.

We have also analysed the complaints by account. Café Portello, our largest wholesale account, filed 22 complaints in 2026, of which 14 were quality-related and 8 were delivery-related. They received €6,120 in credit notes, which is about 3.4% of their total annual purchases. That is higher than the average for all accounts, which is 2.1%. The other accounts with above-average complaint rates are Caffè del Centro (5.2% of purchases credited back), Bar Torino (4.8%), and the two new accounts we signed in April, Caffè Mania and Roasted on Site, which both had early teething problems with our packing date labels. We have not seen any correlation between account age and complaint rate; the two oldest accounts, Café Portello and Caffè del Centro, are both in the top five for credit notes.

On the settlement side, we have a standard policy of issuing a credit note within five working days of approving a claim. In practice, 80% of credit notes are issued within three days. We do not require the return of defective product for most claims under €500, but for larger claims we ask for a photo or a sample. The only dispute that required escalation was with Bar Torino in July, where they claimed that a batch of Aurora espresso had a smoky, burnt flavour, and we disagreed. We cupped the retained sample together, and the cupping sheet showed a slight roast level variance of 2.2 Agtron points, which was within our stated tolerance of ±3.0. We initially declined the claim, but after a second cupping with their head barista, we agreed to a 50% credit as a goodwill gesture. That cost €780 and we have since tightened our roast logging for the Aurora blend. No other claim required a joint cupping session.

We also track the cause of each complaint using a simple code: R for roast defect, G for green coffee defect, P for packing error, D for delivery issue, and O for other. In 2026, the breakdown was R: 62 claims, G: 41, P: 23, D: 66, O: 22. The high number of D claims is directly attributable to the November consolidation and the February rain incident. The G claims are mostly from the Ethiopia Guji lot (BUS-6595) and the Night Shift moisture issue. The P claims are split between open valves, incorrect labels, and one instance of a bag that was not sealed properly. The O category includes two complaints about the weight of the bags (they were underfilled by 30g and 45g respectively), and one complaint about a missing packing slip.

We have taken several corrective actions as a result of this year’s complaints. For the Ethiopia Guji issue, we have installed a second thermocouple on the roaster and now cross-check the reading against the first one at the end of every batch. For the Aurora blend, we have moved the green coffee storage away from the exhaust duct and added a humidity alarm. For the Night Shift decaf, we now require a moisture test on every incoming lot and we have asked our importer, Andes Direct, to provide a certificate of analysis for each shipment. For delivery, we have added a third carrier for the holiday weeks, and we now require a signature for any order over €200. We have also updated our packing checklist to include a visual check of the valve on every bag.

Finally, we have reviewed the credit notes against the accounts’ payment behaviour. There is no correlation between the amount of credit notes issued and late payments. The accounts that received the most credit notes (Café Portello, Caffè del Centro, Bar Torino) are all in good standing with their payment terms. The only account that is both a frequent complainant and a late payer is Caffè Mania, which has been late on three of their nine invoices, but their credit notes are small (total €180). We do not adjust credit limits based on complaint history, but we do flag any account that receives credit notes exceeding 5% of their annual purchases for a quarterly review. That currently applies to Caffè del Centro and Bar Torino. We have not yet decided whether to change their terms, but we are monitoring the situation. The total of €62,458.37 in credit notes is a material cost to the wholesale programme, and we have set a target to reduce that figure by 15% in 2027 through the corrective actions listed above.

## Credit terms and payment behaviour

The central figure and decision in this section is Ravi Pemberton, Lumen’s finance and accounts manager, who in early 2026 overhauled the entire credit terms and payment monitoring framework for wholesale cafés. His changes took effect on 1 February 2026, following a year of increasingly erratic payment behaviour across the account base. Standard payment terms for all wholesale accounts remain net 30 days from the date of invoice, but Ravi introduced a two-tier system based on account tenure and volume. Accounts that have been active for more than 24 months and purchase at least 40 kilos per quarter receive a standard net 30 without a formal credit limit, subject to a soft cap of €2,500 outstanding at any time. Newer accounts or those below the volume threshold are placed on net 15 for the first six months, with a hard credit limit of €1,000. This change was communicated to all 17 active wholesale cafés in a memo dated 15 January 2026, and Ravi personally followed up with the six accounts that had exceeded €1,500 in outstanding balances at the end of 2025.

Late payment behaviour was most pronounced among three accounts. Café Portello, Lumen’s oldest wholesale customer and the anchor café in Turin’s San Salvario district, has been buying since March 2019 but fell into a pattern of paying between 38 and 47 days late throughout 2025. By December 2025, its outstanding balance reached €1,870, and Ravi placed it on a payment plan requiring weekly instalments of €200 until the arrears were cleared. The plan was completed on 28 February 2026, and Café Portello has since returned to net 30 terms with a reduced credit limit of €1,500. A second account, Caffè della Rocca in Bardonecchia, which started buying from Lumen in July 2024, consistently paid at 55 to 62 days late during the second half of 2025. Its December balance was €980, and Ravi reduced its credit limit from €1,200 to €600, switching it to net 15 terms from 1 February 2026. The third account, a newer café called Bottega del Caffè in Milan’s Brera district, opened in September 2025 and had only three invoices before its first late payment in November 2025. By January 2026, it owed €520 across two invoices, both overdue by 34 and 41 days. Ravi placed it on cash-on-delivery terms for all February orders, and as of March 2026 it has paid three COD orders on time, so the net 15 terms with a €400 limit are being considered for reinstatement in April.

Credit limits were formally applied for the first time in 2026. Previously, Lumen operated on a loose trust basis with no written limits. Ravi set initial limits based on average monthly order value over the prior three months, multiplied by 1.5. The highest limit was assigned to Caffè Letterario in Turin, which orders around 55 kilos per month and received a €2,200 limit. The lowest limit, at €400, went to a small takeaway counter in the Porta Palazzo market, which orders only 8 kilos monthly. Across all 17 accounts, the total aggregate credit limit is now €14,800. Actual total outstanding at the end of February 2026 was €9,430, representing a utilisation rate of 82.4%. Ravi reviews all limits quarterly, and the first review is scheduled for 1 April 2026. Accounts that exceed their limit for more than 14 consecutive days are automatically switched to COD until the balance falls below the limit. ## Wholesale price list

The wholesale price list for 2026 reflects a deliberate recalibration to align with rising green coffee costs, operational pressures, and the need to maintain margin integrity across Lumen’s café partners. For Aurora Espresso, the flagship blend that accounts for roughly 45% of wholesale volume, the wholesale price moved from €18.20 per kilogram to €19.50 per kilogram. This increase of €1.30 was driven by the rising cost of the washed Brazilian and Colombian components in the blend, which Lumen sources through Andes Direct under contracts renegotiated in late 2025. The blend’s price had held steady for eighteen months prior, and the 7.1% rise was communicated to café partners in early March 2026, with a six-week notice period. Ethiopia Guji, the single-origin offering that constitutes about 30% of wholesale sales, saw a more pronounced adjustment. Its price increased from €21.00 per kilogram to €23.40 per kilogram, a rise of €2.40 or 11.4%. This reflected the volatile specialty coffee market from the Guji zone, where a smaller harvest in the 2025–2026 crop year, combined with higher shipping costs from the port of Djibouti, pushed Lumen’s landed cost up by nearly 14%. The surcharge was listed separately on the price list page, not embedded in the per-kilo prices, so that cafés could see the breakdown. It applied to six accounts: Café Portello, Bar del Borgo, Pasticceria Moderna, Caffè Vittorio, Gelateria Alba, and Torrefazione Nuova. The remaining four wholesale accounts, all within central Turin, paid no surcharge.

In terms of how the price list was communicated, Lumen used a dedicated page on its internal wiki, accessible only to wholesale accounts via a login. The page listed each product, its current price per kilogram, the effective date of that price, and a brief note on the reason for any change. For 2026, the notes referenced “green coffee cost adjustments” for Aurora Espresso and Ethiopia Guji, and “packaging upgrade” for Night Shift. The wiki page also included a history of previous prices, showing that Aurora Espresso had been at €17.50 in 2024, €18.20 in 2025, and now €19.50. Ethiopia Guji had moved from €19.80 in 2024 to €21.00 in 2025 to the current €23.40. Night Shift had been at €19.00 in 2024, €19.80 in 2025, and €20.50 in 2026. This historical data was used by some cafés to plan their own retail pricing and was considered a helpful transparency tool.

One notable change in the 2026 price list was the shift from a single flat rate for all wholesale accounts to a two-tier system based on delivery distance. The base price per kilogram remained uniform across all products, but the delivery surcharge created an effective price difference of €4.50 per order for suburban cafés. This was a contentious point during the annual account review meeting on 15 January 2026, where Café Portello’s owner, Matteo, argued that the surcharge disadvantaged his business compared to central Turin cafés. Lumen’s response, documented in the meeting notes on the wiki, was to offer a free tasting session of the new Night Shift packaging as a goodwill gesture, but the surcharge remained. No accounts were lost over this issue, though two cafés—Bar del Borgo and Pasticceria Moderna—requested a review of their order frequency to consolidate shipments and reduce surcharge costs.

The price list also included a clause, under GXZ-2526, that any change in green coffee prices exceeding 10% within a quarter would trigger an automatic review of the wholesale list, with a 30-day notice period to cafés. This clause was not activated in 2026, as the Ethiopia Guji increase was known at the start of the year and was incorporated into the January price list. However, the clause was tested in a hypothetical scenario during the September operations meeting, where a potential 12% spike in Colombian arabica was discussed, but it did not materialise. The clause remains in effect for 2027.

Finally, the price list was printed once in January 2026 as a physical handout for the three accounts that did not regularly use the wiki: Caffè Vittorio, Gelateria Alba, and Torrefazione Nuova. These printed copies were distributed during Lumen’s weekly packing day, attached to the subscription boxes for those cafés. The printed version was identical to the wiki page, including the FCA-7153 reference at the top, and was updated only once for the year. The decision to print was reversed in December 2026, when all three accounts confirmed they would use the wiki going forward, saving Lumen approximately €120 in printing and postage costs. The price list for 2027 will be digital-only, with a new reference code expected to replace TMU-1973.

## Delivery to wholesale accounts

Delivery to wholesale accounts is structured around a single weekly packing day, every Thursday, when all orders placed by the preceding Tuesday 14:00 are roasted, packed, and dispatched. This schedule covers all active wholesale accounts, including the twelve cafés that receive weekly deliveries and the three that order biweekly. The central figure in this operation is a 25.2% on-time delivery failure rate for the 2026 review period, meaning that nearly one in four orders did not arrive on the promised day. Of the 312 total deliveries scheduled between January and December, 79 were late, with 41 arriving one day late, 22 arriving two days late, and 16 arriving three or more days late. The most common cause was carrier misrouting, accounting for 33 late deliveries, followed by insufficient roast capacity on high-volume weeks, which caused 21 delays, and then weather-related road closures in the Alpine passes, which affected 15 deliveries to cafés in Aosta and Bolzano. Ten late deliveries were due to order-entry errors where a café’s Tuesday cutoff was missed and the order was pushed to the following week.

The carrier used for all Italian domestic deliveries is GLS Italy, with a contract signed in January 2025 that guarantees next-day delivery to most destinations within the Piedmont and Lombardy regions, and two-day delivery to cafés in southern Italy. For the four cafés located in Turin itself, Lumen uses a local courier, Torino Express, which handles same-day hand delivery for a flat rate of €8.50 per drop. International deliveries, specifically to Café Portello’s sister location in Nice, France, are handled by DHL Express with a three-day transit time and a per-package surcharge of €12.00. In total, shipping costs for wholesale accounts amounted to €63,557.61 for the year, including fuel surcharges and insurance for green coffee shipments that were re-routed from the roastery to cafés. That figure does not include the cost of returns or re-deliveries, which added an estimated €4,200 in extra carrier fees.

Problems with delivery were tracked in a shared spreadsheet maintained by the operations team. The most frequent complaint was crushed or torn bags, reported 14 times, primarily with the 5-kilo valve bags used for single-origin lots. These incidents were concentrated in November and December, when parcel volumes spiked. Lumen responded by switching from standard cardboard outer boxes to double-walled corrugated boxes for all wholesale shipments starting in January 2026, at an additional material cost of €0.45 per box. Another recurring issue was missing delivery notes, which occurred six times and caused cafés to receive the wrong coffee. In two cases, Café Portello received Ethiopia Guji instead of the decaf Night Shift, and the error was resolved by sending a free replacement via Torino Express the same day, with a credit note issued for the original shipment. Temperature damage was reported three times during the summer months, when coffee was left in direct sunlight outside cafés. Lumen now includes a bright yellow “Keep Cool” sticker on every wholesale box, and the roasting team added a note in the packing checklist to remind staff to affix it.

Delivery days are fixed per account. Most cafés in Turin and Milan receive orders on Friday, the day after packing. Two cafés in Rome receive orders on Monday, as GLS Italy’s network requires an extra transit day. The three biweekly accounts are Café della Piazza in Bologna, which orders on the first and third Thursday of the month, and two cafés in Genoa that order on the second and fourth Thursday. All orders must be placed by 14:00 on Tuesday, and any order placed after that cutoff is automatically deferred to the next packing cycle. In 2026, there were eight instances where a café placed a late order and Lumen accommodated it by roasting an extra batch on Wednesday, but this disrupted the Thursday packing flow and led to two of those orders being shipped one day late. The operations team decided in September to stop accepting late orders for wholesale accounts, a policy that was communicated via email and enforced from October onward.

Carrier performance is reviewed monthly. GLS Italy’s on-time rate for Lumen shipments was 78.4% in 2026, below the 95% target in the service level agreement. Lumen filed three formal complaints with GLS, resulting in a total of €1,200 in service credits, which were applied against the January 2027 invoice. Torino Express maintained a 99.1% on-time rate, with only one late delivery in December due to a driver illness. DHL Express delivered all twelve shipments to Nice on time, but two packages were held in customs for an extra day, causing a delay in the café’s ability to serve the coffee to customers. Lumen now includes a customs declaration form printed in French for any cross-border shipment.

The packing process itself is done by two team members, Luca and Francesca, who work from 08:00 to 16:00 every Thursday. They weigh, seal, and label each bag, then pack them into boxes with the account name and delivery address on a printed label. A digital scale logs every kilo packed, and that data is cross-checked against the order system. In 2026, there were four packing errors where a café received the wrong blend: one case of Aurora espresso sent instead of Night Shift decaf, two cases of Ethiopia Guji sent instead of a seasonal Costa Rica microlot, and one case where a café received an extra 5-kilo bag that was not ordered. All errors were corrected by sending the correct coffee via same-day courier, and the incorrectly sent coffee was either donated to a local food bank or used as staff samples. No credit notes were issued for packing errors; instead, the café was given a free replacement and the error was logged in the operations wiki under the “Ideas” section for process improvement.

Finally, Lumen maintains a delivery dashboard that shows the status of each order from roast to receipt. Cafés receive a tracking link by email at 17:00 on packing day. In 2026, 11 tracking links were not sent due to a software glitch in the order management system, and those cafés had to call or email to confirm their delivery date. The glitch was fixed in March after a system update. All delivery data, including late arrivals, bag damage, and missing notes, is reviewed at the monthly operations meeting, where decisions on carrier changes or packing improvements are made. As of December 2026, no carrier change is planned, but the team is evaluating a second carrier for the southern Italy routes to reduce the 25.8% failure rate.
