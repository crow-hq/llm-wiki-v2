SHIPPING TERMS REVISION 2027

1. Purpose
This document revises Lumen Coffee Roasters’ shipping terms to reflect a change in carrier, a new free shipping threshold, updated delivery promises, and a shift in the weekly subscription packing schedule. The purpose of these revisions is to improve reliability, reduce costs, and streamline operations for both wholesale partners and home subscribers, while maintaining the quality and care that defines Lumen’s specialty roasting.
The new carrier was selected after a thorough evaluation of service consistency, transit times, and pricing. The previous carrier, while satisfactory in some regions, had become increasingly unpredictable in delivery windows and more expensive for the volumes Lumen ships. The chosen partner offers better tracking, more uniform handling across zones, and a lower base rate for parcels, which allows Lumen to absorb some costs while passing on savings to customers. This change also reduces the number of damaged parcels reported in recent months, as the new carrier has a superior record for fragile goods.
The free shipping threshold has been adjusted to a level that balances customer expectations with operational reality. Previously, the threshold was set too low, leading to many small orders that barely covered the cost of transport. The new threshold encourages slightly larger orders without discouraging trial purchases, and it applies equally to wholesale accounts and subscriptions. For wholesale clients like Café Portello, this means more efficient deliveries and fewer partial shipments. For subscribers, it simplifies decision-making and aligns with the new packing day.
Delivery times are now defined by geographic zones, reflecting actual transit performance rather than optimistic estimates. Each zone has a promised window from dispatch to arrival, and these windows are based on data from the new carrier’s network. This change reduces customer frustration from missed expectations and allows Lumen’s team to manage communication more honestly. The zones cover all domestic and major European destinations served by the roastery.
The subscription packing day moves to Wednesday. This shift was driven by the need to align production and shipping schedules more tightly. Roasting happens four days a week, and Wednesday packing ensures that fresh coffee leaves the roastery at the start of the week’s shipping cycle, arriving by the weekend for most subscribers. The cut-off for order changes is earlier on Tuesday, giving the small team enough time to pack all subscription boxes accurately. This also frees up other days for wholesale orders and special blends.
Subscription boxes are handled with special care. Each box is labelled with the subscriber’s name, blend or origin, and roast date, and is packed in a way that protects the beans from light and impact. The labels are designed to be readable by the new carrier’s sorting systems, reducing misrouting. Returns and damaged parcels follow a clear procedure: customers report issues within a short window, Lumen verifies the claim with the carrier, and a replacement or credit is issued promptly. The goal is to resolve problems within a few days, keeping trust high and waste low.

Overall, these revisions aim to make Lumen’s shipping more efficient, transparent, and customer-friendly. They reflect the roastery’s commitment to quality from green bean to doorstep, and they support the twelve people working here by reducing manual corrections and improving workflow. The changes take effect at the start of the new year, and all customers will be informed in advance.

2. New carrier selection
The decision to replace Lumen’s existing parcel carrier with a new provider is grounded in a single, central document reference: “WPC-4440”. This code identifies the internal logistics review that was completed in September 2026 and which served as the formal basis for the carrier transition. “DHU-8731” is not a contract number or a lot reference; it is the case file compiled by the operations team after a six-week comparative analysis of five European courier services. The file contains performance data, cost breakdowns, delivery time maps, and customer feedback summaries from the previous twelve months. Every recommendation that follows in this section—carrier identity, service levels, pricing structure, and operational alignment—is drawn from the findings documented in “RPS-1922”. The file was presented to the weekly sync on 15 September 2026, where the team voted unanimously to proceed with the change, and the formal engagement letter with the new carrier was signed on 1 October 2026, effective for all shipments commencing 1 January 2027.
The carrier being replaced is Poste Italiane’s business parcel division, which has handled Lumen’s outbound shipments since the roastery’s founding in 2019. Under the outgoing arrangement, Poste Italiane collected parcels every weekday at 15:00 from the roastery’s loading bay in Turin. For Italian destinations, the service offered 1-2 working day delivery at a base rate of €5.90 per parcel, with free shipping applied to orders over €45. For European Union addresses, the service delivered in 3-5 working days at a flat €12.90 per parcel. These terms are currently documented in the wiki note “Carriers and delivery times”. Poste Italiane’s performance over the 2025-2026 period, as recorded in “FGM-6974”, showed an on-time delivery rate of 89.7% for Italy and 84.2% for the EU. The average time from collection to first scan was 4.3 hours in Italy and 11.6 hours for cross-border parcels. Customer-reported damage incidents, which require photographic evidence and are reported to the carrier within eight days under the existing refund policy, amounted to 1.8% of total shipments in 2025. The carrier’s claims resolution time averaged 14 business days. These metrics, while not catastrophic, fell short of the service standards the roastery requires for its subscription programme and wholesale accounts, particularly as subscription volumes have grown to approximately 420 bags per week and are projected to reach 550 bags per week by mid-2027.

The new carrier selected through the “MEG-3244” process is DHL Express Italy, specifically the DHL Express Easy business service tier. The choice was made after a weighted scoring exercise that evaluated each candidate on six criteria: transit time reliability, geographic coverage, pickup flexibility, per-parcel cost at projected 2027 volumes, damage claim processing speed, and integration with Lumen’s existing shipping software, ShipStation. DHL Express Italy scored highest in four of the six categories. Its transit time reliability for Italian destinations was 97.3% on-time within the stated window during the evaluation period, and for EU destinations it was 94.1%. The pickup flexibility was a decisive factor: DHL Express Italy offers a daily scheduled pickup window that can be set between 14:00 and 17:00, adjustable by 30-minute increments, which directly accommodates the forthcoming shift of subscription packing to Wednesday. The per-parcel cost at the projected 2027 volume of 1,800 parcels per month (combining subscriptions, wholesale drops, and occasional retail orders) came to €5.20 for Italian standard parcels and €11.40 for EU standard parcels, both including fuel surcharges and insurance up to €500 declared value. These rates are lower than Poste Italiane’s current €5.90 and €12.90, and the reduction is one of the factors that allows Lumen to revise the free shipping threshold without eroding margin. The damage claim process with DHL Express Italy is notably faster: claims are acknowledged within 24 hours of submission, and the average resolution time during the trial period was 3.2 business days, compared to the 14-day average under Poste Italiane. This improvement is significant because the existing refund policy, referenced in the wiki note “Refund policy”, requires damaged bags to be replaced without requiring the customer to return the product, and a faster claims cycle reduces the financial exposure and administrative burden on the roastery.
The formal transition plan, as outlined in “HCP-3187”, was executed in four phases during the fourth quarter of 2026. Phase one, completed on 15 October 2026, consisted of a two-week parallel run in which 15% of all outgoing parcels—selected randomly from both subscription and wholesale orders—were shipped via DHL Express Italy while the remainder continued with Poste Italiane. This phase allowed the operations team to test label formatting, pickup timing, and tracking integration without disrupting the main shipping flow. Tobias Lindqvist, the operations coordinator who joined Lumen in March 2026, was assigned to monitor the parallel run and record any discrepancies in delivery performance or customer feedback. His logs, which are appended to “WAT-1342”, noted no significant issues: one parcel was delayed by a single day due to a customs documentation error in Germany, and two labels printed with a misaligned barcode that was corrected by adjusting the ShipStation template. Phase two, completed on 1 November 2026, expanded the parallel run to 50% of all parcels. Phase three, completed on 1 December 2026, involved a full cutover for all non-subscription shipments—wholesale orders to cafés such as Café Portello, sample orders, and occasional retail purchases—while subscription parcels remained with Poste Italiane for one final cycle. Phase four, the complete transition, took effect on 1 January 2027, when all parcels, including subscriptions, began shipping exclusively via DHL Express Italy. The existing contract with Poste Italiane was terminated with 30 days’ notice on 15 November 2026, and the final collection by Poste Italiane occurred on 31 December 2026 at 15:00.

The pickup schedule under the new carrier differs from the outgoing arrangement in several concrete ways. DHL Express Italy will collect parcels Monday through Friday, but the daily pickup time is set to 16:00 rather than the previous 15:00. This one-hour shift is intentional: it accommodates the new Wednesday packing day for subscriptions, which requires packing to be completed by 15:30 to allow a 30-minute buffer before the carrier’s arrival. For the other three roasting days—Monday, Thursday, and Friday—the 16:00 pickup provides additional flexibility for last-minute wholesale orders that are often packed after the morning roast. The pickup is performed by a dedicated DHL Express Italy van that serves the Turin metropolitan area; the driver, who is assigned to Lumen’s account, arrives at the loading bay and scans each parcel using a handheld terminal before loading. This scan generates the first tracking event, which is visible to the customer within minutes via the DHL Express tracking portal. Under the Poste Italiane arrangement, the first scan often occurred at the regional sorting centre, typically two to three hours after collection, which created a gap in tracking visibility that occasionally prompted customer inquiries. The new carrier’s near-instant first scan is expected to reduce such inquiries.
The selection of DHL Express Italy also necessitated changes to the shipping software configuration and label format. The ShipStation account was updated on 15 December 2026 to use DHL Express Italy’s API, which supports print-on-demand labels with integrated customs documentation for EU shipments. Each label now includes the Lumen logo, the customer’s name and address, the service level (DHL Express Easy), a unique tracking barcode, and a field for the declared contents. For subscription boxes, the label also includes a small printed tag reading “Subscription – Week XX, 2027” to allow the packing team to identify subscription parcels quickly during pickup. This tag is not a separate sticker but is embedded in the label design as a secondary text line, generated automatically by the ShipStation rule that identifies orders tagged as “subscription” in the order management system. The label format was tested during the parallel run phase and validated by Odette Thorne’s inspection logs, which are part of the “XKC-9772” record.

3. Free shipping threshold
The new free shipping threshold for 2027 was set at €55 for all shipments within Italy, replacing the previous threshold of €45 that had been in place since Lumen Coffee Roasters began its wholesale and subscription operations. This decision was made by Marisol Delacroix, the operations manager, after a detailed review of shipping cost data from the previous eighteen months and a comparison of three potential carriers during the selection process that ultimately led to the new carrier, EuropaLogistics, replacing the previous carrier, Corriere Nazionale. The effect of raising the threshold from €45 to €55 is intended to align the free shipping offer more closely with the actual average order value for both wholesale cafés and home subscribers, while also offsetting the higher base rates that EuropaLogistics charges for parcels weighing more than five kilograms. The previous carrier, Corriere Nazionale, offered free shipping at €45, but its service reliability had declined in the second half of 2026, with an average of 4.3 percent of parcels delayed by more than one working day in the Italy zone and 7.1 percent delayed in the European Union zone. EuropaLogistics, the new carrier selected after a three-month trial that ended in November 2026, provides a more consistent delivery performance with a contractual guarantee of 98.5 percent on-time delivery within Italy and 96 percent within the European Union, but its pricing structure is different: the base rate for a standard parcel up to two kilograms within Italy is €6.90, compared to the previous €5.90, and the surcharge for each additional kilogram up to ten kilograms is €1.20, compared to the previous €0.90. Because Lumen Coffee Roasters ships subscription boxes that typically weigh between 1.2 kilograms for a single twelve-ounce bag and 2.8 kilograms for a double-bag subscription, and because wholesale orders from cafés such as Café Portello often include multiple five-kilogram bags of green coffee or roasted blends, the average parcel weight for the Italy zone in 2026 was 3.4 kilograms. Under the previous carrier, a 3.4-kilogram parcel would have cost €8.30 to ship within Italy (€5.90 base plus €2.40 for the two extra kilograms), and the free shipping threshold of €45 meant that a customer ordering two bags of Ethiopia Guji at €22 each, for a total of €44, would pay €8.30 shipping, while a customer ordering two bags of Aurora espresso at €24 each, for a total of €48, would qualify for free shipping. Under the new carrier, the same 3.4-kilogram parcel costs €10.50 to ship within Italy (€6.90 base plus €3.60 for the three extra kilograms), and the free shipping threshold of €55 means that a customer ordering two bags of Ethiopia Guji at €22 each, for a total of €44, would now pay €10.50 shipping instead of €8.30, while a customer ordering two bags of Aurora espresso at €24 each, for a total of €48, would still pay €10.50 shipping rather than qualifying for free shipping. Only when the total order value reaches €55 or more does the shipping cost become zero for the customer. For the European Union zone, the free shipping threshold remains unchanged at €0, meaning that no free shipping is offered for EU destinations; the new carrier charges €14.90 for a standard parcel up to two kilograms to EU countries, with a surcharge of €1.80 per additional kilogram up to ten kilograms, compared to the previous €12.90 base and €1.50 per kilogram. The effect of the higher threshold on customer behavior was modeled using data from the subscription and wholesale order history for the period of January to October 2026, which showed that 38 percent of all Italy-zone orders had a value between €45 and €54.99, meaning that under the previous threshold those orders were shipped free of charge to the customer, but under the new threshold they will incur a shipping cost ranging from €6.90 to €14.70 depending on weight. The modeling assumed that approximately 15 percent of those customers would reduce their order value to avoid the shipping cost, 20 percent would increase their order value to cross the €55 threshold, and 65 percent would keep their current order value and pay the shipping cost. The net projected effect on total shipping revenue is an increase of approximately €4,200 per year, based on an average of 2,400 Italy-zone orders per month and a blended shipping cost recovery rate of 62 percent under the new carrier pricing. This revenue increase partially offsets the higher base rates charged by EuropaLogistics, which are expected to raise Lumen Coffee Roasters total shipping cost by approximately €6,800 per year compared to the previous carrier, assuming the same order volume and weight distribution. The free shipping threshold change was communicated to wholesale cafés via a direct email sent on 15 December 2026, with a notice period of thirty days before the new terms took effect on 15 January 2027. Home subscribers were notified through the monthly newsletter sent on 1 December 2026, and a banner was added to the checkout page on the Lumen Coffee Roasters website on 20 December 2026, explaining that free shipping within Italy applies to orders of €55 or more. The wiki note titled Carriers and delivery times, which previously stated free shipping over €45 in Italy, was updated on 10 January 2027 to reflect the new threshold of €55, and the note now reads: Italy, 1-2 working days, €6.90 base, free over €55; European Union, 3-5 working days, €14.90 base. The wiki note also now specifies that parcels are collected every weekday at 15:00, which remains unchanged from the previous carrier arrangement, although the collection point has moved from the loading dock to the new packing area because the subscription packing day has shifted to Wednesday. The effect on subscription boxes is particularly relevant because the subscription packing day moves from Tuesday to Wednesday, as detailed in the Wednesday packing day section of this document, and the free shipping threshold change interacts with the subscription pricing structure: the standard subscription for a single bag of coffee, such as the Ethiopia Guji or the Night Shift decaf, costs €24 per shipment, and the double-bag subscription costs €44 per shipment, both of which are below the new €55 threshold. Therefore, all subscription shipments within Italy will incur a shipping cost under the new terms, unless the subscriber adds items to their order to reach the €55 threshold, such as an additional bag of coffee or a merchandise item like a ceramic cup or a coffee scoop. The subscription packing workflow, which previously roasted on Monday, packed on Tuesday morning, and shipped on Tuesday afternoon, now roasts on Tuesday, packs on Wednesday morning, and ships on Wednesday afternoon, as described in the Wednesday packing day section, and the cut-off for subscription orders to be included in the weekly packing run is now Wednesday at 08:00, which is two hours earlier than the previous Tuesday at 10:00 cut-off. This change was coordinated with Tobias Vasquez, the lead roaster, who adjusted the roasting schedule to ensure that all subscription coffees are roasted by Tuesday evening and available for the Wednesday morning packing session. The free shipping threshold of €55 applies to all orders placed through the Lumen Coffee Roasters website, including both one-time purchases and subscription orders, and the threshold is calculated based on the total value of items in the cart before any discounts or promo codes are applied, but after any applicable taxes. For wholesale cafés that order through the wholesale portal, the same €55 threshold applies to Italy-zone shipments, but wholesale orders typically exceed €55 per shipment because the minimum order quantity for wholesale is three bags of coffee, which at wholesale prices of €14 per bag for single origins and €16 per bag for blends results in a minimum order value of €42 for three bags of Ethiopia Guji or €48 for three bags of Aurora espresso. However, the wholesale pricing includes a 20 percent discount compared to retail prices, so the wholesale order value for three bags of Ethiopia Guji is €42, which is below the €55 threshold, and the wholesale order value for three bags of Aurora espresso is €48, also below the threshold. Therefore, wholesale cafés ordering the minimum quantity of three bags will incur shipping costs under the new terms, unless they increase their order to at least four bags of Ethiopia Guji at €56 or four bags of Aurora espresso at €64, or add other products such as decaf or merchandise to reach the threshold. The effect on wholesale orders was discussed during the weekly sync meeting on 10 December 2026, where the sales team reported that Café Portello, one of the largest wholesale accounts, typically orders eight bags of Aurora espresso and four bags of Ethiopia Guji per week, for a total wholesale value of €176, which comfortably exceeds the €55 threshold, so no shipping cost is incurred. Smaller wholesale accounts, such as independent cafés that order only three or four bags per week, will see an increase in their total cost of goods unless they adjust their order quantities. The new carrier, EuropaLogistics, requires that all parcels be labeled with a unique tracking barcode that is generated at the time of shipment, and the free shipping threshold does not affect the labeling process, which is described in the Subscription box handling section of this document. The returns and damaged parcels procedure, covered in its own section, specifies that damaged parcels must be reported to EuropaLogistics within eight days of delivery, with photographs provided by the customer, and that damaged bags of coffee are replaced without requiring the customer to return the damaged bag, consistent with the refund policy already documented in the wiki. The free shipping threshold of €55 does not apply to returns or replacements; if a customer receives a damaged bag and requests a replacement, the replacement shipment is shipped free of charge regardless of the order value, because the damage is the responsibility of the carrier. The overall effect of the free shipping threshold change, combined with the new carrier selection and the shift of subscription packing to Wednesday, is intended to improve the financial sustainability of Lumen Coffee Roasters shipping operations while maintaining the promise of 1-2 working day delivery within Italy and 3-5 working day delivery within the European Union, as guaranteed by EuropaLogistics under the new contract signed on 1 January 2027.

4. Delivery times by zone
Page 7
From 4 October 2027, Lumen Coffee Roasters will operate under a completely revised delivery time structure. This is the central figure and decision of this section: the new promised delivery times by zone take effect on that date and replace all earlier estimates that were listed in the wiki note “Carriers and delivery times.” The previous carrier, which collected parcels every weekday at 15:00 and delivered within Italy in one to two working days and within the European Union in three to five working days, is being retired. The new carrier, selected through a competitive tender process that concluded in early 2027, offers a more granular zone system with tighter and more reliable windows. The following delivery times are now promised for parcels dispatched from the roastery in Turin, Italy, based on the destination zone. For Italy, parcels are divided into two sub-zones: Zone 1 covers the region of Piedmont and the neighbouring regions of Lombardy, Liguria, and Aosta Valley; Zone 2 covers the rest of the Italian peninsula, including the islands of Sicily and Sardinia. For Zone 1, delivery is guaranteed within one working day from dispatch. For Zone 2, delivery is guaranteed within two working days from dispatch. For the European Union, parcels are divided into three sub-zones: Zone EU1 covers France, Switzerland, Austria, and Slovenia; Zone EU2 covers Germany, Belgium, the Netherlands, Luxembourg, and Denmark; Zone EU3 covers all other European Union member states, including Spain, Portugal, Ireland, Sweden, Finland, Greece, and the Baltic states, as well as the United Kingdom and Norway, which are treated as EU-equivalent for shipping purposes under the new carrier’s network. For Zone EU1, delivery is guaranteed within two working days from dispatch. For Zone EU2, delivery is guaranteed within three working days from dispatch. For Zone EU3, delivery is guaranteed within four working days from dispatch. For all other international destinations, including but not limited to North America, South America, Asia, Africa, and Oceania, delivery is guaranteed within seven working days from dispatch, though the carrier notes that customs clearance may add one to three working days beyond the transit time. These delivery times are counted in working days, meaning Monday through Friday, excluding Italian public holidays and the public holidays of the destination country. The new carrier collects parcels from the roastery once per day, Monday through Friday, at a cut-off time of 14:30. Parcels handed to the carrier after 14:30 on any given day will be considered as dispatched on the next working day. This cut-off time is thirty minutes earlier than the previous carrier’s 15:00 collection, so all packing and labelling must be completed by 14:30 to meet the same-day dispatch window. The delivery times listed above are calculated from the date of dispatch, not from the date of order. For example, a parcel dispatched on a Monday to a customer in Zone 1 will arrive on Tuesday; a parcel dispatched on a Thursday to a customer in Zone EU3 will arrive on the following Wednesday, assuming no public holidays intervene. The carrier provides a tracking number for every parcel, which is emailed to the customer at the time of dispatch, and the tracking system updates in real time with estimated delivery dates. Lumen Coffee Roasters will monitor the carrier’s performance against these promised times on a monthly basis, and any zone that experiences a consistent on-time delivery rate below 95 percent over a rolling three-month period will trigger a review of the carrier’s service in that zone, with potential renegotiation or alternative routing by 16 May 2028. That date, 28 June 2026, is the deadline for implementing any corrective measures if performance issues arise. In addition to the zone-based delivery times, the new carrier offers a guaranteed morning delivery option for Zone 1 and Zone 2 in Italy, which can be selected at checkout for an additional fee of €3.50. This option promises delivery before 12:00 on the guaranteed day. For subscription boxes, which are now packed on Wednesday as detailed elsewhere in this document, the dispatch day is Wednesday, so the delivery time counts from Wednesday’s dispatch. A subscription box dispatched on Wednesday to a customer in Zone EU2, for example, will arrive on the following Monday, assuming no public holidays. The free shipping threshold, which changed concurrently with the new carrier, is set at €60 for Italy and €90 for the European Union, as stated in the free shipping threshold section of this document. This threshold affects the delivery time only in the sense that orders below the threshold pay a shipping fee that is calculated based on the zone and the weight of the parcel, and the same delivery time guarantees apply regardless of whether the customer paid for shipping or received it free. The shipping fees for orders below the free threshold are as follows: for Italy Zone 1, €4.90; for Italy Zone 2, €6.90; for EU Zone 1, €9.90; for EU Zone 2, €12.90; for EU Zone 3, €15.90; for all other international destinations, €24.90. These fees are flat rates per parcel, not per kilogram, and are subject to change only with a formal revision of this document. The new carrier also provides a weekend delivery option for Italy Zone 1 and Zone 2, which can be requested at checkout for an additional fee of €5.00, but this option is not available for subscription boxes because subscription boxes are dispatched on Wednesday and the standard delivery time already covers Thursday or Friday arrival for most Italian customers. The carrier’s network includes a dedicated depot in Turin that sorts parcels by zone before onward transit, and this depot operates on a 24-hour cycle, so parcels collected at 14:30 are sorted overnight and dispatched to the destination depot the following morning. Lumen Coffee Roasters has tested the new carrier’s performance with a pilot shipment of 120 parcels sent to customers across all zones in August 2027, and the results showed a 97.3 percent on-time delivery rate, with the few late parcels all arriving within one additional working day. Based on this pilot, the promised delivery times are considered realistic and achievable. Customers are informed of the delivery times at checkout, both on the wholesale ordering portal for cafés such as Café Portello and on the subscription management page for home subscribers. The delivery times are also printed on the packing slip included in every parcel. For wholesale customers, the delivery time is calculated from the moment the parcel is collected by the carrier, not from the moment the order is placed, because wholesale orders may be held for consolidation or for roasting schedules. For home subscribers, the delivery time is calculated from Wednesday’s dispatch, as subscription boxes are now packed and dispatched on a single weekly packing day. The new carrier uses a colour-coded label system for parcels, which is described in the subscription box handling section of this document, but the delivery time zone is indicated by a two-letter code printed on the label: IT1 for Italy Zone 1, IT2 for Italy Zone 2, EU1 for European Union Zone 1, EU2 for European Union Zone 2, EU3 for European Union Zone 3, and INT for all other international destinations. This code is used by the carrier’s sorting system to route the parcel to the correct depot and to apply the appropriate delivery time guarantee. Lumen Coffee Roasters will provide the carrier with a daily manifest by 14:00 on each dispatch day, listing every parcel to be collected, its zone code, and its tracking number. The manifest must be accurate because the carrier’s system uses it to pre-sort parcels before physical collection. Any parcel not listed on the manifest by 14:00 will be collected the following day and will have a one-working-day delay in its delivery time. This manifest procedure is especially important for the Wednesday subscription packing day, because all subscription boxes must be packed, labelled, and entered into the manifest by 14:00 on Wednesday to ensure they are collected at 14:30 and dispatched the same day. The delivery times for subscription boxes are therefore strictly tied to the Wednesday cut-off: any subscription box that misses the 14:00 manifest deadline will be dispatched on Thursday and will arrive one working day later than the promised time for its zone. Lumen Coffee Roasters has hired an additional part-time packer to ensure that the Wednesday packing day can meet this deadline, as the previous Tuesday packing day often ran late, as noted in the weekly sync of 10 June 2026. The new carrier also offers a service level agreement that includes a penalty of €5.00 per parcel for any delivery that exceeds the promised time by more than two working days, and this penalty is paid to Lumen Coffee Roasters, not to the customer. Lumen Coffee Roasters will use this penalty to issue a credit or a replacement to the affected customer, as per the refund policy. For damaged parcels, the procedure is described in the returns and damaged parcels section of this document, but it is relevant to note here that the delivery time guarantee is suspended for any parcel that is reported as damaged, because the carrier will investigate the damage before confirming delivery. The delivery times for replacement parcels are the same as for original parcels, and the replacement is dispatched on the next available packing day, which for subscription boxes means the following Wednesday. For wholesale customers, replacements are dispatched as soon as the damaged parcel is reported, typically within one working day, subject to the 14:30 collection cut-off. The new carrier’s tracking system also provides a delivery notification that is sent to the customer by email and by SMS if the customer has opted in, and this notification includes the exact delivery time and the name of the person who signed for the parcel. For parcels left without a signature, the carrier uses a photo confirmation of the delivery location, and the delivery time is considered as the time the photo was taken. Lumen Coffee Roasters recommends that customers in zones with higher theft rates, such as certain urban areas in EU Zone 3, request a signature-required delivery at checkout, which is available at no additional cost. The delivery times for signature-required parcels are the same as for standard parcels, but the carrier may attempt delivery twice before returning the parcel to the depot. The carrier’s return-to-depot policy is that parcels are held for five working days before being returned to Lumen Coffee Roasters, and a returned parcel incurs a fee of €8.00, which is deducted from any refund or credit issued to the customer. This fee is waived if the return is due to an incorrect address provided by Lumen Coffee Roasters. The delivery times by zone are reviewed annually, and any changes will be communicated to customers at least thirty days in advance, with the next review scheduled for January 2028. The 19 October 2028 implementation date is the start of the new carrier contract, and all parcels dispatched on or after that date are subject to these delivery times, regardless of when the order was placed. Orders placed before 8 June 2028 but dispatched after that date will be shipped under the new carrier and the new delivery times. The previous carrier’s delivery times are no longer valid for any parcel dispatched from 1 May 2027 onward. This section of the document supersedes all earlier notes in the wiki regarding delivery times, including the note “Carriers and delivery times” and the note “Subscription packing day,” both of which are now outdated. The new delivery times are designed to provide more precise and reliable service to Lumen Coffee Roasters’ wholesale cafés and home subscribers, and they reflect the carrier’s stronger network in southern Europe and the improved transit times for the European Union zones. The carrier has also committed to a 98 percent on-time delivery target for the first year of the contract, with a quarterly review of performance data. Lumen Coffee Roasters will publish a summary of the delivery times on the shipping information page of the website, and the full zone definitions will be available in the customer portal. For any customer who wishes to know the specific delivery time for their address, the tracking system will show an estimated delivery date at the time of dispatch, and this estimate is guaranteed under the terms of the carrier’s service level agreement. The delivery times by zone are as follows, in summary: Italy Zone 1, one working day; Italy Zone 2, two working days; EU Zone 1, two working days; EU Zone 2, three working days; EU Zone 3, four working days; all other international destinations, seven working days. These times are effective from 17 April 2026.

5. Wednesday packing day
Page 10
The decision to move subscription packing to Wednesday was made by Ingrid Okafor, who led the operational review that identified Tuesday as the primary bottleneck in Lumen’s weekly workflow. Under the previous schedule, subscriptions were roasted on Monday, packed on Tuesday morning, and shipped on Tuesday afternoon. That sequence created a cascade of delays: roasting on Monday often ran into the late afternoon because the 15-kilogram roaster was also fulfilling wholesale orders for cafés like Café Portello and single-origin lots for the Ethiopia Guji and Night Shift decaf blends. By the time the subscription beans were roasted, the Tuesday morning packing window was tight, and the part-time packer—approved in the June 2026 board meeting—was still being recruited. The weekly sync on 10 June 2026 noted that packing was late on Tuesdays, with the carrier collection at 15:00 frequently missed. Moving packing to Wednesday resolves that pressure by inserting a full extra day between roasting and packing, and it aligns with the new carrier’s collection schedule, which now includes a Wednesday pickup at the same 15:00 window.

The new Wednesday packing day workflow is as follows. Roasting for subscriptions continues to happen on Monday, as before, because the green coffee—sourced through importers such as Andes Direct—arrives weekly and needs to be rested for at least three days after roasting before packing. All claims and returns are logged in the wiki under the “Operations” area, in a new page called “Claims ledger 2027”. The ledger records the date of the claim, the order number, the customer name, the tracking number, the reason code (DAM-01 for external damage, DAM-02 for internal damage, LOST-01 for lost in transit, RET-01 for undeliverable return, RET-02 for refused delivery), the amount of the credit paid, and the date the credit was issued. This ledger is reviewed every Friday at the weekly sync meeting, and any claim that takes longer than 5 working days to resolve is flagged for the operations manager. The target is that 95% of claims are resolved within 3 working days, and 100% within 5 working days. The old carrier, which we are replacing, had a resolution time of 11 working days on average, and the 8-day reporting window was often extended informally; that is no longer possible. The 8-day window is enforced by the claims system, and if a customer files a report on day 9, the system automatically rejects it, and the customer is directed to the refund policy, which still allows refunds within 30 days of delivery for any reason. The refund policy is separate from this shipping terms document, and it is not changed by this revision. The only overlap is that a customer who files a damage claim and receives the €4,199.92 credit cannot then also file for a refund on the same order; the credit is considered full settlement. If the customer is unhappy with the credit, they can escalate to the refund policy, but they must first return the credit, which is done by reversing the payment or deducting it from the next invoice.
For the new carrier, Nordlog, the claims process is digital and integrated with Lumen’s shipping software. When Lumen files a claim, the software automatically checks the tracking data against the customer’s photos, and if the photos match the damage description, the claim is pre-approved. In the pilot, this pre-approval happened for 78% of claims, and the remaining 22% required a human review by Nordlog’s claims team, which took on average 36 hours. The pre-approval means that the €28,356.54 credit is often issued to the customer before Nordlog even confirms the claim, and Lumen carries the float for a few days. This is acceptable because the credit amount is fixed and the volume of claims is predictable. In 2026, Lumen shipped approximately 21,800 parcels, of which 1.4% were reported as damaged or lost, which is about 305 claims per year. With the new carrier, the pilot data suggests a damage rate of 0.9% and a loss rate of 0.2%, which would be about 240 claims per year. The total annual cost of the €38,048.18 credit, assuming a 1.1% claim rate, is about €16,900, which is budgeted in the 2027 operations plan. This is a slight increase over the old system, where the average cost per claim was €52.10, but the old system also required significant staff time for manual replacements and goodwill gestures, and that time is now freed up. The part-time packer who is being hired, as mentioned in the board meeting of June 2026, will also handle the claims review as part of the Wednesday packing workflow, so no additional headcount is needed.

One specific detail that must not be lost in the transition is the handling of subscription boxes. Subscription boxes are packed on Wednesday morning, and they are labelled with a yellow “SUB” sticker on the top flap, in addition to the standard shipping label. This yellow sticker tells the Nordlog sorters that the parcel contains coffee, which is a non-hazardous food item, and that it should not be placed next to any chemical or strong-smelling parcels. The sticker also triggers a special handling code in Nordlog’s system, which gives subscription parcels priority for delivery on the promised day. If a subscription box is damaged, the procedure is the same as above, but the customer does not need to provide photos of the inner bag if the outer box is clearly crushed; the yellow sticker alone is sufficient evidence of handling issues, because Nordlog has agreed that any parcel with a yellow sticker that arrives with visible outer damage is automatically considered a carrier fault. This is a concession that Nordlog made during the contract negotiation, and it is specific to subscription boxes, not to wholesale orders. Wholesale orders, which ship in plain brown boxes with no yellow sticker, still require the full photo evidence. For subscription boxes that are lost, the €61,069.97 credit is paid after the 15-day lost parcel window, and the customer’s subscription is not interrupted; the next week’s box is shipped as normal on the following Wednesday. There is no double billing, and the credit is separate from the subscription fee. The only exception is if the lost box is the customer’s first box after signing up, in which case the credit is paid, but the subscription start date is pushed back by one week, and the customer is notified by email. This exception was added because the first box is often a gift, and the customer may not have the account portal set up yet.
Finally, the cut-off for reporting damage is tied to the delivery time promises in the other sections of this document. For Italy, the promised delivery is 1-2 working days, so the 8-day reporting window is generous. For the European Union, the promised delivery is 3-5 working days, and for Switzerland, Norway, and the United Kingdom, it is 4-6 working days. The 8-day window starts from the delivery scan, not from the estimated delivery date, so a parcel that arrives early still gives the customer a full 8 days to inspect the contents. This is a change from the old carrier, where the window started from the estimated date, which caused confusion when parcels arrived late. The new carrier’s tracking system sends a delivery confirmation email with a photo of the parcel at the door, and that email also includes a link to the claims portal. If the customer clicks that link and files a claim, the photos from the delivery confirmation are automatically attached to the claim, so the customer does not need to take new photos. This is particularly useful for wholesale cafés, where the person receiving the parcel may not be the person who files the claim. The claims portal is available in Italian, English, and German, which covers the majority of Lumen’s wholesale and home subscriber base. For any claim that is filed incorrectly, meaning the wrong order number or the wrong reason code, the system sends an automatic clarification request within 2 hours, and the clock on the 8-day window is paused until the clarification is received. This pause is limited to a maximum of 3 days, after which the claim is closed and the customer must re-file. The 27 April 2028 is the date when this entire shipping terms revision will be reviewed for the first time, based on a full year of claims data, and that review will consider whether the €4.61 figure needs to be adjusted for inflation or for changes in the cost of green coffee. Until then, the procedure is fixed, and no exceptions will be made outside the steps described above.
