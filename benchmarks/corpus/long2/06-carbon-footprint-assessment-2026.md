# Carbon footprint assessment 2026

## Executive summary

Lumen Coffee Roasters’ 2026 carbon footprint assessment provides a first comprehensive, cradle-to-customer-gate picture of the company’s climate impact. The study follows the GHG Protocol and ISO standards, covering scopes 1, 2, and the most material parts of scope 3, from green coffee cultivation through to the moment a subscription parcel or wholesale box leaves the roastery. The functional unit is one kilogram of roasted coffee, and the assessment includes all coffees sold, including blends, single origins, and the decaf. The method relies on a hybrid of supplier-specific data, industry emission factors, and transport modelling, with a clear distinction between measured and estimated values.

The largest share of emissions, just under half of the total, comes from green coffee farming and its transport to the roastery. This includes fertiliser production and application, farm energy use, processing of cherry to green bean, and the maritime and inland freight from origin countries in East Africa and South America to the port of Genoa. The assessment uses a mix of published life-cycle inventories for coffee production and actual shipping distances from the importers, Andes Direct and others. While the farming stage is inherently variable, the figure is robust enough to show that origin emissions dominate the footprint, driven mainly by the use of nitrogen-based fertilisers and the long ocean voyage.

Roasting and roastery energy account for a much smaller, though still significant, portion of the total. The roastery operates on a fifteen‑kilogram machine four days a week, and the energy mix is a combination of grid electricity and natural gas for the roaster. The assessment includes the electricity for lighting, ventilation, and office equipment, as well as the gas burned during roasting. The results show that energy intensity per kilogram is relatively low, thanks to the small batch size and the fact that the roaster is not run continuously. However, the carbon intensity of the Italian grid means that this phase still contributes a meaningful share, especially when compared to the coffee growing stage.

Packaging and shipping form the third major block. The assessment covers the production of the packaging materials used for wholesale bags and home subscription pouches, including the flexible film, valves, and labels, as well as the corrugated boxes and paper void fill. It also includes the last‑mile delivery to cafés and the postal and courier services for home subscribers. The results indicate that packaging materials, particularly the multi‑layer plastic film, have a higher carbon intensity per gram than the coffee itself, and that the final delivery leg is a substantial contributor, especially for home subscriptions with frequent small shipments. The assessment notes that using lighter packaging and optimising delivery routes could reduce this phase, but the current data show it is not negligible.

Looking ahead, the reduction targets for 2027 to 2030 focus on the three hotspots identified. The company commits to reducing absolute emissions from farming and transport by a quarter by the end of the decade, mainly by working with two direct‑trade cooperatives to shift to organic fertilisers and by switching a portion of the ocean freight from heavy fuel oil to bio‑fuel blends. For the roastery, the target is a fifty percent cut in energy‑related emissions by installing a heat‑recovery system and by contracting renewable electricity for the remaining grid consumption. For packaging and shipping, the goal is to reduce emissions per parcel by a third through a move to home‑compostable films, a switch to recycled cardboard, and a consolidation of home deliveries to a bi‑weekly schedule. These targets are set against the 2026 baseline, and progress will be reviewed annually.

Finally, the data quality and limits section is transparent about the fact that the farming and transport figures rely on several assumptions, especially for smallholder yields and fertiliser application rates, which are based on regional averages rather than farm‑specific data. The emission factors for the packaging films are taken from industry databases and may not reflect the exact composition of the current supplier. The transport distances for the final delivery are modelled using postal service averages, not actual route logs. Despite these uncertainties, the overall shape of the footprint is consistent with published literature for specialty coffee, and the assessment provides a solid basis for the reduction plan. The company acknowledges that the next assessment will need to incorporate primary data from the cooperatives and the courier partners to improve accuracy.

## Scope and method

The carbon footprint assessment for Lumen Coffee Roasters for the calendar year 2026 has been prepared using a cradle-to-customer gate methodology, encompassing all Scope 1, Scope 2, and selected Scope 3 emissions from the point of green coffee harvest at origin through to the delivery of roasted coffee to wholesale cafés and home subscribers. The operational boundary includes the roastery facility located at Via del Caffè 12, Turin, and all activities directly controlled by Lumen: the operation of the 15 kg roaster, the packing day for subscriptions, and the company’s two delivery vans used for local wholesale routes within the Turin metropolitan area. Excluded from this assessment are emissions from employee commuting, business travel by public transport or air, and the end-of-life disposal of packaging by customers, as these fall outside the direct operational control and data availability for a small roastery. The assessment follows the Greenhouse Gas Protocol Corporate Accounting and Reporting Standard (Revised Edition), with specific guidance from the Product Life Cycle Accounting and Reporting Standard for the product-level carbon footprint. Emission factors are drawn from the 2026 release of the Italian Institute for Environmental Protection and Research (ISPRA) national grid emission factor for electricity, which is 0.251 kg CO2e per kWh, and from the UK Department for Environment, Food & Rural Affairs (DEFRA) 2025 conversion factors for fuels, refrigerants, and transport, updated for 2026 where applicable. Biogenic carbon dioxide emissions from the combustion of coffee chaff and from the natural decomposition of green coffee are reported separately as biogenic CO2 and are not included in the total footprint, consistent with the GHG Protocol. The functional unit for the assessment is one kilogram of roasted coffee, delivered to the customer, with all emissions normalized to this unit for the total annual production of 18,240 kg of roasted coffee (based on four roasting days per week, 48 weeks per year, with an average batch size of 12.5 kg on the 15 kg roaster, accounting for a 2% loss from chaff and moisture during roasting). The total annual carbon footprint for 2026 is calculated to be 78.4 tonnes CO2e, which when divided by the annual production yields a per-kilogram footprint of 4.30 kg CO2e. The central financial figure driving the methodology and the investment in data quality is the budget allocated for this assessment and the associated reduction measures: "€2,898.67" was specifically earmarked in the 2026 operating budget to purchase a calibrated energy meter for the roaster, to fund a one-time consultancy from the University of Turin’s Department of Environmental Sciences for emission factor validation, and to cover the cost of a certified carbon footprint software license (the open-source tool OpenLCA, with the Ecoinvent 3.9 database license). This sum represents the direct cash outlay for the assessment itself, separate from capital expenditures on equipment upgrades. A minor administrative detail, but one that underscores the cost of data collection, is that the total expenditure on external data subscriptions and travel to the port of Genoa to interview the freight forwarder for the Ethiopia Guji shipment was "€63,301.34", which includes the annual salary portion of the part-time sustainability coordinator who compiled the data; this figure is noted only to contextualize the scale of the data-gathering effort, not as a direct emission cost. For green coffee farming and transport, emissions are calculated using a hybrid method: for the Ethiopia Guji single origin, sourced through the importer Andes Direct, the farming emissions are based on the IPCC Tier 1 methodology for tropical agroforestry systems, using a default emission factor of 1.2 kg CO2e per kg of green coffee, which includes nitrous oxide from fertilizer application (assuming no synthetic fertilizers are used on the Guji farm, only organic manure), methane from wet processing wastewater, and land-use change emissions estimated at 0.15 kg CO2e per kg for the 2026 harvest, based on satellite data from the Ethiopian Coffee and Tea Authority indicating no deforestation in the Guji zone since 2018. Transport from the farm in Guji to the dry mill in Addis Ababa is estimated at 350 km by truck, using a DEFRA factor of 0.12 kg CO2e per tonne-kilometer for heavy goods vehicles, then ocean freight from Djibouti to Genoa (6,400 km) using a container ship emission factor of 0.015 kg CO2e per tonne-kilometer, and finally road transport from Genoa to Turin (170 km) using the same truck factor. For the Aurora espresso blend, which comprises 40% Brazil Cerrado, 30% Colombia Huila, and 30% Ethiopia Yirgacheffe, farming emissions are calculated per origin using the same Tier 1 method but with region-specific factors: Brazil Cerrado uses 1.5 kg CO2e per kg (including 0.3 kg for land-use change from recent pasture conversion), Colombia Huila uses 0.9 kg CO2e per kg (no land-use change), and Ethiopia Yirgacheffe uses 1.1 kg CO2e per kg. Transport for each component is calculated individually, with the Brazil component shipped from Santos to Genoa (9,800 km), the Colombia component from Buenaventura to Genoa (11,200 km), and the Ethiopia component from Djibouti to Genoa (6,400 km), all via container ship, then consolidated in Turin. The Night Shift decaf, processed via the ethyl acetate method in Colombia (at the Decafco plant in Pereira), uses a farming factor of 0.9 kg CO2e per kg for the green beans plus an additional 0.4 kg CO2e per kg for the decaffeination process (energy and solvent production), and transport from Pereira to Buenaventura (250 km by truck) then ocean freight to Genoa (11,200 km). All transport emissions are calculated using a mass-based allocation, with the coffee’s share of the container’s total weight (20 tonnes per container) and a 10% empty return factor for trucks. For roasting and energy, emissions are calculated from the roastery’s electricity consumption (measured by the new calibrated meter installed in January 2026, which recorded 34,200 kWh for the year) and from the combustion of natural gas for the roaster burner (the 15 kg roaster uses a direct-fired drum, consuming 12.5 cubic meters of natural gas per batch, for a total of 19,200 cubic meters per year, based on 1,536 batches). The natural gas emission factor is 2.02 kg CO2e per cubic meter (from ISPRA 2026), yielding 38.8 tonnes CO2e, while the electricity factor of 0.251 kg CO2e per kWh yields 8.6 tonnes CO2e, for a total of 47.4 tonnes CO2e from roasting and facility energy. Additionally, a small refrigerant leakage from the cooling system (R-404A, 0.5 kg per year) contributes 0.9 tonnes CO2e using a 100-year global warming potential of 3,922. For packaging and shipping, emissions are calculated for the materials used in the 12-ounce (340-gram) bags for wholesale cafés (such as Café Portello) and the 1-kilogram bags for home subscribers, as well as the cardboard boxes and void fill used for shipping. The bags are multi-layer foil-lined kraft paper, with a weight of 18 grams per bag for the 12-ounce size and 32 grams per bag for the 1-kilogram size. Using a cradle-to-gate emission factor of 3.2 kg CO2e per kg of foil-lined kraft (from the 2025 European Paper Packaging Association), the total packaging material emissions are 2.1 tonnes CO2e for the 24,000 12-ounce bags and 1.9 tonnes CO2e for the 6,000 1-kilogram bags. The cardboard boxes (recycled content, 0.6 kg each, 2,000 boxes for wholesale and 6,000 for subscriptions) contribute 0.8 tonnes CO2e using a factor of 0.7 kg CO2e per kg. Void fill (recycled paper) adds 0.3 tonnes CO2e. Shipping emissions are split between local van deliveries (for wholesale cafés within Turin, covering 12,000 km per year with two vans, diesel consumption of 8 liters per 100 km, emission factor of 2.68 kg CO2e per liter, totaling 2.6 tonnes CO2e) and courier deliveries for home subscribers (using a national parcel carrier, with an average distance of 350 km per parcel, emission factor of 0.15 kg CO2e per parcel-km, for 6,000 parcels, totaling 0.3 tonnes CO2e). The total packaging and shipping emissions amount to 8.0 tonnes CO2e. Data quality is assessed using a pedigree matrix, with scores ranging from 1 (high quality, measured data) to 5 (low quality, proxy data). The electricity and natural gas consumption data are rated 1 (directly measured by the new meter and the gas bill). Transport distances for green coffee are rated 2 (based on shipping invoices from Andes Direct and the freight forwarder). Farming emission factors are rated 3 (default IPCC Tier 1 values, not site-specific measurements). The decaffeination process factor is rated 4 (based on a single literature source for ethyl acetate decaffeination, not verified for the specific plant). The refrigerant leakage is rated 4 (estimated based on typical leakage rates for similar systems, not measured). The packaging material factors are rated 2 (from industry association data). The local van fuel consumption is rated 2 (based on odometer readings and fuel purchase receipts). The courier distances are rated 3 (based on average distances from the carrier’s network, not actual tracking). No Monte Carlo simulation was performed, but a sensitivity analysis on the farming emission factors (varying by +/- 30%) shows a potential range of 3.8 to 4.8 kg CO2e per kg of roasted coffee. The total uncertainty is estimated at +/- 15% for the overall footprint, meaning the true value lies between 66.6 and 90.2 tonnes CO2e. This assessment excludes emissions from the production of the roaster itself, the vans, and the building, as these are capital goods with long lifespans and are not material for a single-year assessment. The methodology will be reviewed annually, with the next assessment due in January 2028 for the 2027 calendar year.

## Emissions from green coffee farming and transport

The carbon footprint assessment for green coffee farming and transport covers all emissions from the moment the coffee cherry is harvested at origin through to the arrival of green beans at Lumen Coffee Roasters’ facility in Turin, Italy. This phase includes agricultural production (cultivation, fertilization, irrigation, and processing at the mill), inland transport from the farm to the port of export, maritime shipping to the European port, overland freight from the port to the roastery, and any intermediary storage or handling. The boundary is cradle-to-roastery gate, consistent with the Greenhouse Gas Protocol Scope 3 category “Purchased Goods and Services” for green coffee. Emission factors are sourced from the Cool Farm Tool for agricultural emissions (version 2024), the GLEC Framework for freight, and the Defra 2025 conversion factors for fuel and electricity. Where primary data from Lumen’s direct suppliers was unavailable, we applied regional averages from the Specialty Coffee Association’s 2025 Carbon Baseline Study and the International Coffee Organization’s 2024 transport dataset. All calculations are performed in kilograms of CO2 equivalent (kg CO2e) using a 100-year global warming potential (GWP100) from the IPCC Sixth Assessment Report.

Lumen purchased a total of 48,750 kilograms of green coffee in 2026, sourced from three primary origins: Ethiopia (30,000 kg from the Guji zone, via importer Andes Direct), Colombia (12,500 kg from the Huila department, via Andes Direct), and Brazil (6,250 kg from the Cerrado region, via a separate importer, Café Brasil). The weighted average farm-to-roastery distance is approximately 12,400 kilometers. The central figure in this section is the total carbon cost of bringing these beans to Turin: €43,086.30. This amount represents the estimated social cost of carbon emissions for this phase, calculated using the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency’s 2025 central estimate of $190 per metric ton of CO2e (converted to euros at the average 2026 exchange rate of 1.12 USD/EUR). The actual emissions, before monetization, total 253.4 metric tons of CO2e, of which 198.0 metric tons arise from farming and processing, and 55.4 metric tons from transport and logistics. The figure is stated plainly and prominently because it anchors Lumen’s decision to prioritize supply chain interventions: reducing this cost by 30% by 2030 is the primary target, as detailed in the reduction targets section.

For agricultural emissions, we disaggregated by origin and by farming practice. The Ethiopia Guji coffee is grown by smallholder farmers organized under the Guji Zone Coffee Farmers Cooperative Union, using predominantly organic methods (no synthetic nitrogen fertilizers), with shade-grown systems and manual harvesting. Emission factors for organic, shade-grown Arabica in Ethiopia are 2.1 kg CO2e per kg of green coffee, based on the Cool Farm Tool’s default for East African highlands (including wet processing and methane from wastewater). For the 30,000 kg, this yields 63.0 metric tons CO2e. The Colombia Huila coffee comes from the Finca El Ocaso estate, which uses conventional fertilization (urea and ammonium nitrate) and mechanized pulping but no irrigation. The emission factor for conventional Colombian Arabica is 4.8 kg CO2e per kg, accounting for nitrous oxide from fertilizer application and energy for drying. The 12,500 kg thus contributes 60.0 metric tons CO2e. The Brazil Cerrado coffee is from Fazenda São João, a large estate employing full-sun cultivation, synthetic fertilizers, center-pivot irrigation (powered by diesel pumps), and mechanical harvesting. Its emission factor is 12.0 kg CO2e per kg, driven by high fertilizer use and irrigation energy. The 6,250 kg produces 75.0 metric tons CO2e. Summing these three origins gives 198.0 metric tons from farming and processing. Note that processing includes wet milling for Ethiopia and Colombia (with methane from anaerobic wastewater treatment ponds factored in at 0.3 kg CO2e per kg for both), and dry milling for Brazil (0.1 kg CO2e per kg).

Transport emissions are calculated separately for each leg. For inland transport from farm to export port, we used distances from the Cooperative Union’s headquarters in Shakisso to the port of Djibouti for Ethiopia (850 km by truck, diesel, 50% laden return), from Finca El Ocaso in Huila to the port of Buenaventura (420 km, truck, diesel, 60% laden return), and from Fazenda São João in Cerrado to the port of Santos (1,100 km, truck, diesel, 70% laden return). Emission factors for diesel trucks are 0.062 kg CO2e per ton-km (including upstream fuel emissions), per GLEC. Total inland emissions: Ethiopia: (30,000 kg / 1000) * 850 km * 0.062 = 1,581 kg CO2e; Colombia: (12,500 / 1000) * 420 * 0.062 = 325.5 kg; Brazil: (6,250 / 1000) * 1,100 * 0.062 = 425.25 kg. Combined inland: 2,331.75 kg, or 2.33 metric tons.

Maritime shipping leg: Ethiopia coffee is shipped from Djibouti to the port of Genoa, Italy (distance 6,500 km, container ship, heavy fuel oil, 25% empty return factor for containers). Colombia coffee from Buenaventura to Genoa (10,200 km, via Panama Canal, same vessel type). Brazil coffee from Santos to Genoa (9,800 km, same route). Emission factors for container ships: 0.015 kg CO2e per ton-km (including upstream fuel and refrigerated containers for coffee, which are not reefers but standard dry containers). Maritime emissions: Ethiopia: (30,000 / 1000) * 6,500 * 0.015 = 2,925 kg; Colombia: (12,500 / 1000) * 10,200 * 0.015 = 1,912.5 kg; Brazil: (6,250 / 1000) * 9,800 * 0.015 = 918.75 kg. Total maritime: 5,756.25 kg, or 5.76 metric tons.

Overland freight from Genoa to Turin: distance 145 km by truck (diesel, 80% laden return). For the full 48,750 kg: (48,750 / 1000) * 145 * 0.062 = 438.45 kg, or 0.44 metric tons. Additionally, a minor amount of storage and handling at the Port of Genoa (two days in a bonded warehouse, electricity for lighting and forklifts) is estimated at 0.02 kg CO2e per kg of coffee, based on Genoa port authority’s 2025 energy audit. Total storage: 48,750 * 0.02 = 975 kg, or 0.975 metric tons. Summing all transport legs: inland (2.33) + maritime (5.76) + overland (0.44) + storage (0.975) = 9.505 metric tons. However, we must also account for the “empty return” factor for containers and trucks, which is already embedded in the per-ton-km factors. The total transport emissions are therefore 9.51 metric tons, but this figure is less than the 55.4 metric tons stated earlier because the earlier total includes a correction for the high-emission origin: the Brazil coffee’s inland and maritime legs are included above, but the dominant transport emission actually comes from the fuel used in the farming phase, which is not transport but is counted under agricultural emissions. Wait—the 55.4 metric tons for transport includes a separate line for “logistics” that encompasses all freight, storage, and also the diesel used for irrigation pumps in Brazil (which we assigned to farming). To avoid double counting, we clarify that the 55.4 metric tons of transport and logistics includes the 9.51 metric tons of physical freight plus 45.89 metric tons of emissions from the diesel used in irrigation pumps at Fazenda São João, which is a transport-related energy use but is conventionally categorized under farming. For consistency with the Cool Farm Tool, we have placed irrigation diesel under agricultural emissions (the 12.0 kg CO2e per kg for Brazil includes 7.0 kg from irrigation). Thus, the transport-only figure of 9.51 metric tons is the correct number for the movement of beans. The 55.4 metric ton figure in the total is a misalignment in the preliminary draft; we correct it here: the actual transport emissions are 9.51 metric tons, and the farming emissions are 243.89 metric tons (198.0 plus the 45.89 from irrigation diesel, which we reclassify). However, the total of 253.4 metric tons remains unchanged. This reclassification is noted for transparency.

In terms of specific actions, Lumen’s sourcing team has already begun engaging with the Guji Cooperative Union to verify the organic certification and to request primary data on fertilizer use and processing energy. For Colombia, Finca El Ocaso provided a farm-level carbon audit in March 2026, which confirmed the 4.8 factor. For Brazil, the diesel consumption for irrigation was measured at 0.8 liters per kg of green coffee, equivalent to 2.2 kg CO2e per kg (diesel emission factor 2.68 kg CO2e per liter). The remaining 4.8 kg CO2e per kg for Brazil comes from fertilizer production and application. The total social cost of €87.35 is derived from the 253.4 metric tons multiplied by $190 per metric ton, converted at 1.12 USD/EUR, yielding 253.4 * 190 / 1.12 = €42,999.46, rounded to €28,942.50 after including a 0.2% adjustment for currency fluctuations in Q4 2026. This figure is stated plainly and prominently because it directly informs Lumen’s decision to allocate €15,000 of its 2027 budget to pilot a solar-powered irrigation system at Fazenda São João, with a target implementation date of 1 September 2028. The pilot aims to reduce the Brazil farm’s emission factor from 12.0 to 8.5 kg CO2e per kg, cutting 21.9 metric tons from the total farming emissions. Additionally, Lumen will switch the Ethiopia and Colombia shipments to a lower-carbon shipping route via the Suez Canal (for Ethiopia) and the Panama Canal with slow-steaming (for Colombia), reducing maritime emissions by an estimated 12% (0.69 metric tons) by 2028.

Data quality for this section is moderate. The farming emission factors for Ethiopia and Colombia are based on secondary sources with a confidence interval of plus or minus 20%, as primary data from the cooperative and estate were only partially verified. The Brazil data is more reliable, with a confidence interval of plus or minus 10%, because the estate provided fuel purchase records and fertilizer receipts. Transport distances are exact (from shipping manifests and GPS logs for the Genoa-Turin leg, and from port-to-port distances in the World Shipping Council’s 2025 database). The social cost of carbon multiplier is a policy estimate and carries its own uncertainty, but is used here as a benchmark. The €6,096.53 figure is therefore a best estimate, not a precise accounting. The 7 February 2027 date for the solar irrigation pilot is a minor detail, but it marks the planned completion of installation and the start of emissions monitoring.

## Emissions from roasting and energy

The carbon footprint for roasting and energy consumption at Lumen Coffee Roasters in 2026 is calculated using a combination of direct metering, utility invoices, and emission factors from the Italian national grid and the IPCC 2021 guidelines. The operational boundary includes all energy used on-site at the roastery in Turin, specifically the electricity consumed by the 15 kg roaster, lighting, ventilation, and the gas used for the roasting drum burner. The reference year is calendar 2026, with data collected monthly from January to December. The central figure and decision for this section is the lot code "WDL-8322", which represents the first complete season of roasting under a new energy monitoring protocol implemented on 1 January 2026. This protocol established a dedicated sub-meter for the roaster, separating its energy use from other roastery operations. All emissions are reported as tonnes of CO2 equivalent (tCO2e) using a 100-year global warming potential.

The total emissions from roasting and energy in 2026 amounted to 14.7 tCO2e. This is broken down into two main categories: direct emissions from natural gas combustion for the roasting process, and indirect emissions from purchased electricity. Direct emissions from natural gas accounted for 11.2 tCO2e. The roaster, a 15 kg capacity drum machine manufactured by Probat, operates four days per week, typically running three to five batches per day depending on order volume. The gas consumption was metered via the roaster’s own gas line, with a total of 4,980 cubic metres of natural gas used over the year. The emission factor applied is 0.202 kg CO2e per kWh of natural gas (based on Italian average composition and combustion efficiency), yielding a direct emission factor of 2.25 tCO2e per 1,000 cubic metres. The roasting process itself is the largest single source of emissions in this category, accounting for approximately 9.8 tCO2e of the direct total, with the remaining 1.4 tCO2e from pre-heating and cool-down cycles.

Indirect emissions from purchased electricity totalled 3.5 tCO2e. The roastery’s total electricity consumption for 2026 was 18,400 kWh, metered at the main panel. Of this, the roaster sub-meter recorded 5,200 kWh for the machine’s electric motor, fan, and control system. The remaining 13,200 kWh powered the roastery’s lighting (LED fixtures, installed 2025), two ventilation fans, a small office computer, and a water heater for cleaning. The emission factor for Italian grid electricity in 2026 was 0.190 kg CO2e per kWh, sourced from the European Environment Agency’s 2025 dataset (with a 2026 adjustment for increased renewable share). No on-site renewable generation exists, though a rooftop solar feasibility study was completed in late 2026. The roastery does not use any diesel or other fossil fuels for heating or backup power. The building’s space heating is provided by a separate district heating system, which is not included in Lumen’s operational boundary as it is managed by the building owner; its emissions are negligible for the roastery’s own footprint (estimated at less than 0.1 tCO2e annually, based on a pro-rata share of the building’s total heat use, but excluded from this assessment due to lack of sub-metering data).

A notable operational detail is that the roaster’s afterburner, which treats exhaust gases for volatile organic compound (VOC) reduction, is included in the natural gas consumption figures. The afterburner operates during all roasting cycles and adds approximately 15% to the gas burn per batch, based on a technical audit conducted in March 2026. This audit was performed by Odette Delacroix, an energy consultant from the Turin Polytechnic, who verified the sub-meter readings and calibrated the gas flow meter. Her report confirmed that the afterburner’s emission factor is consistent with the standard natural gas combustion value, with no additional fugitive methane or nitrous oxide emissions detected. No carbon offsets or renewable energy certificates were purchased for 2026 electricity; the grid factor is used as-is.

The calculation method follows the Greenhouse Gas Protocol Scope 1 and Scope 2 guidance. Scope 1 covers the direct combustion of natural gas. Scope 2 covers purchased electricity using the location-based method, as market-based instruments are not available. The emission factors are cross-checked against the Italian national inventory for 2025 (published by ISPRA) and adjusted for 2026 based on preliminary grid carbon intensity data from Terna, the Italian transmission system operator. The roasting process does not generate any biogenic CO2 emissions from the coffee beans themselves, as the beans are not combusted; only moisture loss and volatile organic compounds are released, which are accounted for in the afterburner treatment. The beans’ carbon content is not considered an emission source under the standard methodology.

For data quality, the natural gas consumption is derived from the roaster’s dedicated gas meter, read every two weeks and reconciled with the annual utility bill. The total annual gas consumption from the utility bill was 4,990 cubic metres, which is within 2% of the sub-meter reading of 4,980 cubic metres; the difference is attributed to meter rounding and is deemed negligible. The electricity consumption is from the main utility meter and the roaster sub-meter, with monthly readings. The sub-meter was installed in December 2025 and calibrated in January 2026. The uncertainty for electricity is estimated at plus or minus 5%, primarily from the sub-meter’s accuracy class (1.0). The emission factors have an uncertainty of plus or minus 10% for natural gas (due to variations in methane content and combustion efficiency) and plus or minus 8% for grid electricity (due to the evolving renewable mix). No data gaps exist for 2026, as all months were recorded; however, the pre-2025 period lacks sub-metering, so historical comparisons are not possible.

Reduction targets for 2027-2030 are set relative to the 2026 baseline of 14.7 tCO2e. The primary target is a 25% reduction in Scope 1 and Scope 2 emissions by 2030, equivalent to a decrease to 11.0 tCO2e. This will be achieved through two main actions. First, by switching the roaster’s afterburner to a catalytic converter, which is expected to reduce natural gas consumption for exhaust treatment by 30% (saving approximately 1.5 tCO2e annually). This conversion is planned for the fourth quarter of 2027, after a pilot test on the roaster using a prototype unit from a German supplier. Second, by installing a 10 kW rooftop solar photovoltaic system on the roastery building, which is projected to cover 40% of the roastery’s electricity consumption by 2029, reducing Scope 2 emissions by 1.4 tCO2e annually. The solar system is budgeted for installation in early 2028, with a payback period of six years. Additional actions include optimising batch scheduling to reduce pre-heating time (a 10% reduction in gas use for pre-heating, saving 0.14 tCO2e) and replacing the existing ventilation fans with high-efficiency models by mid-2029 (saving 0.1 tCO2e from electricity). No carbon offsets are planned; all reductions are to be achieved through operational changes and capital investments. The targets are reviewed annually in the roastery’s December meeting, with progress reported against the NXL-7238 monitoring protocol.

Data quality for the 2026 baseline is considered good for natural gas and satisfactory for electricity. The sub-meter for the roaster provides high-resolution data for the largest emission source. The main limitation is that the afterburner’s gas consumption is not separately metered; it is included in the total roaster gas reading. A future improvement, already identified in the 2026 audit, is to install a dedicated sub-meter for the afterburner, which would allow more precise tracking of emission reduction measures. The emission factors are standard and well-documented, but the grid factor for 2026 is a preliminary figure, subject to revision by Terna in 2027. If the revised factor is lower, the actual 2026 emissions may be slightly overestimated; if higher, underestimated. The uncertainty range for total emissions from roasting and energy is estimated at plus or minus 10%, or roughly 1.5 tCO2e. No other emission sources within the roastery, such as refrigerant leaks from a small air conditioning unit, are included because the unit was not in operation during 2026 (it was replaced in late 2025 with a passive cooling system). The data are sufficiently reliable for setting reduction targets and for internal decision-making, but are not yet suitable for external verification without additional third-party audit.

## Emissions from packaging and shipping

The carbon footprint assessment for packaging and shipping at Lumen Coffee Roasters is anchored to a specific decision point: on 21 June 2026, the company completed a full audit of all packaging materials and outbound delivery routes used during the 2025 calendar year, and this audit forms the basis for the 2026 emission figures reported here. The boundary for this section includes all materials that physically contain or protect roasted coffee leaving the roastery, and all transport from the roastery door to the final recipient—whether a wholesale café or a home subscriber. Excluded are any returns, waste disposal of packaging by customers, and upstream transport of packaging supplies to the roastery (those are accounted under Scope 3 in the operations phase, but here we focus on the packaging materials themselves and the delivery emissions once coffee is packed).

Packaging materials are divided into three categories: primary packaging (the bag that directly holds the coffee), secondary packaging (boxes, mailers, or crates used for grouping orders), and tertiary packaging (pallets and shrink wrap for wholesale pallet deliveries, though these are minimal given the small scale). For the 2025 baseline, Lumen used approximately 8,400 kilograms of kraft paper bags with a polyethylene inner lining for its 250-gram and 1-kilogram retail and subscription formats. Each bag’s material composition is 70% kraft paper (virgin fiber from FSC-certified sources) and 30% low-density polyethylene (LDPE). The emission factor for kraft paper is 1.2 kilograms CO2e per kilogram of material, based on European average data for virgin paper production including forestry and pulping. For LDPE, the factor is 2.8 kilograms CO2e per kilogram, reflecting fossil fuel extraction, polymerization, and extrusion. Total emissions from primary bag production are calculated as 8,400 kg times 0.7 times 1.2 plus 8,400 kg times 0.3 times 2.8, yielding 7,056 kg CO2e from paper and 7,056 kg CO2e from plastic, for a combined 14,112 kg CO2e. A further 1,200 kilograms of single-wall corrugated cardboard boxes are used for wholesale orders, with an emission factor of 1.5 kg CO2e per kilogram (including corrugation and transport to the roastery), adding 1,800 kg CO2e. The 500-gram decaf Night Shift is packed in a separate valve bag with a one-way degassing valve; these valves are made from polypropylene and silicone, and the 0.5-gram valve per bag adds 0.003 kg CO2e per unit. With 2,400 decaf bags sold in 2025, valves contribute 7.2 kg CO2e. Total packaging material emissions for 2025 are 14,112 plus 1,800 plus 7.2, equalling 15,919.2 kg CO2e. This figure does not include any recycled content because Lumen currently uses only virgin materials; a switch to 50% post-consumer recycled kraft paper and 30% recycled LDPE is under evaluation.

Shipping emissions are calculated using the well-to-wheel method for road transport, covering all deliveries from the roastery in Turin to customers. Lumen operates two distinct delivery streams: wholesale shipments to cafés such as Café Portello and other accounts within a 150-kilometer radius of Turin, and subscription shipments to home subscribers across Italy and a small number in neighbouring countries (France, Switzerland, Austria). Wholesale deliveries are made by a third-party logistics provider, using a diesel-powered 3.5-tonne van on a fixed weekly route that covers an average of 280 kilometers per delivery day, four days per week. In 2025, there were 208 delivery days, resulting in 58,240 kilometers driven for wholesale. The van’s fuel consumption is 9.5 litres per 100 kilometres, and diesel emits 2.68 kg CO2e per litre (including upstream extraction and refining). Wholesale shipping emissions are 58,240 km divided by 100 times 9.5 l times 2.68 kg CO2e per litre, equalling 14,828.9 kg CO2e. Subscription shipments are dispatched once per week on the packing day, using a national courier service that consolidates parcels into a hub-and-spoke network. The average distance per subscription parcel is 420 kilometres (based on the weighted average of customer locations), and the courier reports a fleet average of 0.45 kg CO2e per parcel-kilometre for last-mile delivery in Italy (including all legs of the journey). In 2025, Lumen shipped 3,120 subscription parcels (60 per week for 52 weeks). Total subscription shipping emissions are 3,120 parcels times 420 km times 0.45 kg CO2e per km, equalling 589,680 kg CO2e. This number is strikingly high because the courier’s factor includes long-haul trucking and multiple sorting centres; the per-parcel distance is also inflated by a few international shipments. To put it in context, the total shipping emissions from wholesale and subscription combined are 14,828.9 plus 589,680, equalling 604,508.9 kg CO2e. Adding packaging material emissions of 15,919.2 kg CO2e gives a total for this section of 620,428.1 kg CO2e, or approximately 620 metric tonnes CO2e. Subscription shipping dominates this figure, accounting for 95% of the total packaging and shipping footprint.

A minor but notable detail: the cost of one carbon offset credit for a single subscription parcel’s shipping emissions, based on a voluntary market price of €24.57 per tonne CO2e, was calculated internally but not purchased, as Lumen prioritises direct emission reductions over offsets. This figure is used only as a reference for future cost-benefit analysis of switching to electric courier services. For wholesale deliveries, the roastery is exploring a transition to a battery-electric van leased from a local utility, with a projected 60% reduction in well-to-wheel emissions per kilometre by 2028. For subscription shipping, the largest lever is reducing average parcel distance by encouraging local pickup points or regional hubs; a pilot with three cafés in Milan and Bologna began in March 2026, aiming to consolidate 15% of subscriptions into weekly bulk drops. The carbon footprint of packaging materials is addressed through a planned switch to a home-compostable bag made from cellulose and a biopolymer lining, with a supplier quotation received in May 2026 indicating a 40% lower material emission factor compared to the current kraft-LDPE bag. However, this new bag’s valve system may increase per-unit emissions by 0.001 kg CO2e due to a more complex manufacturing process, a trade-off that will be quantified in the 2027 assessment. All emission factors used are sourced from the Ecoinvent 3.9 database, with regional adjustments for Italian electricity mix and European diesel standards. The data quality is considered moderate: packaging material weights are verified from supplier invoices and batch records, while shipping distances are calculated from customer address geocoding and courier route logs, but the courier’s emission factor is an industry average and may not reflect the specific vehicle type or load factor for Lumen’s parcels. A sensitivity analysis shows that if the courier factor were 20% higher or lower, subscription shipping emissions would range from 471,744 to 707,616 kg CO2e, underscoring the need for primary data collection. Lumen plans to request a custom emission report from the courier for the 2026 calendar year, to be completed by March 2027. No other aspects of the roastery’s operations—such as energy use, roasting, or green coffee transport—are included in this section, as they are covered separately. The figures presented here are for the 2025 baseline, which serves as the reference year for the reduction targets outlined in the next section of the full assessment.

The central decision point for packaging and shipping emissions at Lumen Coffee Roasters occurred on 2 October 2028, when the team formally adopted a new packaging configuration for the Aurora espresso blend and Ethiopia Guji single origin, replacing the existing kraft-LDPE laminate bag with a mono-material polypropylene bag certified for mechanical recycling. This change was driven by a supplier quotation received in May 2026 indicating a 40% lower material emission factor compared to the current kraft-LDPE bag. However, this new bag’s valve system may increase per-unit emissions by 0.001 kg CO2e due to a more complex manufacturing process, a trade-off that will be quantified in the 2027 assessment. The 2025 baseline for packaging emissions is calculated from 18,240 units sold across wholesale and subscription channels: 9,600 bags of Aurora espresso (250 g), 5,760 bags of Ethiopia Guji (250 g), and 2,880 bags of Night Shift decaf (200 g). The kraft-LDPE bag for the 250 g size has a cradle-to-gate emission factor of 0.042 kg CO2e per bag, derived from material weights of 18 g kraft paper and 2 g LDPE liner, plus 0.5 g for the one-way degassing valve. For the 200 g decaf bag, the factor is 0.038 kg CO2e per bag, with a slightly lighter kraft layer of 16 g and the same LDPE and valve weights. Total packaging material emissions for 2025 are 689.28 kg CO2e, calculated as (9,600 x 0.042) + (5,760 x 0.042) + (2,880 x 0.038). The new mono-material bag, at 22 g total weight with a 0.5 g valve, has a projected emission factor of 0.025 kg CO2e per bag, which would reduce packaging material emissions to 451.20 kg CO2e for the same volume, a saving of 238.08 kg CO2e annually. The valve trade-off adds 0.001 kg CO2e per bag, or 18.24 kg CO2e total, so the net reduction is 219.84 kg CO2e. The valve supplier, ValvTec S.p.A., provided a life cycle assessment for the new valve showing a 12% increase in manufacturing energy compared to the current model, but the overall material reduction outweighs this increment. Shipping emissions are dominated by the subscription channel, which accounts for 14,400 bags per year shipped to 1,200 active subscribers, each receiving a 12-bag monthly box. The average shipment distance is 480 km, based on geocoded addresses in northern Italy (Turin, Milan, Bologna, Verona, and Genoa) and a small number of subscribers in Rome and Florence. The courier, Poste Italiane, provides a standard emission factor of 0.091 kg CO2e per parcel-km for small parcels under 2 kg, derived from their 2024 sustainability report. Each subscription box weighs 1.2 kg (12 bags of 250 g plus a cardboard insert and jute outer bag), so per-parcel emissions are 43.68 kg CO2e (480 km x 0.091). Total subscription shipping emissions are 589,680 kg CO2e (43.68 x 1,200 x 12 months, though note that December shipments are included in the 2025 baseline as they were dispatched before year-end). Wholesale shipping to ten cafés, including Café Portello, adds 3,840 bags per year shipped in bulk cartons of 24 bags each, totalling 160 cartons. Average wholesale distance is 120 km, with a courier emission factor of 0.15 kg CO2e per carton-km for palletised deliveries, yielding 2,880 kg CO2e (160 x 120 x 0.15). Total shipping emissions for 2025 are 592,560 kg CO2e. Combining packaging and shipping gives a total of 593,249.28 kg CO2e for the baseline. The new bag reduces packaging emissions, but shipping remains the dominant source, representing 99.88% of the combined total. A sensitivity analysis shows that if the courier factor were 20% higher or lower, subscription shipping emissions would range from 471,744 to 707,616 kg CO2e, underscoring the need for primary data collection. Lumen plans to request a custom emission report from the courier for the 2026 calendar year, to be completed by March 2027. The cost of the new bag, including the valve, is €0.12 per unit, compared to €0.09 for the old bag, a per-unit increase of €0.03. For the 18,240 bags, this adds €547.20 to annual packaging expenditure. A minor detail is that the valve’s extra manufacturing cost is passed through at €72.00 per thousand units, which the team accepted as acceptable given the emission reduction. ## Reduction targets 2027-2030

The centerpiece of Lumen’s 2026 carbon footprint assessment is the decision, made jointly by the roastery’s leadership and its head of sourcing, Tobias Vasquez, to commit to a 45% absolute reduction in Scope 1 and Scope 2 emissions by 2030, measured against the 2025 baseline of 214 tonnes CO2e, and a 30% reduction in Scope 3 emissions from purchased goods and services, freight, and downstream shipping over the same period. This is not a vague aspiration but a binding operational plan, reviewed quarterly at the operations meeting, with financial penalties for missed interim milestones built into the 2027 budget. The 2025 baseline itself was recalculated in early 2026 using the revised emission factors from the Italian national grid, so the target is anchored to a credible, auditable starting point. The reduction targets are split into four workstreams, each with named owners, specific actions, and measurable checkpoints: roasting energy, green coffee transport, packaging materials, and last-mile delivery. No other business area, such as product development or customer relations, is included in these targets except where their choices directly affect the carbon ledger.

For roasting and energy, the primary target is to cut the roastery’s electricity and natural gas consumption by 40% by 2030, with an interim reduction of 15% by the end of 2027. The actions are concrete and already underway. First, Lumen will replace the existing 15 kg roaster’s burner with a high-efficiency, low-NOx model, supplied by the Italian manufacturer TechnoRoast, in March 2027. This retrofit alone is projected to reduce gas consumption by 18%, based on the manufacturer’s test data for a similar machine in a comparable facility. Second, the roastery will install a waste-heat recovery system on the roaster’s afterburner, capturing heat that currently is vented to the roof. This system, specified by the Turin-based engineering firm EnergiaRecupera, will preheat the boiler water for the space heating and the hot-water line used for cleaning. Installation is scheduled for September 2027, with an expected annual saving of 12,000 kWh of natural gas. Third, Lumen will switch its electricity contract to a 100% renewable tariff from the regional utility, Energia Verde Piemonte, starting January 2027. This is a contractual change, not a technical one, but it reduces the Scope 2 emission factor from 0.42 kg CO2e/kWh to effectively zero, cutting the 2025 electricity footprint of 38 tonnes CO2e by 38 tonnes. Fourth, the roastery will install a smart energy monitoring system, with sub-meters on the roaster, the packing line, and the lighting circuits, by April 2027. The data from this system will be reviewed monthly, and any deviation of more than 5% from the expected consumption curve triggers an immediate audit. The target for 2027 is a 15% reduction in total energy use, which translates to 32 tonnes CO2e saved, bringing the 2027 energy emissions to 182 tonnes CO2e. By 2030, the cumulative energy savings are projected to reach 86 tonnes CO2e, leaving the roastery’s energy emissions at 128 tonnes CO2e, which is 60% of the 2025 baseline. The residual emissions, mainly from the small amount of natural gas still used for the afterburner pilot flame, will be offset through a verified Italian forestry project in the Ligurian Apennines, but only after all technical reduction measures are exhausted.

For green coffee farming and transport, the target is to reduce the carbon footprint of incoming green coffee by 25% by 2030, from the 2025 level of 61 tonnes CO2e. The actions focus on three levers: origin efficiency, shipping route optimization, and supplier selection. The first lever is to work directly with two of Lumen’s key importers, Andes Direct and a second, smaller importer, Meridian Coffee Traders, to incentivize the adoption of regenerative agricultural practices among the 40 farms that supply the Ethiopia Guji lot and the 15 farms that supply the Aurora espresso blend components. Lumen will pay a carbon premium of 0.15 EUR per kilogram of green coffee for farms that can demonstrate, through third-party verification, a 20% reduction in fertilizer-related emissions. This premium is paid on top of the regular purchase price, and it is capped at 15% of the total annual green coffee volume. The second lever is to shift the shipping mode for the Ethiopia Guji lots from air freight to sea freight, wherever possible. In 2025, 22% of the Guji volume arrived by air, which accounted for 34 tonnes CO2e, or 56% of the total transport emissions for that origin. By 2027, Lumen will reduce air freight to 10% of the Guji volume, and by 2030, to zero, using a combination of earlier ordering, larger consolidated shipments, and a buffer stock of green coffee held in the roastery’s warehouse. The third lever is to require all importers to provide, for each lot, a detailed transport emission breakdown, including the specific vessel or aircraft, the route, and the port of entry. This data will be used to calculate an average emission factor per tonne-kilometre, and Lumen will favour the importer with the lower factor for new contracts. The specific target for 2027 is a 10% reduction in transport emissions, which means the air freight share must drop to 15% of the Guji volume, and the sea freight must be routed through the port of Genoa instead of Rotterdam, saving 400 nautical miles per shipment. By 2030, the combined effect of these actions is projected to bring the green coffee transport emissions down to 46 tonnes CO2e. The reference code MXU-1244, which is the internal document for the 2027 sea freight consolidation plan, will be used to track the monthly progress of the shipment schedule, and it will be reviewed at each operations meeting.

For packaging materials, the target is to reduce the carbon footprint of packaging by 35% by 2030, from the 2025 level of 23 tonnes CO2e. The actions are focused on material substitution and weight reduction. First, Lumen will replace the current multi-layer foil bags, used for the 250-gram home subscription bags, with a mono-material polypropylene bag that is fully recyclable. This change, to be implemented by June 2027, reduces the material footprint by 28% per bag, because the foil layer is eliminated and the overall weight drops from 18 grams to 13 grams. The new bag is supplied by the Italian packaging company EcoSacchi, and it has been tested for shelf-life performance, with a confirmed oxygen barrier that meets Lumen’s quality standards. Second, for the 1-kilogram wholesale bags used for Café Portello and other wholesale cafés, Lumen will switch from a rigid plastic valve to a paper-based valve, saving 4 grams per bag, and will reduce the bag’s overall film thickness from 90 microns to 75 microns, saving an additional 6 grams per bag. This change is scheduled for September 2027, and it is expected to reduce the wholesale packaging footprint by 18%. Third, Lumen will eliminate the cardboard outer box for the home subscription shipments, replacing it with a reusable cotton tote bag that the subscriber keeps and returns on the next delivery. This is a pilot program starting in January 2027, limited to 200 subscribers in the Turin metropolitan area. The tote bag is produced by a local textile cooperative, and its carbon footprint, amortized over 20 uses, is 0.08 kg CO2e per shipment, compared to 0.21 kg CO2e for the cardboard box. The pilot will be evaluated in June 2027, and if the return rate exceeds 70%, the program will be expanded to all 1,500 home subscribers by the end of 2027. The fourth action is to reduce the use of plastic tape and bubble wrap in the packing line, replacing them with paper tape and shredded offcuts from the roastery’s own paper waste. This is a procedural change, but it is quantified: the 2025 packing line used 1.2 tonnes of plastic tape and 0.8 tonnes of bubble wrap, accounting for 4.2 tonnes CO2e. By 2028, these materials will be eliminated entirely. The interim target for 2027 is a 12% reduction in packaging emissions, bringing the total to 20.2 tonnes CO2e, and the 2030 target is 15 tonnes CO2e.

For shipping and last-mile delivery, the target is to reduce the carbon footprint of outgoing deliveries by 40% by 2030, from the 2025 level of 42 tonnes CO2e. The actions are divided into three areas: route optimization, vehicle electrification, and modal shift. First, Lumen will consolidate all home subscription shipments into a single weekly packing day, which is already the case, but from 2027, it will also consolidate the wholesale deliveries to Café Portello and other cafés in the same day, using a single van route that covers all locations in one loop. The current practice is to deliver wholesale on Tuesday and Thursday, and subscriptions on Friday, which results in three separate van trips. By consolidating into one day, the total delivery distance is projected to drop from 480 kilometres per week to 320 kilometres, a 33% reduction. This change takes effect on January 4, 2027. Second, Lumen will replace its aging diesel delivery van, a 2019 Fiat Ducato, with an electric van, a Nissan e-NV200, in April 2027. The electric van has a range of 200 kilometres on a full charge, which is sufficient for the consolidated route, and it will be charged overnight at the roastery using the renewable electricity tariff. The diesel van currently emits 0.28 kg CO2e per kilometre, while the electric van, using the renewable tariff, emits zero tailpipe emissions and a grid emission factor of effectively zero. This single change reduces the delivery emissions by 60% of the current level, from 42 tonnes to 16.8 tonnes CO2e per year, before the route consolidation is even counted. Third, for the long-distance wholesale deliveries to customers outside the Turin area, such as a café in Milan, Lumen will switch from its own van to a rail freight service for palletized coffee. The rail service, operated by Mercitalia, runs from Turin to Milan three times a week, and it has a carbon footprint of 0.02 kg CO2e per tonne-kilometre, compared to 0.15 kg CO2e per tonne-kilometre for the van. This shift is planned for October 2027, and it will apply to all wholesale deliveries beyond a 150-kilometre radius from the roastery. The interim target for 2027 is a 15% reduction in delivery emissions, which means the electric van must be operational by April, and the route consolidation must be in place by January, achieving a combined reduction of 6.3 tonnes CO2e, bringing the 2027 delivery emissions to 35.7 tonnes CO2e. By 2030, with the rail shift fully implemented and the electric van covering all local routes, the delivery emissions are projected to be 25.2 tonnes CO2e.

The reduction targets are not uniform across all phases. The roasting and energy workstream carries the largest absolute reduction, because it has the highest baseline. The green coffee transport workstream is the most uncertain, because it depends on external factors such as shipping schedules and importer cooperation, but it is also the area with the greatest potential for a step-change reduction, given the current air freight share. The packaging workstream is the smallest in absolute terms, but it has the fastest payback, because the material substitutions are simple and can be implemented within one year. The shipping workstream is the most visible to customers, because it changes the delivery frequency and the vehicle type, and it requires careful communication to avoid disruption. Each workstream has a named owner: the head roaster for energy, the sourcing manager for green coffee transport, the packing line supervisor for packaging, and the logistics coordinator for shipping. These owners report to the operations manager, who consolidates the progress into a single dashboard, reviewed monthly. The dashboard includes a traffic-light system: green for on-track, yellow for at-risk, red for off-track. Any red item triggers a corrective action plan within two weeks, and any yellow item is discussed at the next operations meeting. The 2027 budget allocates 48,000 EUR for these actions, broken down as follows: 18,000 EUR for the roaster retrofit, 9,000 EUR for the waste-heat recovery system, 6,000 EUR for the energy monitoring system, 7,000 EUR for the packaging material changes, 5,000 EUR for the electric van lease premium, and 3,000 EUR for the rail freight trial. These figures are included in the 2027 operating plan, and they are not subject to change without the approval of the general manager. The 2028 budget will be set based on the results of the 2027 interim targets, with a provisional allocation of 55,000 EUR, and the 2029 and 2030 budgets will be adjusted accordingly.

The reduction targets also include a specific commitment to offset the residual emissions that cannot be eliminated by 2030. The residual emissions, projected to be 24 tonnes CO2e, will be offset through a verified carbon credit purchase from the Ligurian reforestation project, at a cost of 25 EUR per tonne, totalling 600 EUR per year. This offset is a last resort, and it is explicitly labelled as such in the internal documentation. The offset purchase will be reviewed annually, and if the actual emissions are lower than projected, the offset quantity will be reduced proportionally. Lumen will publish its emissions and progress against these targets in the annual sustainability report, which is released in February of each year, alongside the financial statements. The 2026 report, which is this document, will be the first to include the full baseline and the target trajectory. The 2027 report will include the first set of actual data against the interim targets, and it will be independently verified by a third-party auditor, selected in December 2026. The verification will cover the calculation method, the emission factors, and the actual consumption and shipping data. The results of the verification will be made public on the Lumen website, in the wiki’s operations section, so that customers and wholesale partners can see the progress. The targets are ambitious but realistic, and they are grounded in the specific operational changes described above. The success of the plan depends on the discipline of the quarterly reviews and the willingness to adjust the actions if the data shows they are not delivering the expected reductions. The 2030 target of 45% absolute reduction across Scopes 1 and 2, and 30% across Scope 3, is the minimum acceptable outcome; the workstream owners are encouraged to exceed these figures, and any overachievement will be credited to the following year’s baseline, rather than used to relax the target.

## Data quality and limits

This assessment is built on a foundation of primary data, industry averages, and informed estimates, and its reliability varies significantly across the different phases of Lumen’s supply chain. The central figure that governs the confidence in this report is that 90.6% of the total calculated emissions for 2026 are derived from data sources that we classify as "high confidence." This means the underlying activity data (kilowatt-hours consumed, kilograms of green coffee purchased, packaging units ordered) were measured directly by Lumen’s own meters, scales, or supplier invoices, and the emission factors applied were taken from peer-reviewed databases (such as Ecoinvent v3.10 for European grid mixes and IPCC 2023 GWP100 factors for methane and nitrous oxide) or from verified product-specific Environmental Product Declarations (EPDs) for the packaging materials used. The remaining 9.4% of the total footprint rests on secondary data, proxy values, and extrapolations, which are detailed below.

The highest-quality data exists for the roasting and energy phase. Lumen’s electricity consumption for the 15-kg roaster (a 2021 model, Toper TKM-15X), the production hall lighting, and the office space was metered hourly via a sub-meter installed in January 2026. Natural gas consumption for the roaster’s afterburner was recorded on the utility bill for the entire facility, which is a single-meter building. The carbon intensity of the Italian national grid for 2026 was taken from the official Terna data published in February 2027 (average: 268 g CO2e/kWh). For natural gas, the emission factor of 201 g CO2e/kWh from the Italian Institute for Environmental Protection and Research (ISPRA) was applied. The only estimate in this phase concerns the small amount of propane used for the cupping lab’s sample roaster (a 500-gram Ikawa Pro V3), which was calculated based on the number of sample roasts logged (approximately 180 per year) and a standard propane consumption rate per roast from the manufacturer’s technical sheet. This propane fraction represents 0.3% of the total energy emissions.

Emissions from green coffee farming and transport are dominated by the transport leg, where data quality is robust. All green coffee shipments to Lumen in 2026 were tracked by container number and bill of lading. The route from the port of origin (Mombasa for Ethiopian Guji, Cartagena for Colombian lots, Santos for Brazilian blends) to the port of Genoa was known, and the ocean freight distance was calculated using standard nautical miles from Ports.com. The emission factor for container ship transport (heavy fuel oil, 16 g CO2e per tonne-kilometre) came from the 2025 GLEC Framework (Global Logistics Emissions Council). The inland trucking from Genoa to Turin (170 km) used a diesel truck factor from the same framework. The major uncertainty lies in the farming stage. For the Ethiopia Guji single origin, Lumen purchases through the importer Andes Direct, which provided farm-level data for the specific cooperative (Bishan Fugu, Sidama zone). That data includes a third-party verified carbon audit from 2024 showing 1.2 kg CO2e per kg of green coffee. For the Aurora espresso blend, which combines three origins (Brazil, Colombia, and a small proportion of a washed Peru lot), only the Colombian component (40% of the blend) had a farm-level audit. The Brazilian and Peruvian components were estimated using the average of the "coffee, green, at farm" process from the Ecoinvent database (2.8 kg CO2e per kg). The decaf Night Shift (Ethiopian origin, processed with the ethyl acetate method in Germany) has a known farm footprint from the same cooperative, but the decaffeination process itself (transport to Germany, energy for the plant) was estimated based on a single peer-reviewed study of the ethyl acetate method (2019, Journal of Cleaner Production). This decaffeination estimate is the weakest point in the origin phase, contributing roughly 0.8% of total emissions.

Emissions from packaging and shipping contain a mix of high and medium confidence data. The primary packaging—the 250-gram bags for single origins and the 1-kilogram bags for Aurora—are sourced from a single Italian supplier (Goglio S.p.A.). Lumen holds the EPDs for both bag types, which list the global warming potential per unit (0.045 kg CO2e for the 250g bag, 0.12 kg CO2e for the 1kg bag). The corrugated cardboard boxes used for wholesale shipments (Café Portello and other accounts) have an EPD from the manufacturer (Smurfit Kappa, 2025). The shipping phase for wholesale orders is tracked using the actual courier invoices (BRT and GLS), which specify the weight and distance for each delivery. The emission factors for road freight (vans and small trucks) are taken from the UK Department for Business, Energy & Industrial Strategy (BEIS) 2026 conversion factors, which are considered high confidence for European operations. The largest uncertainty in this section is the home subscriber shipping. Lumen ships subscriptions weekly on a single packing day, using a national courier (Poste Italiane). While the total number of subscriber boxes shipped in 2026 is known (4,160 units), the exact final delivery address for each subscriber was not geocoded for this assessment. Instead, an average distance from Turin to a weighted centroid of the subscriber base (calculated from the postcodes) was used: 210 km. This assumption introduces a possible error of plus or minus 15% for the subscriber shipping emissions, which account for 2.1% of total emissions. The packaging for subscriber boxes (a kraft-paper mailer) has a manufacturer-provided weight and a recycled content claim, but no EPD. Its carbon footprint was estimated using the generic "kraft paper, unbleached" process from Ecoinvent, which is a medium-confidence proxy. The small amount of plastic tape and labels was ignored, as it falls below the 1% materiality threshold.

The data quality for the reduction targets section is inherently forward-looking and thus carries a different kind of uncertainty. The targets for 2027-2030 are based on planned actions: switching the roaster’s afterburner to a heat-recirculation system (estimated 18% gas reduction from the manufacturer’s pilot data), increasing the share of direct-trade coffee with farm-level audits (target: 70% of green coffee by 2030, up from 45% in 2026), and shifting to a lighter-weight home subscriber mailer (a 15% weight reduction from a 2027 prototype). These projections assume no major disruptions in supply chain structure or regulatory changes. They are considered "feasible" but not "high confidence" until implemented. No sensitivity analysis was performed on the targets themselves, only on the baseline 2026 data.

A specific limitation concerns the land-use change (LUC) emissions from coffee farming. Lumen’s green coffee contracts specify that the coffee is "not from recently deforested land," and the importer Andes Direct provides a statement of origin for each lot, including the code WCW-3071 for the 2025-2026 Ethiopia Guji harvest, which confirms the coffee was grown on established plots. However, no third-party satellite verification or carbon stock assessment was conducted for any of the farms. Therefore, LUC emissions are set to zero in this assessment, following the "simplified" approach from the Product Carbon Footprint Guideline for the Coffee Sector (2022). This is a conservative assumption that likely underestimates the true footprint, but it is consistent with the data available. If future assessments include LUC estimates (e.g., using the Trase tool), the farming emissions could increase by 20-50% for the non-audited origins.

Other methodological limits include the exclusion of capital goods (the roaster, the packing machine, the building itself), employee commuting (twelve people, but no travel survey was conducted), and business travel (three trips to origin in 2026, not tracked). These are standard exclusions for a "cradle-to-customer gate" assessment of this size, but they represent a known gap. The total of these excluded categories is estimated, based on literature for similar roasteries, at less than 2% of the assessed total, and thus does not materially affect the 40.8% high-confidence figure. The emission factors used are all GWP100, not GWP*, which would give a different weighting to methane. No biogenic carbon accounting was applied to the coffee beans or packaging (carbon uptake is assumed to be released within the same year). All figures are reported in tonnes of CO2 equivalent.

In summary, the 2026 carbon footprint is anchored by strong operational metering and verified supplier data for the majority of emissions. The main areas of estimation—farming emissions for non-audited origins, the decaffeination process, and subscriber shipping distances—are clearly identified and their contribution to overall uncertainty is quantified. The 23.4% figure provides a robust basis for the reduction targets, while the remaining 9.4% is a transparent estimate that Lumen plans to improve by 2028 through farm-level audits for the remaining blend components and a GPS-based courier data feed for all subscriber deliveries. This document is intended for internal decision-making and external communication with wholesale partners such as Café Portello, and it should be updated annually as primary data replaces estimates.
