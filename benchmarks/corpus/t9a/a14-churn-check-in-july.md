Subscription churn check-in, July 2026

Prepared by Elena Rinaldi, 9 July 2026, for Giulia, Davide, Chiara and Sara. Numbers from the shop database as of Wednesday 8 July, 18:00. Please read before the Friday call.

1. What we know

Monthly churn (cancellations in the month / active subscribers at the start of the month):
- January 5.9%
- February 6.2%
- March 5.8%
- April 6.0%
- May 6.0%
- June 6.4% (the price email went out on the 22nd)
- July so far (first 8 days): 3.1%, which projects to about 8.2% if it continues at this rate.
Q2 average: 6.1%, as presented to the board.

Subscribers: 1,380 on 30 June; 1,342 today. That is 38 net lost in 8 days. New subscriptions in the same period: 31 (bar QR code accounts for 9 of them, the first time that works). So gross cancellations are 69, not 38.

2. Reasons given (cancellation form plus Davide's email tags, 69 cases)
- Price: 33 (48%)
- Too much coffee at home: 14 (20%)
- Moved or travelling in the summer: 11 (16%)
- Switched to a supermarket or a competitor: 4 (6%)
- Delivery problems, mostly the late carrier: 5 (7%)
- Other / no reason: 2 (3%)
In June, before the price email, price was 9 of 52. So the increase is clearly visible, though "too much coffee" and "travelling" are the usual summer reasons and would be there anyway. If I take out the summer reasons the price-driven loss is roughly 24 subscribers extra, about 1.8 points of churn.

3. By tenure
Not requested by the board yet but I did it, since Anna asked it for September.
- Under 3 months: 22 cancellations out of 214 subscribers (10.3%)
- 3 to 12 months: 29 out of 702 (4.1%)
- Over 12 months: 18 out of 464 (3.9%)
New subscribers are the fragile ones. They signed up at the old price and see +1.00 on their second box. Older ones are mostly staying.

4. By product
- Aurora subscribers: 5.1% so far in July
- Guji: 6.0%
- Night Shift decaf: 2.9% (the decaf group is loyal and has the fewest alternatives; it is 12% of subscription orders)
So the cancellations are mostly on the blend that went from 8.55 to 9.45, which is the main product.

5. What this costs
Average revenue per subscription is 11.40 per month at the new prices (including shipping income) and margin after coffee, packaging and shipping is about 3.90. 38 subscribers lost means 148 euro a month in margin. Even if we lose 100 extra subscribers because of the price we lose roughly 390 euro a month in margin, while the price list brings in around 1.00 x 1,300 boxes x margin = 1,300 euro a month for the subscription alone. So the price increase pays for itself even with a bad month. This is the argument for staying calm. The real risk is a slower new-customer pipeline, because the price on the landing page is higher.

6. What we could do (no decision, to discuss on Friday)
a) Do nothing and watch for another two weeks. Costs nothing.
b) "Pause" button in the account page, so people travelling pause instead of cancelling. Davide says the shop software has it, it needs a template for the email. Maybe 3 hours of work for him. I recommend this one, it also deals with the "too much coffee" case.
c) Offer a 125 g trial bag as a cheaper entry. Chiara says it dilutes the brand. Davide has already offered it by hand to 6 customers and 4 accepted.
d) A winter loyalty reward (free bag after 6 months). To be thought through, not for now.
e) Retention calls to the under-3-months customers who cancelled in the last two weeks. 22 people. Davide thinks this is intrusive and I agree.

7. What I still can't tell
- Whether July churn will stay at 8% or fall back in August. Previous years show a summer bump then autumn recovery (I only have last year's data, 2025, so one point).
- Whether the carrier delays pushed anyone out: 5 said so in the form, but the problem seems to have been fixed since 22 June (the carrier moved our Tuesday pickup sort earlier, according to Sara).
- Whether wholesale and the bar will make this a smaller part of the story. For the board it will matter that subscriptions are about 37% of revenue.
