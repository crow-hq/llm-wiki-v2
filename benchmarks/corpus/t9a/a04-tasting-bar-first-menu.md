Tasting bar - first menu and prices (draft 3)

Prepared by Chiara Russo with Marco Bellini and Elena Rinaldi
Date: 8 June 2026

This is the menu we want printed by 19 June for the opening on Saturday 27 June. Please comment by Wednesday 10 June, after that I send it to the printer (Tipografia Sarti, they need three working days plus one for cutting).

Principles we agreed last week

1. Small menu. We are a roastery that happens to serve coffee, not a café that happens to roast. Eight drinks, no more. Nothing needs a blender.
2. Everything on the bar is coffee we sell in bags, so a guest can buy what they just drank. Each drink on the menu has the bag next to it on the shelf.
3. Prices have to sit a bit above the cafés we sell to, otherwise Luca will get complaints from Portello and the others. Nobody should be able to say we undercut them with a bar on Via Cigna.
4. One filter option at all times and one "guest" option that changes with the roast calendar.

- Espresso doppio 2.40
- Macchiato 1.60

Food: sealed pastries only, from Forno Balbo (cornetti, a lemon tart, a crumbly almond biscuit) at 1.80-3.00. Water free from the tap with the tasting.

Why these prices

Elena ran the numbers on a cup. Aurora costs us 0.19 per shot for the coffee itself (18 g at the new July price, so I used the future price, not today's). Labour is the real cost: two baristas on Saturday and Sunday means roughly 11 hours x 2 x 11 euro gross hourly, about 240 euro a weekend plus contributions, say 310. To cover labour, rent share, electricity and the milk we need about 105 drinks on a Saturday at an average ticket of 3.00. Elena thinks we will not hit that in July and August, and it is not the point. The point is that the bar loses less than 600 euro a month in the first quarter, which is inside what the board approved. If it does not, it goes back to the board in September and we decide whether to open Fridays or to close Sundays.

Marco's comments, kept here so we don't lose them

- He wants the espresso to be Aurora only on the bar for the first month, no switching of blends in the hopper, because the grinder drifts. Guest espresso is therefore a second grinder, the old one from the lab. Fine.
- Filter: 15 g in, 250 ml out, V60 02, 93 degrees. No Chemex until we have the people for it.
- Cold drip: it takes 8 hours to drip, we have two towers, so 3 litres per run. He thinks we can sell 12 bottles a day at best. Do we print it at all? My opinion: yes, because it supports the cold brew pilot of 500 cans that the board approved. If it is on the menu we hear what people think of it.

Things to decide before printing

- "Espresso 1.40": do we hold the price Italians expect (1.20-1.30) or go 1.40? Elena says 1.40. Luca says 1.30. I say 1.40 with water on the side, because at 1.40 nobody leaves, at 1.20 we cannot pay Ilaria.
- A tasting flight (three espressos of three origins with notes) at 6.00, for groups. I would leave it for the cupping evening in July and not put it on the first menu.
- Card payments only? The reader arrives this week. We take cash too. We won't give change over 50 euro.

Allergens and notes

All milk is cow, oat is a standard barista oat drink (no gluten claim, so don't promise "gluten free" on the menu). Pastries: gluten, eggs, milk, tree nuts for the almond biscuit. A one-page allergen sheet goes at the till, Sara has the template from the ASL.

Layout

A5 card, cream paper, our name in the top left, prices right-aligned, a small drawing of the roaster at the bottom. Two languages, Italian first. 300 pieces, plus a poster version A3 for the wall.

A word on tone. The Italian text should not explain coffee to people. Two lines at the top: "Everything here is roasted ten metres from where you stand. Ask us anything." That is all. The tasting notes go on the small cards next to each bag on the shelf and are written by Marco, not by me, because he is the one who will be asked about them.

Staff and training

Ilaria has two years of bar experience, Matteo is new and has never touched a 2-group. Marco does the training afternoon on 20 June (3 hours: dosing, tamping, milk, cleaning, the end-of-day backflush). Sara prints the closing checklist and sticks it behind the till. Nobody opens the till without having done the opening checklist: machine on at 9:00, first shot at 9:30, grinder purge, temperature log.

Waste and cost control

We start by tracking three numbers every weekend: drinks sold, milk litres used, pastries thrown away. Elena wants them in the shared sheet by Monday morning. If milk waste is above 10% after four weekends we change the cartons (1 L instead of 2 L, which costs more per litre but wastes less on Sunday afternoons).
